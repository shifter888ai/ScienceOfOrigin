VI · Dynamics

Interactions

An exploration of how systems and manifestations interact, how different principles and rules may coexist through interaction, and how interactions may contribute to transformation and the origination of new systems.

Interactions within existence

Science of Origin investigates not only how manifestations and systems originate, but also how they relate and interact after they come into existence. Interactions may connect systems, influence their development and contribute to changes that would not arise from examining each system in isolation.

The nature, conditions and consequences of these interactions remain open subjects of research.

Interaction between systems

Systems may interact with other systems within the same manifestation or across different manifestations. They may be connected, interdependent, overlapping or relatively independent while still having some form of interaction.

An interaction may involve an exchange of influence, a change in relationships, or an effect on the structure or operation of one or more systems. The form and extent of an interaction may vary from one situation to another.

Different principles and rules

Different systems may operate according to different principles and rules. These principles and rules may even appear incompatible, yet the systems may still interact.

This raises important questions: under what conditions can such interactions occur? Do the systems retain their own principles and rules during interaction? Can an interaction create a context in which different rules operate together, or produce effects that neither system could produce independently?

These questions should be investigated without assuming that all systems must share one universal set of principles or rules.

Interactions between manifestations

Different manifestations may also interact. Their interactions may involve systems within them, relationships between them, or effects that extend beyond any one system.

Whether interactions between manifestations follow the same patterns as interactions between systems, or involve additional forms and conditions, remains to be explored.

Effects of interaction

Interactions may have different outcomes. They may:

  • Preserve existing systems or relationships.
  • Influence the operation or development of a system.
  • Change relationships, structures or operating conditions.
  • Transform one or more participating systems.
  • Contribute to the emergence or origination of a new system.

These outcomes are not assumed to occur in every interaction. Identifying the conditions that lead to different outcomes is part of the research.

Emergence of new systems

Interactions among existing systems may give rise to new systems. A resulting system may have its own organization, principles and rules, which may differ from those of the systems involved in its emergence.

A new system need not be merely a combination or continuation of its originating systems. It may develop characteristics and capabilities that are distinct from those of the systems that contributed to its origination.

Independent origination capabilities

A newly formed system may develop its own capability for origination. If so, it may originate further systems or manifestations rather than remaining limited to the capabilities of the systems from which it emerged.

Whether, how and under what conditions such independent origination capabilities develop are open research questions. The term describes a possibility to investigate, not a conclusion already established by the framework.

Novel forms of interaction and origination

Systems with different forms of origination may themselves interact. Such interactions may produce forms of origination that differ fundamentally from those of the systems involved.

This possibility opens a broader research direction: interactions may not only produce new systems, but may also contribute to new ways in which systems originate and interact. The range and limits of such possibilities are not yet known.

These questions connect with Dependence & Independence, Relationships & Networks and Causality & Influence. The possible transformation and emergence of new systems are examined further in Transformation & Emergence, while Independent Origination Capabilities considers what may follow when a new system develops its own origination capability.

Research directions

  • Identifying and describing different forms of interaction between systems.
  • Investigating interactions between systems with different or apparently incompatible principles and rules.
  • Examining interactions within one manifestation and between different manifestations.
  • Distinguishing influence, exchange, transformation and the origination of a new system.
  • Investigating the conditions under which interactions produce new systems.
  • Exploring whether newly formed systems can develop independent origination capabilities.
  • Examining whether different forms of origination can interact to produce fundamentally new forms.
  • Identifying which interactions can be observed, described, measured or experimentally investigated.
Research status: This page records working descriptions, conceptual possibilities and research questions. It does not claim that all proposed forms of interaction, emergence or independent origination have been empirically demonstrated. These ideas remain open to examination, clarification and revision.
