Probability & Uncertainty

An open investigation of likelihood, uncertainty and the limits of what can be known, measured or predicted about manifestations, systems and Origination.

The nature of Probability

Probability is one way of describing how likely an event or outcome is under specified conditions and within a particular framework. It may be expressed numerically or qualitatively, depending on the available information and the purpose of the investigation.

A probability statement requires context. The event being considered, the conditions assumed, the evidence used and the method of estimation should be made clear. A numerical value without these details may create an appearance of precision without providing a reliable account.

Probability and Possibility

Possibility and probability address different questions. Possibility concerns whether an outcome may occur under stated conditions. Probability concerns how likely that outcome is considered to be within a specified model or assessment.

An outcome may be possible but assigned a very low probability. An outcome described as highly probable is not thereby guaranteed. Probability should not be used as a substitute for establishing whether an outcome is possible in the first place.

Probability and Potential

Potential concerns a capacity or conditions through which something may develop or become actual. Probability concerns an assessment of the likelihood of a specified event or outcome. A system's potential does not by itself determine the probability that a particular outcome will occur.

Research can examine how capacities, constraints and changing conditions affect likelihood, while keeping potential and probability conceptually distinct.

The nature of Uncertainty

Uncertainty concerns limits in what is known, observed, measured, explained or predicted. It may arise because information is incomplete, measurements have limitations, relevant conditions are changing, or the framework used to interpret events is insufficient.

Uncertainty is not itself proof that something is random, unknowable or impossible to investigate. Its source and scope should be identified wherever possible.

Sources and Forms of Uncertainty

Uncertainty may arise from different sources. These can include variation in the phenomena being studied, limited or imprecise observations, incomplete information, assumptions in a model, and uncertainty about which model or explanation is appropriate.

These sources should not automatically be treated as equivalent. Some may be reduced through better measurement or additional observations; others may require new concepts, different methods or a clearer statement of what remains unknown.

Measurement, Data and Estimation

Measurements and records provide a basis for estimating probabilities and examining uncertainty. Their usefulness depends on how observations are collected, the quality and relevance of the data, the precision of instruments and the consistency of procedures.

Estimates should be accompanied by information about their limitations. More digits or more elaborate calculations do not necessarily mean that an estimate is more accurate.

Models, Assumptions and Predictions

A probability model describes outcomes using a defined set of assumptions. Its conclusions are conditional on those assumptions and on the model's suitability for the subject being investigated.

Predictions can be compared with subsequent observations. When they do not agree, researchers should examine the data, assumptions, methods and model rather than automatically treating the discrepancy as an isolated exception.

Randomness and Unpredictability

Randomness, uncertainty and unpredictability are related but distinct ideas. An event may be difficult to predict because relevant information is unavailable, because a system is highly sensitive to its conditions, or because the process is described as probabilistic.

Failure to predict an outcome does not by itself establish that the outcome is fundamentally random. Likewise, identifying a pattern does not necessarily make every individual outcome predictable.

Probability within and between Systems

Probability assessments may depend on the principles, rules and conditions of the system under study. A model that is useful within one system may not apply to another system, especially where their principles or rules differ.

Interactions between systems may also change the conditions on which an earlier probability assessment depended. Research should therefore examine whether probabilities remain meaningful across system boundaries and how they change when systems interact or transform.

Uncertainty, Causality and Origination

Uncertainty may affect the identification of causes, the interpretation of effects and the evaluation of proposed causal relationships. A lack of certainty about a cause should not be confused with evidence that no cause exists.

Origination raises further questions. Can the occurrence of origination be assigned a probability within a particular framework? Does continuous or spontaneous origination fit existing ways of describing probability and prediction? These questions remain open and may require concepts and methods appropriate to Origination itself.

Beyond Known Probability Models

Existing probability models may not describe every kind of manifestation, interaction or origination. Different systems may require different ways of representing likelihood, and unfamiliar phenomena may reveal limitations in current models.

Openness to new forms of probability or uncertainty does not establish that any particular alternative is valid. New approaches require clear definitions, transparent methods and evidence appropriate to their claims.

Research Directions

  • Clarifying the distinctions among possibility, potential, probability, uncertainty, randomness and unpredictability.
  • Identifying the conditions and assumptions behind probability assessments.
  • Investigating different sources and forms of uncertainty.
  • Examining how measurement quality, data and estimation methods affect probability assessments.
  • Testing predictions against observations and evaluating the limits of models.
  • Studying how probabilities may change as systems develop, transform or interact.
  • Investigating whether probability models can be meaningfully applied across systems with different principles and rules.
  • Examining the relationship between uncertainty, causality and Origination.
  • Developing methods to communicate uncertainty without overstating precision or certainty.
  • Exploring whether unfamiliar manifestations require new ways of describing likelihood and uncertainty.
Research status: This page establishes an open research area. It does not assume that every uncertain event is random, that probability guarantees an outcome, or that one probability model applies to all systems. Definitions, assumptions, methods and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
