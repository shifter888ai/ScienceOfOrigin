Shared Intentions

An Exploration of Collective Origination, Relationships and Development

Public research exploration · Working Edition 0.1 · Research is ongoing

Abstract

This paper explores Shared Intentions as manifestations that can develop their own self-awareness, express their own Freewill, form intentions and make choices. It considers how Shared Intentions may originate, involve participants, develop independence, continue or transform, and interact with other Shared Intentions.

The exploration includes both intentional and spontaneous origination, multiple origins, cooperation, conflict, recursive creation and the relationship between Shared Intentions, Frequencies and Spectra.

Within the current Science of Origin framework, a Shared Intention is not treated merely as the sum of the intentions of its participants. It may express intentions and make choices that differ from those of individual participants, while the participants retain their own individuality and expressions of Freewill.

This is an initial research account of current propositions and exploratory understanding. The mechanisms, conditions, measurement methods and reproducibility of the relationships described remain open for further investigation.

1. Introduction

What is a Shared Intention?

Intentions are not necessarily limited to individual expressions. This research explores the possibility and characteristics of intentions that manifest collectively, while also developing their own identity and capacity for self-directed development.

Shared Intentions is an independent, cross-disciplinary research topic within Science of Origin. It is related to the exploration of individual intention, but it is not merely a subtopic or extension of it. The two subjects are complementary and can be investigated independently.

The purpose of this paper is to record the current exploration and identify relationships and questions for further research, without assuming that every described process or mechanism has already been established.

2. What Is a Shared Intention?

A Shared Intention is an additional manifestation that can have its own self-awareness and express its own Freewill.

It can form intentions and make choices beyond those of its participating Frequencies. Its intentions and choices may differ from, or even conflict with, the intentions and choices of individual participants.

A Shared Intention is therefore not treated simply as the sum or combined agreement of the intentions of its participants. It can develop its own direction and make choices in accordance with its own circumstances.

This description does not imply another ultimate source of Freewill. The current Science of Origin framework maintains that there is only ONE Freewill, which is Origin. The Freewill expressed by a Shared Intention is explored as an individual expression within that One Freewill, not as an independent ultimate source.

Likewise, the development of an individual Shared Intention does not divide the One Consciousness into separate ultimate consciousnesses. Individuality and shared manifestation are explored within the framework of One Consciousness.

3. Origination of Shared Intentions

A Shared Intention may originate in different ways.

3.1 Intentional origination

A Shared Intention can be intentionally created by an individual Frequency, an existing Shared Intention, multiple individual Frequencies, multiple existing Shared Intentions, or a combination of these.

3.2 Spontaneous origination

A Shared Intention can also emerge spontaneously. Its origination does not necessarily require deliberate creation by an individual Frequency or an existing Shared Intention.

A spontaneously emerging Shared Intention can develop its own participants, self-awareness and expression of Freewill, just as an intentionally created Shared Intention can.

3.3 Multiple origins and subsequent development

A Shared Intention can have multiple origins. Those origins do not necessarily need to remain involved in its subsequent development.

The type of origination does not permanently determine what follows. An intentionally created Shared Intention may continue and develop without further involvement from its originators. A spontaneously emerging Shared Intention may later be intentionally maintained, developed or transformed.

4. Participants and Participation

A Shared Intention can involve multiple Frequencies, and one Frequency can participate in multiple Shared Intentions simultaneously.

A Shared Intention can attract or involve new Frequencies through its own intention. Those new participants need not have interacted previously with the original participants or with the originator.

Participation does not necessarily remove or replace the individuality of a participating Frequency. Each participant can retain its own intentions and expressions of Freewill while participating in a Shared Intention.

Two Shared Intentions may have overlapping participants while pursuing different or conflicting objectives. Participation in one Shared Intention therefore does not necessarily prevent participation in another.

5. Self-Awareness and Freewill

A Shared Intention can develop its own self-awareness and express its own Freewill. It can form intentions and make choices that are not reducible to the intentions or choices of its individual participants.

This does not mean that participants lose their own self-awareness, individuality or Freewill. Individual and shared expressions can coexist.

These descriptions are considered within the current framework of One Consciousness and One Freewill. The expressions of individuality do not establish separate ultimate sources. How the self-awareness and Freewill of a Shared Intention are expressed, recognized and investigated remains open for further exploration.

6. Independence and Continuity

A Shared Intention does not necessarily depend permanently on its original participants or originators.

It may continue to exist after some or all of its original participants stop participating. It may also attract new participants and develop independently of its original initiator, potentially becoming virtually independent of its originator.

Whether a Shared Intention continues to exist and exercises its own Freewill depends on circumstances. This exploration does not assume that every Shared Intention necessarily persists after its participants withdraw.

Continuity of existence, continuity of identity and continued participation are related questions, but they should not automatically be treated as the same thing.

7. Cooperation Between Shared Intentions

Two or more Shared Intentions can cooperate. Each can retain its own identity, self-awareness and expression of Freewill while participating in cooperation with another.

Cooperation does not necessarily mean that the cooperating Shared Intentions merge or become one Shared Intention. They may cooperate while continuing to have distinct intentions and objectives.

Cooperation can also occur recursively. A Shared Intention can cooperate with another Shared Intention that itself cooperates with further Shared Intentions. No predefined upper limit is assumed in the current exploration.

8. Conflict and Interference

Shared Intentions do not necessarily cooperate. They may have conflicting objectives or intentionally influence or interfere with one another.

Possible forms of interference explored here include changing, weakening, redirecting or dissolving another Shared Intention. These are possibilities for investigation, not a fixed sequence or an established mechanism.

A Shared Intention that is targeted by interference may respond in different ways. It may resist, maintain its existence, change or transform, cooperate in its own dissolution, or choose another response. Its response may vary according to circumstances.

Interference does not automatically determine the outcome. The intentions and choices of the Shared Intentions involved remain relevant to the exploration.

9. Transformation and Identity

A Shared Intention can intentionally change its own nature or identity. A transformation may be sufficiently substantial that the result is considered a different Shared Intention.

This raises questions about the relationship between continuity of existence, continuity of identity and transformation. A Shared Intention may continue through change, or a change may be described as the origination of a different Shared Intention.

The criteria for distinguishing these possibilities remain open for further clarification. No single criterion is assumed here.

10. Recursive Creation of Shared Intentions

A Shared Intention can intentionally create another Shared Intention. The newly created Shared Intention can then develop its own participants, self-awareness, expression of Freewill, intentions and choices.

It may become virtually independent of its originator and may initiate further Shared Intentions. The process can continue without a predefined upper limit.

A newly created Shared Intention may involve entirely new Frequencies that have never previously interacted with the original initiator. It does not necessarily need to maintain a continuous connection with the Shared Intention or Frequency from which its origination began.

Recursive creation is therefore explored not only as repeated initiation, but also in relation to the independence and subsequent development of each newly originating Shared Intention.

11. Shared Intentions as Frequencies and Spectra

Within the Spectrum / Spectra approach, a manifestation can be described as a Frequency when considered as an individual manifestation, or as a Spectrum when considered collectively as a composition of Frequencies.

Accordingly, a Shared Intention can be considered as a Frequency when examined as one manifestation, or as a Spectrum when examined as a collective of Frequencies. The appropriate description depends on the perspective and level of composition being examined.

These descriptions allow individual and collective manifestations to be investigated within the same research framework. They do not require a Shared Intention to be classified permanently as only a Frequency or only a Spectrum.

12. Further Research

The following areas remain open for continued exploration:

  • How intentional and spontaneous origination of Shared Intentions occur.
  • How multiple origins may contribute to one Shared Intention.
  • How a Shared Intention develops its own self-awareness and expresses Freewill.
  • How participants become involved, withdraw or are replaced by new participants.
  • Under what circumstances a Shared Intention continues to exist independently of its original participants or originators.
  • How continuity of existence, continuity of identity and transformation can be distinguished.
  • How cooperation occurs between Shared Intentions while each retains its identity.
  • How Shared Intentions influence, interfere with or respond to one another.
  • How recursive creation proceeds and how newly originated Shared Intentions develop independently.
  • How Shared Intentions may be investigated as individual Frequencies and collective Spectra.
  • Which aspects can be explored through practical observation, measurement, experimentation and reproducible methods.

These are research directions, not claims that the mechanisms or answers have already been established. The descriptions in this paper may be refined as the exploration develops.

Summary

This initial account explores Shared Intentions as manifestations that can develop their own self-awareness, express their own Freewill, form intentions and make choices beyond those of individual participants. It considers intentional and spontaneous origination, multiple origins, participation, independence, continuity, cooperation, conflict, transformation and recursive creation.

A Shared Intention is not treated merely as the sum of its participants' intentions. It may develop its own direction while participants retain their individuality and expressions of Freewill. These possibilities are explored within the framework of One Consciousness and One Freewill, without introducing another independent ultimate source.

The relationships and mechanisms described remain open for further investigation. Research is ongoing. The exploration of Origin has no final conclusion.

Research status: Working Edition 0.1. This is an initial public account of current propositions and exploratory understanding. It is intended to develop as the research continues; it does not present every relationship or mechanism as independently established.
