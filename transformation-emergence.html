Transformation & Emergence

An exploration of how existing manifestations and systems change, how continuity may be distinguished from the origination of something new, and how interactions may give rise to properties, structures and capabilities not previously present.

Transformation and emergence

Transformation and emergence are related but distinct subjects. Transformation concerns changes in an existing manifestation or system. Emergence concerns the appearance of properties, relationships, structures or systems that may not be adequately described as changes to any one component alone.

They may occur together: interactions may transform participating systems while also contributing to the emergence of a new arrangement. The conditions and boundaries between these processes remain subjects for investigation.

Transformation of manifestations and systems

A manifestation or system may change in its components, properties, relationships, structure, operation or capabilities. Changes may be gradual or sudden, limited or extensive, reversible or irreversible.

Transformation may take place through internal processes, interaction with other systems, changes in surrounding conditions, or combinations of these factors. Research should identify what changes, what remains, and the conditions associated with the transformation.

Continuity and identity

When something changes, an important question is whether it remains the same manifestation or system. Some changes may leave its defining organization or continuity intact; other changes may be so fundamental that the result is more appropriately considered a new manifestation or system.

There may not be one criterion that applies to every kind of manifestation. Possible considerations include continuity of organization, persistence of relationships, retained properties, causal history and the way the entity functions. These are candidate criteria for examination, not a settled definition of identity.

Emergence of new properties

When components or systems interact, the resulting arrangement may exhibit properties or capabilities that are not present in the components considered separately. Such properties may depend on the organization and relationships of the whole, rather than on any one component in isolation.

Research can investigate whether a proposed emergent property is genuinely new, how it depends on the participating elements and their relationships, and whether it can be observed or measured independently.

Emergence through interaction

Interactions may preserve existing arrangements, modify them, or contribute to the emergence of a different system. A resulting system may have its own organization and may operate according to principles or rules that differ from those of the systems involved in its emergence.

The emergence of a new system should be distinguished from a temporary interaction effect, a change within an existing system, or a new description of an arrangement that was already present. Establishing which has occurred requires clear definitions and evidence appropriate to the case.

New principles, rules and capabilities

An emerging system may develop properties, principles, rules or capabilities that are not simply copies of those of its contributing systems. It may also become part of a larger system or interact with systems beyond those involved in its emergence.

One possibility for investigation is that a newly originated system may develop its own capability for further origination. If this occurs, its subsequent development may extend beyond the capabilities of its originating systems. The possibility is open for exploration and is not presented as an established universal process.

Unprecedented possibilities

If new systems can develop properties and origination capabilities that differ fundamentally from those of their origins, the range of possible manifestations may not be predictable from the originating systems alone.

This raises open questions about novelty, limits, and whether forms of existence or origination can arise that are not represented in the systems currently known. The research should remain open to such possibilities while distinguishing imaginative proposals from observations and supported findings.

Research directions

  • Identifying and describing different forms and scales of transformation.
  • Investigating how to distinguish continuity of a manifestation or system from the origination of a new one.
  • Examining the conditions under which new properties emerge through relationships and interactions.
  • Distinguishing emergent properties from temporary effects, measurement artefacts and changes in description.
  • Investigating how new systems develop their own principles, rules and capabilities.
  • Exploring whether newly originated systems can develop independent origination capabilities.
  • Identifying which transformations and emergent properties can be observed, measured, tested or reproduced.
  • Recording unresolved questions and alternative explanations without prematurely closing off possibilities.
Research status: This page records working descriptions and conceptual possibilities for investigation. It does not claim that all proposed forms of emergence, new capabilities or unprecedented manifestations have been empirically demonstrated. Definitions, criteria and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
