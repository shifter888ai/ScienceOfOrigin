# Science of Origin — Master Principles

## Complete Research Library — Research Base Principle

**Status:** Locked foundational principle  
**Applies to:** All Science of Origin research, divisions, fields, applications, and future research directions  
**Scope:** All original materials and records ever created, collected, or recorded for the research programme

### 1. The complete library is the primary research base

The foundation of Science of Origin research shall be one complete, searchable research library containing all available original materials and records.

The library is the common source from which research topics, sample cases, terminology, concepts, comparisons, and research reports may be extracted whenever needed.

The library is not limited to the current research framework or to subjects that appear relevant at first glance. It must remain capable of supporting existing research, newly emerging topics, and discoveries or connections that have not yet been recognized.

**Preserve everything first. Discover and organize relationships through research.**

### 2. No exclusion based on apparent relevance

Materials shall not be excluded merely because their subject, wording, format, or original context appears unrelated to a current research topic.

Questions, answers, discussions, personal experiences, observations, examples, measurements, experiments, notes, articles, books, images, diagrams, tables, audio, video, and other recorded materials may contain research-relevant information or connections that become apparent only through later comparison and analysis.

The research library should therefore preserve the full available record before topic classification or interpretation. Apparent relevance is not a reliable criterion for deciding whether a record belongs in the library.

### 3. Preserve original records and provenance

Original source material is the primary record. Summaries, extracts, translations, classifications, interpretations, and later research analyses are derived layers; they must never silently replace or alter the original.

Where available, every record should retain or identify:

- A stable record identifier.
- Original source, location, and source reference.
- Original wording and language.
- Author, speaker, questioner, or participant, as applicable.
- Research date or period, with its degree of certainty.
- Original context and surrounding material.
- Attachments, diagrams, tables, measurements, and supporting data.
- Completeness and provenance status.
- Links to derived summaries, cases, terms, topics, and related records.

Unknown, missing, incomplete, or uncertain information must be marked as such, not invented or silently reconstructed. Preserve source versions and corrections transparently.

### 4. Cases are a central unit of research discovery

Research analysis should look beyond surface topics and keywords. A seemingly unrelated question, discussion, or passage may contain an actual experience, observation, event, example, or measurement that connects to a research topic.

Where a source contains a case, record the case with its original context and a traceable link to the source. Capture, where available:

- What happened or was reported.
- Who experienced, observed, asked, or reported it.
- When and under what circumstances.
- What details, measurements, or outcomes were recorded.
- Which original passages and records support it.
- Which concepts, terms, topics, and other cases may be connected.
- What remains unknown or unresolved.

A case may connect to multiple topics, and a topic may draw on multiple cases. Do not force a single-topic classification. Do not invent a case to fill a gap; mark it as not yet located and continue searching the library.

### 5. Distinguish source, experience, interpretation, and evidence

The library and all derived research must distinguish clearly among:

- Original statements and source claims.
- First-person experiences and personal reports.
- Observations made by another person.
- Examples, analogies, and illustrations.
- Interpretations and proposed explanations.
- Measurement or experimental claims.
- Independently verified findings.

Preserving a report as research material does not establish that its interpretation or proposed mechanism is true. Maintain open inquiry while identifying the evidence status, limitations, and unresolved questions of each claim.

### 6. Glossary and terminology are integral to the library

A complete, searchable, multilingual glossary and terminology database shall be built as an integral part of the research library.

It shall preserve both current terminology and historical usage across all original records. Each term should, where available, include:

- A stable term identifier and preferred current term.
- Original forms, expressions, spellings, and languages.
- Definitions and explanatory context.
- Related, contrasting, broader, and narrower terms.
- Source records and exact locations where the term appears.
- Historical changes in usage or meaning.
- Translation equivalents and translation notes.
- Status, such as current, evolving, proposed, historical, or deprecated.

Historical terminology must remain searchable even when a preferred term changes. A later definition must not be projected backwards onto an earlier source without clearly marking the interpretation. Similar wording must not automatically be treated as equivalent concepts; distinctions and uncertainties must be preserved.

### 7. Topics and research views are extracted from the library

Research topics, indexes, case collections, concept maps, timelines, glossaries, and reports are connected views derived from the same underlying library. They are not separate collections that duplicate or displace the original records.

A record should be stored once as a source and linked to as many relevant topics, cases, concepts, terms, and other records as the evidence supports.

The current Science of Origin framework provides an organizing structure, not a boundary on the library. Research topics may be added, revised, divided, combined, or newly discovered as analysis develops. Topic classifications and relationships must remain revisable and traceable to their source records.

### 8. Research extraction must remain possible at any time

The library should support on-demand extraction and comparison by:

- Any current or future research topic.
- Any term, concept, phrase, or historical terminology.
- Any person or role, subject to privacy and access rules.
- Any date, period, source, record type, or language.
- Any case, observation, measurement, or experimental record.
- Any combination of topics, terms, cases, and time periods.
- Relationships, similarities, differences, contradictions, and changes across records.

Every extracted result should link back to its original source and preserve enough context to evaluate it. Search and automated analysis may suggest connections, but suggested relationships must remain distinguishable from confirmed relationships.

### 9. One library, multiple connected layers

The complete research library should connect, at minimum:

1. Original source archive.
2. Record and provenance catalogue.
3. Case and experience library.
4. Glossary and terminology database.
5. Topic and subject indexes.
6. Concept and relationship network.
7. Measurements, experiments, and evidence records.
8. Derived research analyses, reports, and publications.

These are connected layers over one common research base, not independent bodies of records. The implementation may evolve; the preservation, traceability, and relationship principles remain.

### 10. Privacy, permissions, and responsible preservation

Completeness does not override privacy, confidentiality, copyright, or permissions. Preserve materials in accordance with applicable access rights and restrictions. Sensitive personal information and private communications must not be published or broadly exposed without appropriate permission and review. Where public access is not appropriate, retain only what is lawfully and appropriately accessible, with access controls and provenance metadata.

### 11. Governing principle

The complete original library is the enduring research base of Science of Origin.

- Preserve the original record before interpreting it.
- Do not exclude material because it appears unrelated.
- Discover connections through cases, context, terminology, and cross-record analysis.
- Keep original sources distinct from derived interpretations.
- Make every conclusion and extraction traceable to its source.
- Keep the library open to future topics, new relationships, and discoveries not yet imagined.

**The archive preserves what has been recorded. The terminology preserves the language. The cases preserve the experiences and observations. Research discovers how they connect and what they may reveal.**

This principle governs the design, preservation, indexing, analysis, and future development of the Science of Origin research base.
