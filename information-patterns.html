A fundamental research dimension

Information & Patterns

An open exploration of the nature of Information and Patterns, how they relate to Things, Life, Energy, Matter and systems, and how they may arise, persist, interact and change.

The nature of Information

What is Information? How should it be distinguished from data, signals, meaning, knowledge or the physical representation through which it is conveyed?

Different fields use the term Information in different ways. Science of Origin should clarify the meaning and scope used in each investigation rather than assume that one description necessarily covers every possible form of Information.

The nature of Patterns

A Pattern may refer to an arrangement, relationship, sequence, structure or regularity that can be recognized across observations or within a system. What makes something a Pattern, and how can a Pattern be distinguished from coincidence or an interpretation imposed by an observer?

Patterns may be stable or changing, simple or complex, repeated or unique. The criteria for identifying them should be made explicit and appropriate to the subject being examined.

Information and Matter

Information can be represented, stored or transmitted through physical systems. How does the representation of Information relate to the material structures and processes that carry it?

The relationship between Information and Matter should be examined without assuming in advance that Information is either wholly reducible to Matter or independent of every material process. Different meanings of Information may require different explanations and methods.

Information and Energy

Physical processes can transmit, transform and process signals that carry Information. What relationships connect Information, Energy and the processes through which signals are produced, received or changed?

Any proposed relationship should identify the relevant system, definitions and measurable quantities. Information, Energy and Matter should not be treated as interchangeable terms merely because they are connected in particular processes.

Patterns in Things and Life

Patterns can be investigated in the structures, forms, behaviors and processes of Things and Life Forms. Some patterns may recur across different manifestations, while others may be specific to a particular system or set of conditions.

Research can examine how patterns arise, how they are maintained, and how they change. It should distinguish patterns observed in a subject from the models or classifications used to describe them.

Information in Systems

Systems may contain, process or exchange Information through their components and interactions. How Information is organized and used may depend on the principles, rules, structure and boundaries of each system.

Different systems may encode or interpret signals in different ways. Whether Information can pass between systems, and what may be preserved or altered during that interaction, are questions for investigation.

Information, Consciousness and Freewill

The relationship between Information, Consciousness and Freewill is an open research subject. How should Information associated with perception, awareness, decision-making or intention be distinguished from the processes and properties of Consciousness and Freewill themselves?

Science of Origin should not assume that Consciousness is simply Information processing, nor that every informational process involves Consciousness. These possibilities require clear definitions and evidence appropriate to the claims being examined.

Emergence and transformation of Patterns

Interactions among components or systems may produce new arrangements and patterns. Existing patterns may also be disrupted, reorganized or transformed as a system changes.

Research can investigate the conditions under which a pattern emerges, whether it persists, and how it relates to the properties of the system. The appearance of a pattern does not by itself establish a particular cause or mechanism.

Beyond known forms

Current concepts of Information and Patterns may not encompass every possible way in which organization, representation or relationships can occur. New observations or methods may require distinctions that are not presently recognized.

Openness to unfamiliar forms does not establish that they exist. Proposed forms and relationships should be clearly described and distinguished from observations, interpretations and supported findings.

Research directions

  • Clarifying the meanings and scope of Information, data, signals, meaning and knowledge.
  • Developing criteria for identifying, describing and comparing Patterns.
  • Examining how Information is represented, stored, transmitted and transformed in different systems.
  • Investigating relationships among Information, Energy and Matter without treating them as interchangeable.
  • Studying recurring and distinctive patterns in Things, Life Forms and other manifestations.
  • Examining how systems organize, process and exchange Information.
  • Investigating the relationship between Information, Consciousness and Freewill without assuming their equivalence.
  • Studying the emergence, persistence, disruption and transformation of Patterns.
  • Developing observations and methods that distinguish actual patterns from coincidence, measurement artifacts or observer interpretation.
Research status: This page establishes an open research area and records questions for investigation. It does not assert that Information is a fundamental substance, that all Patterns have a common origin, or that any proposed relationship with Consciousness has been established. Definitions, methods and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
