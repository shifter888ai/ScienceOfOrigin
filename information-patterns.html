Information & Patterns

Investigating what Information and Patterns are, how they are recognized and represented, how they relate to Things, Life, Energy, Matter and systems, and how they may arise, persist, interact and change.

The nature and meanings of Information

Information is used in different ways across scientific, technical and everyday contexts. It may refer to a reduction in uncertainty, a distinguishable signal or record, an arrangement that can be interpreted, or content that has meaning to an observer or system. These uses are related, but they are not automatically identical.

Research should state which meaning of Information is being used. In particular, a quantitative measure of information, the physical signal or record that carries it, and the meaning attributed to that signal should be distinguished. A description that is useful in one field should not be assumed to settle the nature of Information in every other context.

Questions about whether Information is fundamental, dependent on physical representation, or differently constituted across different domains remain open within this research framework.

The nature of Patterns

A Pattern may refer to an arrangement, relationship, sequence, structure or regularity recognized within observations or a system. Patterns can occur in spatial arrangements, temporal sequences, repeated events, relationships among components and changes across different scales.

Identifying a Pattern requires criteria. The observations, comparison method, scale, time interval and degree of similarity should be specified where relevant. A pattern may be present in the subject being examined, arise from the method of classification, or be perceived through an observer's chosen perspective. These possibilities should not be conflated.

Patterns may be stable or changing, simple or complex, repeated or unique. A perceived regularity does not by itself establish its cause, purpose or origin.

Information, data, signals and meaning

Data may be recorded distinctions or values; a signal may carry or convey distinctions; and meaning concerns how content is interpreted or understood within a context. Information may be involved in each, but the terms should not be treated as interchangeable.

The same signal may be interpreted differently by different systems, while different signals may convey equivalent information under a defined encoding or convention. Research should identify the sender, receiver, encoding, context and interpretation when these are relevant to the question.

Information can be measured or described without necessarily establishing that it has meaning to a conscious observer. Conversely, meaningful information may depend on context that is not captured by a purely quantitative measure.

Information and physical representation

Information can be represented, stored or transmitted through physical systems. A record, signal or message may depend on a material or physical medium, and its persistence or transmission may involve physical processes.

The relationship between Information and Matter should be examined without assuming in advance that Information is wholly reducible to Matter or independent of every material process. It is important to distinguish the information being described from the particular physical representation that carries it.

Research can ask what is preserved when a representation changes, what is lost through noise or degradation, and under what conditions information can be copied, transformed or recovered.

Information and Energy

Physical processes can transmit, transform and process signals that carry Information. Such processes may involve the use, transfer or dissipation of Energy. The relationship depends on the physical system, the operations performed and the definitions and measurements being applied.

Any proposed relationship should identify the relevant system, information measure, physical process and measurable quantities. Information, Energy and Matter should not be treated as interchangeable terms merely because they are connected in particular processes.

Patterns in Things and Life

Patterns can be investigated in the structures, forms, behaviors and processes of Things and Life Forms. Some patterns may recur across different manifestations, while others may be specific to a particular system, scale or set of conditions.

In living systems, patterns may be examined in organization, development, regulation, inheritance, behavior and interaction with an environment. Research should distinguish observed regularities from the models used to describe them, and should avoid assuming that a pattern found in one form of life applies universally.

Related research areas include the Nature of Things, the Nature of Life, and Energy and Matter.

Information in systems

Systems may contain, process, store or exchange Information through their components and interactions. How Information is organized and used may depend on the principles, rules, structure and boundaries of each system.

Different systems may encode or interpret signals in different ways. Whether Information can pass between systems, and what may be preserved, reinterpreted or altered during that interaction, are questions for investigation.

Research should distinguish the existence of a signal or record from the ability of a particular system to detect, process or interpret it. A system's failure to recognize information does not necessarily mean that no signal was present. Related questions about organization and interaction are explored in Systems and Interactions.

Information, Consciousness and Freewill

The relationship between Information, Consciousness and Freewill is an open research subject. How should Information associated with perception, awareness, decision-making or intention be distinguished from the processes and properties of Consciousness and Freewill themselves?

Science of Origin should not assume that Consciousness is simply Information processing, nor that every informational process involves Consciousness. These possibilities require clear definitions and evidence appropriate to the claims being examined.

Research should also distinguish information about an experience from the experience itself, and a description of a decision from the question of how intention and Freewill relate to that decision. See Consciousness and Freewill for the framework's foundational treatment.

Emergence, persistence and transformation of Patterns

Interactions among components or systems may produce new arrangements and patterns. Existing patterns may also be disrupted, reorganized or transformed as a system changes. Research can examine the conditions under which a pattern appears, persists, disappears or is replaced by another arrangement.

The appearance of a pattern does not by itself establish a particular cause or mechanism. Investigations should consider alternative explanations, measurement artifacts, selection effects and the possibility that an apparent regularity may not persist under different conditions.

A change in a pattern should also be distinguished from the origination of a new manifestation. A new arrangement may arise within an existing system without necessarily constituting a new system or manifestation. Related questions of change and emergence are considered in Transformation and Emergence.

Beyond known forms

Current concepts of Information and Patterns may not encompass every possible way in which organization, representation or relationships can occur. New observations or methods may require distinctions that are not presently recognized.

Openness to unfamiliar forms does not establish that they exist. Proposed forms and relationships should be clearly described and distinguished from observations, interpretations and supported findings.

Research directions

  • Clarifying the meanings and scope of Information across different fields and levels of investigation.
  • Distinguishing quantitative information measures, physical representations, signals, data and meaning.
  • Developing explicit criteria for identifying, describing and comparing Patterns.
  • Examining how Information is represented, stored, transmitted, interpreted and transformed in different systems.
  • Investigating relationships among Information, Energy and Matter without treating them as interchangeable.
  • Studying recurring and distinctive patterns in Things, Life Forms and other manifestations.
  • Examining how systems detect, organize, process and exchange Information, including across different system boundaries.
  • Investigating the relationship between Information, Consciousness and Freewill without assuming their equivalence.
  • Studying the emergence, persistence, disruption and transformation of Patterns.
  • Developing observations and methods that distinguish actual patterns from coincidence, measurement artifacts, selection effects or observer interpretation.
  • Identifying which questions can be measured or experimentally investigated and which remain conceptual or unresolved.
Research status: This page establishes an open research area and records questions for investigation. It does not assert that Information is a fundamental substance, that all Patterns have a common origin, or that any proposed relationship with Consciousness has been established. Definitions, methods and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
