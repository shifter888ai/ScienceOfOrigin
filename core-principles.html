Science of Origin — Foundational Framework

A working account of Origin, Freewill, imagination, existence and non-existence, Oneness, Zero and the freedom of origination.

Working edition · Version 0.2
This framework is evolving. Its descriptions are not intended to become fixed doctrine or to define the limits of further exploration.

1. The Nature of Origin

Science of Origin explores Origin and what Origin may imagine and express. Origin is not defined by this framework, nor confined to any description, concept, logical system or imagination. Every description is itself an expression within the exploration; it cannot stand as an absolute boundary around Origin.

The purpose is not to decide in advance what Origin must be. The exploration remains open to what Origin may be, imagine, express or leave unmanifested.

Among the fundamental aspects, or natures, of Origin currently recognized in this exploration are Infinity, Consciousness, Freewill, Spirit and Void. This is not an exhaustive list, and these aspects do not define or limit Origin. No hierarchy or fixed sequence is implied.

2. Freewill

Freewill is one of the fundamental aspects, or natures, of Origin. It is not treated as a separate object that exists alongside existence. Existence itself is an expression of Origin's Freewill. Everything is at will.

This freedom includes the freedom to express or not express, to manifest or not manifest, to change or not change, and to originate or not originate.

Non-existence is also an expression of Freewill. Neither existence nor non-existence has absolute priority over the other. Neither is compulsory.

3. Existence and Non-Existence

Existence and non-existence are both possibilities of expression. Origination and cessation may occur simultaneously, all the time; neither needs to dominate the other. Something may come into existence, continue, change, remain idle or cease to exist at will.

Activity and idleness, manifestation and non-manifestation, continuation and cessation are not opposing conditions imposed by an external authority. They are among the possibilities of Freewill.

4. Imagination and Existence

Whatever can be imagined, exists. Imagination is itself an expression of existence; it is not merely a mental representation of something that might exist later.

Imagination and manifestation are not two separate stages. The physical is also an imagination of Origin. What is imagined is not necessarily waiting for a later act of manifestation to become existent.

Different imaginations may be contradictory and still exist. They need not be capable of being manifested together within one particular universe. The limits or logic of one manifestation do not establish absolute limits on all imagination.

5. Unlimited Creativity and Origination

Origin imagines things never imagined before, all the time. Creativity is the freedom to originate something that has never existed before. It is not limited to repeating, combining or modifying what is already known.

Origination may be spontaneous, intentional or arise through combinations of these. A spontaneous origination may occur without a predetermined intention. Spontaneity may also be chosen intentionally. An origination may develop a purpose or structure, change them, or continue without a fixed purpose or structure.

Any existence may originate, continue, transform, remain idle or cease to exist at will. These are possibilities, not stages in a prescribed sequence.

6. No Objective Rules or Laws

No objective rules or laws ever existed. What are called rules or laws are imaginations or agreements; they are not objective authorities that exist independently of those expressions.

An agreement may be continued, changed, replaced, suspended, ignored or discontinued at will. A pattern or a repeated event does not, by itself, establish an objective rule or law. What happens repeatedly may be observed and investigated without turning the pattern into a compulsory universal constraint.

There is no universal requirement that origination follow one particular structure or process. A choice made in one context is not a rule prescribing how everything must originate.

7. Oneness and Relative Independence

All existences are Oneness. There is no absolute independence; independence is always relative.

Distinct identities, intentions, functions, activities and structures may have relative independence while remaining within Oneness. Relationships may become closer or more distant; structures may integrate or differentiate. Relative independence and interaction are not evidence of absolute separation.

8. Zero, Infinity and Manifestation

Zero is not a numerical starting point. In Zero-Origin exploration, Zero represents the idle of Origin. Infinity represents the possibility before manifestation. These descriptions are not intended to impose a fixed sequence or a compulsory process on Origin.

Idleness is not the absence of possibility. Manifestation may occur, not occur, or coexist with idleness in other expressions. The meanings and relationships of Zero, Infinity, idleness and manifestation remain open to continued exploration.

9. Purpose Is an Imagination

Origin has no ultimate purpose. Having a purpose is itself just another imagination.

An existence may imagine a purpose, develop it, change it, abandon it or never have one. Origination and existence do not need an externally assigned reason or purpose in order to occur.

10. An Ever-Evolving Exploration

The framework itself is an evolving imagination. All is evolving; nothing is ever fixed. Concepts, descriptions, models and agreements may be revised, replaced, expanded or left behind.

No current statement in this framework is intended to close further possibilities. A new understanding may change how earlier ideas are described, and an unfamiliar possibility may open an entirely new direction of exploration.

11. Continuing Exploration

Science of Origin explores possibilities without imposing predetermined boundaries. Questions may lead to new concepts, models or ways of investigating. A model may be useful for exploring one possibility without becoming a universal description of all origination.

Descriptions of structures, loops, spaces, universes, designers or operating arrangements belong to particular explorations and models. They should not be converted into universal requirements or objective laws.

12. Research Perspective

Openness and objectivity can coexist. Exploration need not reject an unfamiliar possibility in advance, and openness does not require treating every interpretation as an established finding.

In recording research, it is useful to distinguish what has been experienced or observed, what has been imagined or reported, what has been inferred, what is proposed as a model, and what has been independently examined. These distinctions clarify the status of a statement; they do not impose a boundary on what may be explored.

Evidence, methods and conclusions may be examined within their relevant scope while the wider field of inquiry remains open. Conventional recognition is not a prerequisite for a subject to be explored, and lack of independent verification should be stated accurately rather than used as an automatic reason for dismissal.
