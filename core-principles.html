Core Principles of Origin

The foundational principles that guide Science of Origin: the nature of Origin, infinite originating capability, openness, manifestation, and experimental inquiry.

1. Origin: Infinity and its descriptive perspectives

Origin (the Ultimate Source) cannot be adequately defined by finite human language. Infinity is the closest definitional description: not merely an extremely large quantity or an endless sequence, but the infinite nature of the Source, which is described as both everything and nothing simultaneously. The Source is formless, imageless, beginless and endless, and is presented as the one absolute existence from which all relative existences arise.

The following are approximate perspectives on the same Origin, not separate entities, interchangeable definitions or exhaustive descriptions:

  • Infinity — definitional perspective: the closest description to a definition of the indefinable Source.
  • Spirit — perspective of nature: the closest description of the Source's nature, while still not fully describing it.
  • Consciousness — perspective of attributes: an approximate description of the Source through its attributes.
  • Freewill — perspective of functionality: an approximate description of the Source through its functionality.
  • Voidness — visual perspective: a description of the Source in stillness, when it is not in motion or manifesting; this is the perspective of non-manifestation or apparent nothingness.

These perspectives do not divide the Source into parts. The Source is described as infinite yet manifesting and encompassing all finite things; formless yet manifesting all forms; timeless yet manifesting beginnings and endings. Each manifestation is a polarity or expression of the whole, not an independent absolute existence.

See Origin and Consciousness and Freewill.

2. Freewill as the functional perspective of Origin

Freewill describes Origin from the perspective of functionality; it is not, by itself, an exhaustive definition of Origin. Consciousness describes Origin from the perspective of attributes. These perspectives are closely related in the framework, but the shorthand “Freewill = Origin” is too narrow to serve as the formal definition of the Ultimate Source.

Within this framework, every relative existence is a creation and manifestation of the Source, and each manifestation expresses the whole. The claim that originating capability is intrinsic to manifestations should be understood within this relationship, rather than by treating every manifestation as an independent ultimate Origin.

Origin is described as having infinite spontaneous creation. Its manifestations may in turn give rise to further originations. No capability needs to be newly granted or acquired; what appears to develop as a new capability may be an expression of capability already intrinsic to the nature of the manifestation.

Capability must therefore be distinguished from its expression. An existence may express only a limited range of possibilities in a particular experience or manifestation without its intrinsic originating capability being diminished.

An originated existence may originate further things and produce outcomes beyond its originator's original intentions, expectations or design. Such outcomes are familiar in real-world experience. This does not mean every outcome is predictable or that every mechanism is already understood.

3. Will, intention, experience and origination

Will, intention and experience differentiate particular originations without creating or limiting the intrinsic capability of Freewill.

  • Will: associated with Freewill, the functional perspective of Origin.
  • Intention: an expression of Will that may give a particular origination its direction or character.
  • Experience: what is encountered through manifestation; it may influence later intentions and expressions, without creating, increasing or diminishing intrinsic capability.
  • Origination: the coming-about of a particular manifestation or possibility.

These relationships are not necessarily a one-way chronological sequence. Experience may inform further intention, while spontaneous intention may arise without being determined by prior experience.

4. Spontaneous creation and intentional origination

The original description of the Source presents its creation as entirely spontaneous, with two modes: automatic and autonomous. These are the Source's described modes of spontaneity and should not be collapsed into the later distinction between spontaneous and intentional origination within manifestations.

In the later discussion of intention, spontaneous intention may arise randomly as well as in other ways; it should not automatically be interpreted as a concealed deliberate plan or as something determined by prior experience. Intentional origination has a deliberate direction or purpose. These describe how particular originations arise and are not definitions of the Source itself.

The relationship between automatic and autonomous spontaneity, spontaneous intention and intentional origination remains a subject for careful clarification. See Spontaneous and Intentional Imagination.

The distinction between spontaneous and intentional origination concerns how a particular origination arises, not whether the underlying capability exists. See Spontaneous and Intentional Imagination.

5. Intrinsic infinity and apparent limitation

Origin and its intrinsic originating capability are infinite. Nothing can truly limit this capability. Rules, conditions, boundaries and constraints may shape what is experienced or manifested within a particular context, and may make some possibilities appear unavailable. Within this framework, such limitations do not restrict the underlying nature of Freewill; they are apparent or illusory in relation to that intrinsic capability.

A limitation may therefore govern an experience or the operation of a particular manifestation without becoming an absolute limit on Origin. The range currently expressed must not be confused with the range of intrinsic capability.

6. Infinity, openness and the known

The known is never infinity. What has been observed, experienced or understood is finite in relation to the inexhaustible nature of Origin and its possibilities. The unknown is not exhausted by the expansion of knowledge, and no current body of knowledge defines the boundary of what may be possible.

Infinity means openness to possibilities that are not yet known, including possibilities that have not yet been imagined. Openness is fundamental to Science of Origin; limitation is its opposite. No existing theory, model, observation, experimental method or current understanding should be treated as an absolute boundary on discovery. Even present descriptions of Infinity remain open to refinement.

7. Observation and experimentation

Science of Origin is an experimental science grounded in observation and experience. Its research begins from what has been observed, experienced or encountered, and proceeds through further investigation and experimentation. An observation does not need to have been made or recognized by other humans, civilizations or extraterrestrial beings before it can become a subject of research.

Observation and experimentation are open-ended in nature. No collection of observations, set of experiments or established method exhausts what may be observed or investigated. Research methods should expand when new observations call for them, rather than treating current methods as the limits of inquiry.

Care is still needed to distinguish an observation from an interpretation, an explanation and a conclusion. A first-hand observation can be a genuine starting point for experimental inquiry even when its mechanism or independent reproducibility has not yet been established. The status of each claim should be made clear without dismissing unfamiliar observations merely because they are not yet widely recognized.

8. An open-ended research principle

Science of Origin does not treat the current known, the currently unknown, or the limits of existing research methods as the limits of existence or possibility. Individual findings may be examined and conclusions may be reached within a defined scope, but no finding closes the wider field of inquiry.

Infinite Origin, infinite possibilities, and openness to infinite observation, experimentation and discovery.
