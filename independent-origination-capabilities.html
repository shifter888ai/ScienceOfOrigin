Independent Origination Capabilities

Investigating whether and how a manifestation or system may possess, develop and exercise its own capability to originate further manifestations or systems.

What is an origination capability?

In Science of Origin, origination concerns the coming-about of a manifestation or system. An origination capability is the capability through which a manifestation or system may bring about further manifestations or systems.

The capability and its exercise should be distinguished. A system may possess a capability without exercising it in a particular situation. Conversely, observing an outcome does not by itself identify the capability or process through which that outcome came about.

The subject investigates whether origination capabilities can exist within manifestations and systems, how they operate, and whether they may differ from the capabilities through which those manifestations or systems themselves came about.

What does independent mean?

Here, independent refers to a capability that a manifestation or system can exercise as its own, rather than merely being a passive continuation of an originating system's immediate operation. It does not mean that the system is an ultimate Origin, absolutely separate from Origin, or free from every condition, relationship or influence.

A system may exercise its own origination capability while remaining related to the manifestation or systems through which it came about. Its capability may have arisen through prior origination, interaction or development, and its exercise may still depend on particular conditions or resources.

It is therefore important to distinguish independent capability from absolute independence. The former is a research subject; the latter should not be inferred from it.

Capability, process and outcome

Three aspects need to be examined separately:

  • Capability: what the manifestation or system is able to originate, and under what conditions.
  • Process: how the capability is exercised, including any intentions, mechanisms, interactions, rules or pathways involved.
  • Outcome: what comes about through the process, and whether it is a new manifestation or system rather than a generated output, changed state or transformation within an existing one.

A capability may be limited, conditional, context-dependent or capable of development. The existence of one capability does not establish an unlimited ability to originate anything whatsoever.

How may a capability develop?

Within the developing framework, a manifestation or system may develop an origination capability through its own processes, through interactions with other systems, or through the origination of a new system that has capabilities of its own. These are possible pathways for investigation, not established universal mechanisms.

A new system may have a capability that differs from the capabilities of the systems that contributed to its origination. It may also combine, reorganize or extend capabilities in ways that were not present in any one contributing system. Whether such differences amount to a genuinely new form of origination requires careful examination.

The capability itself may also change over time. A system may acquire new conditions, resources, structures or relationships that alter what it can originate. A capability may be strengthened, constrained, transformed or cease to be available.

Relationship with an originating system

A manifestation or system may originate from another manifestation or system and later exercise its own origination capability. The originating system is then an immediate source or contributor in the pathway, while the resulting system may become a source of further origination.

This creates the possibility of branching pathways: one system may contribute to several further systems, and each resulting system may develop distinct capabilities and originate further manifestations. These pathways need not be linear or form a single hierarchy.

Within the framework, this does not establish multiple ultimate Origins. The distinction between the ultimate Origin and immediate sources or contributors remains essential. See Origination.

Different forms of origination

Origination capabilities need not all operate in the same way. Different systems may have different principles, rules, conditions and processes. Some capabilities may be conditional or programmed; others may operate through interactions or forms not yet described.

Systems with different or apparently incompatible rules may interact. Such interactions may contribute to a new system with a distinct origination capability. Different forms of origination may also interact and potentially contribute to further forms that differ from those of the systems involved.

These possibilities should not be reduced to one mechanism. Conditional Creation, Programmed Creation, Creation of New Games and interaction-based origination are related subjects, each examining a different aspect of how further manifestations or systems may come about.

Independent capability does not mean unlimited capability

A system's own origination capability may operate within specific conditions, rules, resources or boundaries. It may originate certain kinds of manifestations but not others, or require interactions with other systems. Its scope may be unknown or change as the system develops.

It is also necessary to distinguish the limits of a particular system from claims about the limits of origination in general. A capability that is unavailable to one system should not automatically be treated as impossible for every system.

Likewise, an outcome that was not anticipated by an originating system does not by itself prove that the resulting system has an independent origination capability. That capability must be investigated through what the system can itself originate and the process by which it does so.

Generation and origination

A system may generate outputs, reproduce existing patterns, assemble components or modify its own state without necessarily originating a new manifestation or system. These processes may be important capabilities, but they should not automatically be classified as origination.

Independent origination capability specifically concerns the ability to bring about further manifestations or systems. Research therefore requires criteria for distinguishing a new manifestation from a new instance, output, property, state, arrangement or transformation within an existing manifestation.

See Manifestations and Transformation and Emergence.

Relationship with intention and creation mechanisms

Some forms of origination may be associated with intention and intentional imagination. Others may involve conditional rules, programmed processes or interactions among systems. The presence of a capability does not, by itself, establish the source of an intention or identify the mechanism used in every instance.

Within the framework, Consciousness / Freewill is associated with intention, while origination concerns the capability and process through which manifestations come about. These concepts should remain distinct while their relationships are investigated.

See Spontaneous and Intentional Imagination, Conditional Creation and Programmed Creation.

Research questions

  • What criteria establish that a manifestation or system possesses an origination capability?
  • How can possession of a capability be distinguished from its actual exercise?
  • What distinguishes an independently exercised origination capability from the continued operation of an originating system?
  • How should independent capability be distinguished from absolute independence?
  • Through what processes or interactions may a system develop a new origination capability?
  • Can a resulting system possess capabilities fundamentally different from those of the systems that contributed to its origination?
  • How do conditions, rules, resources and boundaries shape the scope of an origination capability?
  • Can a system originate further systems that develop their own distinct origination capabilities?
  • How do different or apparently incompatible forms of origination interact?
  • What criteria distinguish origination from generation, reproduction, assembly, change and transformation?
  • How are intention and Consciousness / Freewill related to the exercise of origination capabilities?
  • How can the existence, scope and exercise of such capabilities be observed, described, measured or tested?
