Read the Research Framework

Three levels of research organization

  • Major Divisions — broad research domains that provide orientation.
  • Fundamental Topics — central subjects and foundational concepts within and across divisions.
  • Extended Topics — more specific concepts, relationships, applications, observations, experiments and research questions.

These levels provide a useful way to navigate the research; they are not a rigid hierarchy or a ranking of importance. A topic may connect with several divisions. An Extended Topic may later become a Fundamental Topic, and new discoveries may lead to new topics or even new divisions.

How to read the connections

  • The central connections place Origin at the center of the investigation and connect it with the seven Major Divisions.
  • Primary relationships show the current organizational placement of topics.
  • Cross-connections indicate research associations across divisions and topics.

A line represents a relationship identified for research and navigation. It does not, by itself, establish causation, dependency, equivalence or experimental confirmation. The map is an evolving representation of current understanding, not a definition of Origin or a complete picture of reality.

A map that can grow

The present map contains selected topics from the current framework. It is an initial working representation, not an exhaustive inventory. As the research develops, nodes and connections can be added, revised, moved, reclassified or removed. Relationships may also be refined into more precise types as their basis becomes clearer.

Its purpose is not to freeze research into a diagram, but to make the evolving body of inquiry easier to explore, examine and extend.
