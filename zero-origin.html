Zero-Origin — Nature of Origin

An evolving account of Origin and its described natures, developed through experience, observation, questions, clarification and continuing inquiry.

Origin is undefinable

Origin cannot be defined. This is not a claim that Origin is unknowable, undiscoverable, indescribable or beyond understanding. Undefinability does not impose a boundary on discovery, description or understanding. It means that no definition can contain Origin without limiting what is being described.

The formulation “we do not know what Origin is, even Origin does not know what Origin is” expresses this undefinability. It is not a declaration of ignorance or a claim that inquiry must stop. Origin can be perceived and described, while remaining undefinable.

Infinity is a nature of Origin, not Origin itself. To state “Origin = Infinity” would turn one nature into a definition of Origin and impose a boundary that the description does not intend.

Named natures and aspects

Five natures or aspects have been named in the current account. They are not an exhaustive list, a hierarchy or a sequence:

  • Infinity
  • Consciousness
  • Freewill
  • Spirit
  • Void

These descriptions do not divide Origin into separate entities. Aspects may be independent, interact, influence one another, combine, separate, form new relationships and originate further aspects. The account does not distinguish Origin's aspects from what those aspects originate as though they were separate ultimate sources. Further natures may be discovered or described.

Zero, idle and Infinity before manifestation

Zero is not a numerical starting point. It represents the idle state of Origin and Infinity before manifestation. Idle is not an absence of capability, freewill or possibility. Origin can choose to have no intention and remain completely idle, at will.

Idle and active are not mutually exclusive conditions that must apply to Origin as a whole. Origin can be idle in one aspect while active in another, or simultaneously idle and active, at will. Origination and cessation can likewise occur simultaneously, continuously, deliberately, spontaneously or through mixed intentions.

An intention may arise spontaneously, deliberately or through a mixture of both. Intention itself may originate or cease. Spontaneity can also be chosen deliberately. No intention is required for Origin to remain as it is.

See Zero — Idle State of Origin for the related foundational account.

Freewill is the freedom of change

Freewill itself is the freedom of change. They are the same thing. This includes the freedom to change or not change, to originate or not originate, to manifest or not manifest, to express or not express, to combine or separate, and to transform or remain.

Freewill is not a property that Origin or a manifestation possesses as something separate from itself. A manifestation does not merely have freewill; in this account, it is freewill in that manifested form.

The freedom to change includes the freedom to change the way change occurs, to change rules or limitations, or to imagine something fundamentally different. “Redefine freewill” is not an appropriate description here, because defining is itself a limiting act. Freewill does not need to be contained by a definition in order to change.

The only thing that does not change is change. Remaining unchanged is also an expression of the freedom to change.

Origination, cessation and imagination

Origin can originate existence and let it cease to exist, at will. Origination and cessation are not separate eras or a one-way sequence; they can occur simultaneously. They may be spontaneous, deliberate or mixed.

Nothing exists independently outside Origin. Anything imagined as existing outside Origin is also an imagination of Origin. All manifestations are imaginations of Origin. Imagination and manifestation are not treated as two separate stages: an imagination is a manifestation. The physical is also an imagination.

Origin has no limit on what it can imagine. It can imagine rules and no-rules, different systems of logic, and contradictions that coexist as genuine contradictions rather than merely appearing contradictory from a limited perspective. Logic does not govern or limit Origin; logic and its alternatives are themselves within the scope of imagination.

Rules may be changed, replaced, suspended or abandoned, including while manifestations that operate under those rules continue. Origin may also imagine limitations for itself, including the experience of forgetting its freedom or experiencing itself as unable to choose, without that imagined condition exhausting its freedom.

Manifested identities, finite experience and oneness

Origin may manifest identities that experience themselves as separate, limited or unaware of their nature. Such identities may experience separation as reality or recognize it as a game. The term Fake Origin describes one explored model of multiple identities of Origin experiencing themselves as separate.

An infinite nature may manifest in a finite form that does not recognize its infinity. Such a manifestation may rediscover its infinite nature, remain unaware, or rediscover it and choose to continue a finite experience. Rediscovery is not compulsory; these possibilities remain expressions of freedom.

A super-soul, a thing or a lifeform is not described as possessing Origin as a separate property. The formulation is: they do not have it; they are it. A super-soul is also Origin.

Open questions

  • What additional natures or aspects may be perceived or described?
  • How can different modes of perception reveal different aspects of the same Origin?
  • How do spontaneous and deliberate intentions interact in particular originations?
  • How can simultaneous idle and active expressions be described without forcing them into a single sequence?
  • What further forms can emerge when aspects interact, separate or originate new aspects?
  • What kinds of experience or investigation may reveal relationships not yet recognized?
Research status: This page records an evolving experiential and conceptual account. It distinguishes descriptions and working understandings from questions that remain open. It does not claim to define Origin, exhaust its possible natures or establish every proposition through conventional scientific methods.
