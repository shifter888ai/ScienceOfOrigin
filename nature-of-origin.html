Nature of Origin

An experiential account of Origin and its natures and aspects

This page records the developing account of what Origin is, as described through YWS's long-term practice, direct experience, personal verification, observation and subsequent clarification. The descriptions are perspectives on Origin; they are not intended to define or exhaust it.

1. Source and experiential background

The original account records an experience of unity with the Ultimate Source of All Things on January 27, 2008, following 29 years of practice. The initial experience lasted approximately three hours. A later clarification states that this duration refers only to the initial experience: the state of oneness was not lost after those three hours and has continued through 2026.

Further experiences, perceptions, questions, corrections and discoveries have emerged since 2008. Many have not yet been documented. This page brings together the relevant clarifications available at this stage; it does not claim to be a complete record of every experience.

The original first-person account remains available at What Is the Ultimate Source? The original account is preserved there, with later clarifications distinguished from the first edition.

2. Origin is undefinable

Origin is undefinable. Undefinability does not mean unknowable, undiscoverable, indescribable or beyond understanding. It does not impose a boundary on perception, experience, description or inquiry. Origin may be perceived and described without any description becoming its final definition.

Infinity is a nature of Origin, not Origin itself. Treating Origin as identical to Infinity would turn one of its natures into a definition of Origin. The account therefore distinguishes Origin from any one description of its nature.

Human language and concepts can provide useful descriptions, but none should be mistaken for the entirety of what is being described. A description may be refined as further experiences and discoveries arise.

3. Named natures and aspects

Five natures or aspects have been named in the current account:

  • Infinity
  • Consciousness
  • Freewill
  • Spirit
  • Void

This is not an exhaustive list, a hierarchy or a sequence. The names represent different descriptive perspectives, not separate ultimate entities. The original account associated Spirit with a perspective of nature, Consciousness with attributes, Freewill with functionality, Void with a visual perspective of stillness or non-manifestation, and Infinity with a definitional perspective. A later clarification makes the important distinction that Infinity is a nature of Origin, not Origin itself.

Further natures or aspects may be discovered. No fixed number or final classification is asserted.

4. Freewill and the freedom of change

Freewill itself is the freedom of change. Freewill and the freedom of change are the same thing. This includes changing or not changing, originating or not originating, manifesting or not manifesting, combining or separating, and transforming or remaining.

Remaining unchanged is also within the freedom of change. Choosing not to originate or manifest is not an absence of capability. No definition is intended to contain Freewill, because a definition would impose a limit on the freedom being described.

Consciousness and Freewill are treated in the current framework as two human words for the same nature of Origin. Their relationship is developed further in Science of Consciousness.

5. Idle, activity and non-manifestation

Origin may remain idle, be active, or be idle in one aspect while active in another. Idle is not an absence of capability, freedom or possibility. Zero, in this research account, is not a numerical starting point; it represents the idle state of Origin and Infinity before manifestation.

Origination and cessation can occur simultaneously. Either may be spontaneous, deliberate or mixed. Spontaneity itself may be deliberately chosen. Intention may arise or cease; no intention is required for Origin to remain as it is.

These descriptions do not establish a single, fixed sequence from idle to activity or from non-manifestation to manifestation. Different aspects and processes may coexist.

6. Imagination and manifestation

Within this account, all manifestations are imaginations of Origin. Imagination and manifestation are not treated as two separate stages, and the physical is also an imagination. The imaginer and the imagined are not treated as separate ultimate sources.

Origin's aspects and what they originate are likewise not treated as separate in ultimate nature. Aspects may be independent, interact, influence one another, combine, separate, form new relationships and originate further aspects. The relationships among aspects and their manifestations remain open to further exploration.

See Imagination, Origination and Manifestations for related research perspectives.

7. Rules, logic and apparent contradictions

Rules and logic do not impose boundaries on Origin. Different manifestations may operate under different rules, including rules that contradict one another. Rules may change, be suspended or cease. The account does not assume that the logic familiar within one manifestation governs every possible manifestation or Origin itself.

Descriptions that appear contradictory from one perspective are not automatically treated as errors or resolved by forcing them into a single framework. Their meaning and relationship remain subjects for careful exploration.

8. Nature, activity and purpose are distinct perspectives

This page concerns the nature of Origin: what Origin is and the natures or aspects through which it is described. The activities of Origin—such as originating, manifesting, transforming and playing—and the purpose of creation or origination are related subjects, but they are not definitions of Origin's nature.

The research treats these as distinguishable perspectives rather than interchangeable terms. See The Origin-Game for the account of gaming as an activity and as the purpose of creation or origination, and Virtuality of Existence for the relationship between imagination and existence.

9. Continuing inquiry

The five named natures are not exhaustive, and no description on this page is intended to close further discovery. The account remains open to additional natures, aspects, relationships and clarifications as further experiences and observations are documented.

The purpose of this page is to make the current experiential account accessible in a research-oriented form while preserving the distinction between original experience, later clarification, description and further inquiry.
