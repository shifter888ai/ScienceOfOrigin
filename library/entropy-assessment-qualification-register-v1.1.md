## 1. Purpose and handling

This register extracts statements that qualify how particular assessment points are described, tested, interpreted, or applied. It preserves the source's distinctions rather than imposing a common scoring rule.

The line references below point to numbered visible text runs in the RTF transcription. They are source locators, not Word paragraph numbers. Where several adjacent runs form one sentence or passage, the full relevant range is given.

The source uses the labels `RR`, `88` and `JJ`, but the inspected overview does not define them. This register does not infer their meanings, units, comparability, or a universal better/worse direction.

### Q4. Case-specific and level-based assessment

The source explains that auxiliary-section assessments use levels rather than the main area's scoring approach. The reason given is that the auxiliary points concern finer-grained, concrete cases; different cases for the same soul can have very different values, so one score cannot summarize them. The source therefore uses an overall level to assess basic capability. It gives an example in which one case may have thousands of copies/units while another has hundreds or tens, and says the same numeric amount can represent different underlying capabilities at different levels (L0383–L0396).

**Handling:** Preserve the auxiliary level as a case-sensitive, overall basic-capability assessment as described by the source. Do not flatten individual case values into one common numerical score or assume that equal numbers across levels mean equal capability.

## 3. Cross-cutting cautions supported by the source

1. **No universal score direction:** The source explicitly identifies one reverse-value point and separately identifies points with no simple high/low good/bad direction. These are distinct qualifications.
2. **No common unit established:** The overview says the scale is not wholly percentage-based and may be unlimited at higher stages; auxiliary sections use levels. This register does not normalize values.
3. **Laboratory results are not practical-performance equivalents:** The source itself says Zones 6–8 are mainly lab-tested, values are reference-only, and practical performance can differ.
4. **Purpose and context can matter:** Some points are explicitly tied to purpose, intention, situation, environment, strategy or experience. These qualifications belong to the points where they appear; do not generalize them to every assessment.
5. **Case-level differences matter:** The auxiliary-section rationale explicitly rejects summarizing all case-level variation with a single number.
6. **Source comments remain source comments:** Subject-specific explanations attached to RR/88/JJ values are preserved as comments, not elevated into universal scoring rules.

## 4. Items not established by this extraction

The inspected source passages do not provide a complete operational scoring specification. In particular, this register does not establish:

- what `RR`, `88` and `JJ` formally mean;
- a common unit or conversion across all assessment points;
- a formula for applying the reverse-value designation;
- universal better/worse directions for points not explicitly qualified;
- a calibration procedure, repeatability standard, uncertainty interval, or validated relationship between laboratory and practical values;
- independent scientific validity, reliability, or reproducibility.

These remain open source questions. They should not be filled by inference from the displayed numbers or from one point's explanatory comment.
