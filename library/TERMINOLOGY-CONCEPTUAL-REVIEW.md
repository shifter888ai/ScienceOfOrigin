# Terminology Conceptual Review — First Core Pass

**Status:** Working review; no glossary decisions or public-page changes are made by this document.  
**Purpose:** Compare current framework pages with preserved historical source layers, identify compatible readings and unresolved distinctions, and prepare owner review.  
**Method:** Source wording is quoted or summarized with repository-relative locators. Interpretations below are explicitly marked as review readings, not silently promoted to definitions or external findings.

## 1. Consciousness and Freewill

### Current source statements

- `science-of-consciousness.html`, section “Consciousness and Freewill”: the current research position treats Consciousness and Freewill as “the same nature of Origin, described by two different human words”; they are not two independent natures or separate research subjects. The page also says Spectrum of Consciousness and Spectrum of Freewill refer to the same Spectrum.
- `consciousness-freewill.html`, section “The descriptive relationship”: Consciousness describes the Source from the perspective of attributes; Freewill describes it from the perspective of functionality. They are related descriptive perspectives, not separate entities or interchangeable definitions.
- `origin.html`, “Descriptive perspectives on Origin”: Consciousness is the perspective of attributes; Freewill is the perspective of functionality. Both are among the five named natures or aspects.
- `nature-of-origin.html`, historical experiential account, section E: Consciousness and Freewill are separately listed as approximate descriptions from the perspectives of attributes and functionality.
- Earlier project materials listed Science of Consciousness and Science of Freewill separately; the current Science of Consciousness page explicitly preserves that as research history.

### Review reading

These statements can be read consistently if “same nature” means the same underlying nature of Origin is being described through two distinct human perspectives. That does **not** make the words identical in meaning: attributes and functionality remain different descriptive angles. Nor does it require two independent underlying natures.

### Distinctions to preserve

1. Same underlying nature does not mean interchangeable definitions.
2. Attribute-perspective and functionality-perspective remain distinct.
3. Historical separate research-field organization must remain visible as history, not be silently erased.
4. The same-Spectrum statement applies to Spectrum of Consciousness and Spectrum of Freewill; it does not establish that Spectrum of Things is the same Spectrum.

### Status

**Current framework reading:** compatible when “same nature” is distinguished from “same descriptive perspective.” No new glossary wording is proposed in this pass.

## 2. Zero, Idle and Infinity

### Current source statements

- `zero-idle-state-of-origin.html`: Zero represents the idle state of Origin; it also represents Infinity before manifestation. The page explicitly says “before manifestation” is not necessarily a time before and does not treat Zero as a numerical starting point, nothingness, physical vacuum or manifested object.
- `origin.html`: Zero represents the idle state of Origin and Infinity before manifestation. Idle is not absence of capability; Origin may be idle, active, or idle in one aspect while active in another.
- `terminology.html`, “Zero and Idle”: “0 = Infinity” is an observed understanding, not an arithmetic equation. Zero, idle and Infinity before manifestation are connected descriptions, not synonyms for origination or a manifested thing.
- `zero-origin.html`: the current evolving account says Infinity is a nature of Origin, not Origin itself; Zero is the idle state of Origin and Infinity before manifestation.
- `zero-origin-research-working-record.html`, Part II, section 3: Zero represents “the idle of Origin” and “undefined Infinity.” The illustration “0 begets 1 / 1 begets 2 / 2 begets 3 / 3 begets all” is expressly described as a human-friendly description, not a literal sequence, mechanism, pattern or rule. It then states “Zero begets all.”
- `zero-origin-research-working-record.html`, Part I: an earlier draft says “Origin is Freewill itself” and “Freewill is Infinity.” This is an earlier historical formulation and must not be silently substituted for the current distinction between Origin and its described natures.

### Review reading

The current pages support this working relationship:

- **Zero** names or represents the idle state of Origin.
- **Idle** describes that state, without implying incapability or simple inactivity.
- **Infinity before manifestation** describes the Infinity associated with Origin in that idle-state context.
- **“0 = Infinity”** is a reported/observed understanding, not arithmetic identity.
- **“Zero begets all”** is retained as an illustrative statement in the historical working record; the adjacent text explicitly rejects reading the 0–1–2–3 illustration as a literal mechanism or chronological sequence.

### Distinctions to preserve

1. Origin is not defined as Infinity; Infinity is described as a nature or closest human-understandable description.
2. “Before manifestation” is not automatically chronological.
3. Idle does not mean no capability, freewill or possibility.
4. The 0–1–2–3 illustration is not an asserted physical or metaphysical mechanism.
5. Historical “Origin = Freewill = Infinity” wording belongs to an earlier layer and should remain traceable as such.

### Status

**Current framework reading:** internally coherent at the descriptive level, while the precise nature of the idle state and its relation to manifestation remain open research questions.

## 3. Origin, Oneness and the Fake Origin model

### Current source statements

- `origin.html`: Origin is the Ultimate Source, not a beginning in time or an object among objects. “True Origin” refers to “Oneness Origin,” meaning one ultimate Origin rather than multiple independent ultimate sources.
- `origin-models.html`: Fake Origin is described as a manifestation originated from True Origin—an imitation that presents relative independence as absolute independence. It is explicitly not a second ultimate Origin.
- `origin-and-its-imitation.html`: distinguishes resemblance, source-like function, claims of sourcehood and being the ultimate Origin. A manifestation may act as an immediate source within a domain without becoming the ultimate Origin.
- `oneness-division.html`: distinguishes structural oneness from foundational Oneness Origin. A system may function as one while retaining distinct components; structural examples do not prove the foundational proposition about Origin.

### Review reading

The pages distinguish at least three different uses of “one” or “source”:

1. **Foundational Oneness:** the framework's proposition that there is one ultimate Origin.
2. **Structural unity:** a system or collection functioning as one while retaining distinguishable parts.
3. **Relative/immediate sourcehood:** a manifestation generating or organizing further manifestations within a domain.

The proposed Fake Origin model belongs to the third category when a manifested construct presents relative independence as absolute independence. It is not a competing ultimate source.

### Distinctions to preserve

- True Origin / Oneness Origin is not interchangeable with structural oneness.
- Immediate sourcehood is not ultimate sourcehood.
- Relative independence is not absolute independence.
- Do not automatically equate every historical use of 虚拟, 幻相 or 虚假 with the specific current Fake Origin model.

### Status

**Current framework reading:** the conceptual categories are explicitly distinguished in current pages. The criteria for identifying a particular manifestation as Fake Origin remain a separate research question.

## 4. Spirit, Void and Voidness

### Current source statements

- `origin.html` and `zero-origin.html`: Spirit is among the five named natures/aspects; the current Origin page describes Spirit as the perspective of nature and the closest description of the Source's nature. Void is also among the five named natures/aspects.
- `nature-of-origin.html`, historical experiential account, section E: Spirit is a description from the perspective of nature; Voidness is a description from a visual perspective, associated with the Source being still, not in motion and not creating, described there as non-manifestation or nothingness. Infinity is listed in that historical passage as a definitional perspective.
- `terminology.html`, “Origin and Infinity”: Voidness is described as a visual perspective on the Source in stillness and non-manifestation.
- The current five-name list uses **Void**, while the historical perspective passage and current Terminology entry use **Voidness**.

### Review reading

The source record currently supports a distinction between **Void** as a named nature/aspect in the current five-name list and **Voidness** as a visual perspective associated with stillness/non-manifestation in the historical account. The sources inspected here do not establish that these are identical terms, nor do they establish a complete definition of Void.

Likewise, the inspected sources do not establish a one-to-one translation or identity map between Spirit, Soul, 灵魂 and 灵体. Those mappings must remain open unless a direct source statement is located.

### Questions retained

1. Is Voidness a perspective on Void, a distinct term, or a historical description later renamed as Void?
2. Does the current use of Void intentionally retain the historical visual/non-manifestation description, or is its scope broader?
3. What is the exact relationship, if any, between Spirit and Soul / 灵魂 / 灵体?

### Status

**Unresolved.** Preserve the current and historical forms separately; do not standardize Void/Voidness or Spirit/Soul translations yet.

## 5. Infinity: nature, description and research subject

### Current source statements

- `origin.html`: Infinity is the closest description of Origin that humans can understand, but it is not a definition of Origin; the page also lists Infinity among the five named natures/aspects.
- `zero-origin.html`: “Infinity is a nature of Origin, not Origin itself.”
- `zero-idle-state-of-origin.html`: Zero represents Infinity before manifestation.
- `infinity-of-imagination.html`: examines the proposed Infinity of imagination; it says this is not a separate force alongside Origin, but the proposed unrestricted scope of imagination associated with Origin.
- `terminology.html`: Infinity is both a descriptive perspective/nature associated with Origin and a key research domain; it is not treated as a numerical quantity that can itself be directly measured.

### Review reading

“Infinity” appears in several related roles that should not be collapsed:

1. A named nature/aspect associated with Origin.
2. A closest human-understandable description of Origin, without defining Origin.
3. The Infinity associated with the idle state before manifestation.
4. The proposed unrestricted scope of imagination associated with Origin.
5. A research domain in which particular manifestations, relationships and implications may be investigated without claiming to measure or exhaust Infinity itself.

### Status

**Partly clarified.** The current sources distinguish these uses, while the relationships among Infinity, possibility, imagination and manifestation remain open.

## 6. Imagination, Origination and Manifestation — initial cross-check

### Current source statements

- `origin.html`: imagination is used in a broad foundational sense for the proposed capacity through which possibilities and manifestations arise from Origin. All manifestations are understood as imaginations of Origin; imagination and manifestation are not separate stages in this account.
- `infinity-of-imagination.html`: distinguishes imagination (scope and possibilities associated with Origin), origination (capability/process through which manifestations come about) and manifestation (what comes about).
- `terminology.html`: preserves the same functional distinctions and identifies non-origination and non-manifestation as separate terms.
- `zero-origin-research-working-record.html`, Part I: an earlier draft uses stronger identity language around Origin, Freewill and Infinity. It is a historical layer, not a replacement for the current role distinctions.

### Review reading

The current pages distinguish the **conceptual roles** of imagination, origination and manifestation, while also stating that imagination and manifestation are not separate stages in the foundational account. These statements need not be collapsed into a single synonym set: a manifestation may be described as an imagination of Origin, while “origination” names the capability/process through which it comes about.

### Status

**Working distinction supported by current pages.** The precise relationship between imagination as capacity/scope and imagination as the manifestation itself remains a topic for continued conceptual review.
