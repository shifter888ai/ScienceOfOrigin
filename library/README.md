## Purpose

This directory establishes the working foundation for the complete Science of Origin research library. The library is the common research base for all existing and future fields, applications, topics, cases, terminology, and discoveries.

The library is not limited to the current research framework, the current website, or material that appears relevant at first glance. All available material must remain discoverable, including material whose significance or relationships are not yet known.

## One source base, connected layers

The library will connect these layers without turning them into competing or duplicated archives:

1. Original source archive
2. Source and provenance catalogue
3. Record catalogue
4. Case and experience library
5. Glossary and terminology database
6. Topic and subject indexes
7. Concept and relationship network
8. Measurements, experiments, and evidence records
9. Derived analyses, reports, and publications

A source is preserved once. Records, cases, terms, concepts, and research views link back to it and may link to one another.

## Implementation sequence

1. Inventory all available sources and identify gaps.
2. Establish stable identifiers and metadata.
3. Preserve and verify originals before extracting or transforming content.
4. Register source files, versions, checksums, provenance, language, and access status.
5. Build source-linked records and case extraction.
6. Build the multilingual glossary and terminology relationships.
7. Add cross-source search, relationship discovery, and research views.
8. Validate completeness, traceability, and retrieval.

The sequence is a practical implementation order, not a restriction on what may be discovered or added.

## Preservation rules

- Never silently overwrite an original source.
- Never replace an original with a summary, translation, cleaned transcript, or extracted passage.
- Keep derived material separately identified and linked to its source.
- Preserve original language, wording, structure, context, and available attachments.
- Record unknown, missing, uncertain, partial, or inferred metadata explicitly.
- Retain historical terminology and changing meanings.
- Do not force a record into one exclusive topic.
- Do not treat a source claim, personal report, interpretation, or proposed mechanism as independently verified merely because it is archived.
- Do not publish private or restricted material without appropriate permission and review.
