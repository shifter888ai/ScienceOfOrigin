A fundamental research dimension

Things & Life

An open exploration of the nature of Things and Life Forms, how they come into manifestation, and how life may relate to Consciousness, Freewill and Origination.

The nature of Things

Things are among the subjects of manifestation investigated by Science of Origin. What constitutes a Thing? Are Things best understood through their properties, structure, relationships, behavior or capacity to interact?

The word Thing is used broadly at this stage. Its scope and criteria require clarification through research rather than being fixed by an assumed definition.

The nature of Life

Life is another fundamental subject of investigation. What distinguishes a Life Form from other Things? Possible points of examination include organization, activity, continuity, development, interaction and the capacity to maintain or transform a system.

These are questions for investigation, not a proposed universal definition. The characteristics familiar from known terrestrial life should not automatically be treated as the limits of every possible form of life.

Living and non-living

The distinction between living and non-living Things is useful in many contexts, but its fundamental basis remains open to examination within this project.

Are living and non-living fundamentally different forms, or might they share properties and differ by degree, organization or capability? Could there be forms that do not fit the categories currently used? Clear criteria and observable distinctions would be needed to investigate these possibilities.

Life and Consciousness

The relationship between Life and Consciousness is a central open question. Does every Life Form involve Consciousness? Could Consciousness exist in forms that are not recognized as life? Are there different relationships between Consciousness and different forms of manifestation?

Science of Origin does not assume that human experience provides a complete model for all possible manifestations. The terms and criteria used to investigate this relationship may need to extend beyond familiar examples.

Life and Freewill

The relationship between Life, Freewill and Origination also requires investigation. How, if at all, do the capacities of a Life Form relate to intention, choice and the realization of intentions?

Different forms of life may have different capabilities and may operate under different principles and rules. Whether these differences involve different forms or degrees of Freewill, and how they relate to origination, remains an open research question.

Different forms of life

Known forms of life should not be assumed to exhaust the possibilities. Life may, as an open research possibility, take forms that differ substantially from familiar biological examples or that arise under different conditions and systems.

Unfamiliarity alone does not establish that a proposed form exists. Any candidate would need to be described clearly, distinguished from non-living systems using stated criteria, and examined through appropriate observations or methods.

Origin of Things and Life

How do Things and Life Forms come into manifestation? This question connects the nature of Things and Life with Origin and Origination.

The project investigates whether different Things and Life Forms may originate through different processes, whether origination may occur within existing manifestations, and whether interactions among systems may give rise to new forms. The relationship between the ultimate source and particular manifestations should remain distinct from the mechanisms through which those manifestations arise.

Beyond known forms

There may be forms of Things and Life that are not yet recognized or understood. Exploring such possibilities requires openness without treating possibility as proof.

New observations, comparisons and methods may reveal properties or distinctions that current categories do not capture. Research should record what is observed, what is inferred, what remains unknown and what evidence would be needed to evaluate each possibility.

Research directions

  • Clarifying the scope and criteria of the terms Thing, Life and Life Form.
  • Identifying properties that distinguish living from non-living manifestations, while examining the limits of that distinction.
  • Investigating possible relationships between Life and Consciousness.
  • Examining how Life, Freewill, intention and Origination may relate.
  • Comparing different forms of life without assuming that familiar examples define all possibilities.
  • Investigating how Things and Life Forms originate and how they may arise through interactions among systems.
  • Developing observation and measurement methods appropriate to different proposed forms of manifestation.
  • Distinguishing observed properties and reproducible findings from interpretations and unresolved possibilities.
Research status: This page establishes an open research area and records questions for investigation. It does not assert that unfamiliar forms of life or particular relationships among Life, Consciousness and Freewill have been demonstrated. Definitions, criteria, methods and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
