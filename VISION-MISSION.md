# Science of Origin — Vision, Mission & Strategic Direction

## 1. Vision

To contribute to the advancement of human civilization by expanding humanity's understanding of Origin, existence, and the possibilities of manifestation.

Science of Origin seeks to make room for discoveries and understandings that may extend beyond currently established boundaries of human knowledge. Its long-term vision is an expanding body of knowledge that can be examined, developed, and carried forward by future generations.

## 2. Mission

Science of Origin investigates Origin and its manifestations without prescribing in advance what form inquiry must take. Understandings develop through continued exploration, using approaches appropriate to each subject.

Its mission is to communicate these understandings clearly and humbly, make their foundations available for examination, and invite capable independent thinkers to investigate them further and share what they discover.

Science of Origin investigates Origin; it does not claim to define Origin.

## 3. Intended Audience: An Initial 100

The website and its core materials are intended first for a small initial audience of approximately 100 people who may be especially capable of recognizing, examining, developing, and communicating unfamiliar ideas.

The aim is not to rank human worth or intelligence, nor to create followers. The number 100 is an initial outreach focus, not an exclusive boundary. Relevant qualities may include:

- Independent and original thinking.
- Intellectual curiosity and openness to unfamiliar possibilities.
- Willingness to examine observations and methods rather than accept or reject ideas solely by convention.
- Ability to distinguish observation, experience, interpretation, experimentation, and established findings.
- Capacity to communicate clearly and contribute constructively to further inquiry.

These people are invited to examine the work independently. Agreement is not a prerequisite for engagement.

## 4. What the Website Is For

The website is a durable point of access to the research, not merely a personal record or a mass-marketing channel. It should:

1. **Transmit:** Present the founder's observed and experimented understandings faithfully, clearly, and humbly.
2. **Enable examination:** Make the reasoning, context, methods, records, and research status available wherever possible.
3. **Invite independent inquiry:** Help readers formulate questions, examine the material, and pursue further observations and experiments.
4. **Support further communication:** Make important ideas understandable and referenceable so that others can communicate and develop them accurately.
5. **Preserve continuity:** Organize the knowledge so that research can continue beyond the founder's active involvement.

## 5. Research and Communication Principles

### Open-ended inquiry

Science of Origin remains open to different forms of inquiry. Depending on the subject, exploration may involve observation, experience, conceptual exploration, theoretical development, hypotheses, philosophical examination, experimentation, or other approaches. No single method or category is prescribed in advance. The basis and status of each understanding should be made clear, distinguishing observations and experiences, interpretations, explanations, experimental findings, and independently reproduced results.

### Intellectual openness

No current understanding, method, or established body of knowledge is assumed to exhaust the nature or possibilities of Origin. Unfamiliar observations and possibilities remain open to investigation.

### Evidential clarity

Communication must distinguish, as applicable:

- **Observed or experienced understandings:** What has been directly observed or experienced, and by whom or in what context where disclosure is appropriate.
- **Experimented understandings and findings:** What has been examined through described experimental procedures.
- **Independently reproduced findings:** What has been reproduced independently, with the scope and conditions made clear.
- **Interpretations and explanations:** How observations or results are being understood.
- **Open investigations:** Questions, possibilities, and relationships that remain under examination.

A lack of independent reproduction does not by itself erase the value of a first-hand observation as a starting point. Equally, an observation or interpretation should not be described as an independently established result when it has not met that standard.

### Humility and independence

Present what is understood without demanding belief. Give readers enough context to examine claims for themselves, disagree where warranted, and pursue independent conclusions. The objective is not to create followers, but to enable independent minds to carry inquiry further.

## 6. Long-Term Continuity

Science of Origin should become a body of work that does not depend on the founder's permanent participation. This requires, over time:

- A well-organized and maintained research archive.
- Clear records of original observations, experiences, experimental procedures, and relevant context.
- Traceable distinctions between observations, interpretations, experimental results, and independently reproduced findings.
- Accessible explanations of terminology, research architecture, and relationships among subjects.
- Documentation that allows others to continue, question, refine, and extend the research.

The founder's eventual step back from active work is a continuity objective: the research should be capable of developing responsibly beyond any one individual.

## 7. Strategic Direction for the Website

The website should be developed for depth, clarity, and intellectual usefulness rather than mass appeal.

- Lead with the purpose and significance of the research, not promotional claims.
- Give serious readers a clear route from the overall framework to foundational subjects, research branches, methods, and detailed records.
- Explain specialized terminology precisely without diluting the underlying ideas.
- Make the status and basis of important statements visible.
- Preserve the distinct identity and scope of each research branch.
- Avoid presenting aspirations, proposed relationships, or open investigations as established findings.
- Prefer durable, citable research materials over short-lived promotional content.

## 8. Guiding Statement

**Make the understandings discoverable. Make their foundations examinable. Keep the inquiry open. Enable independent minds to carry the work further.**
