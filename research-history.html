Central Research History

One continuous body of research · Many perspectives that overlap completely

This is the shared historical record of Science of Origin. It brings together research developments, discussions, discoveries, experiences, questions and original sources across all subjects and fields—not as separate histories, but as connected parts of one continuous body of research.

Purpose

The Central Research History records developments across Science of Origin research, including Zero-Origin Research, Science of Consciousness, Spectrum research, methodological exploration, applications and future directions. Its entries may include material originating before this website.

A record is not exclusive to the subject or field in which it first arose. One record may be relevant to multiple topics, research perspectives and applications. It should be preserved once as a source record and connected wherever it is relevant, rather than copied into separate field-specific histories.

One body of research, different perspectives

Current research pages, historical records and original sources are not separate bodies of knowledge. They are different ways of presenting, following and examining the same research.

Organizational distinctions are introduced for human reading, navigation and communication. They do not imply fundamental separation. These perspectives overlap completely: a current understanding may reinterpret an earlier record; a historical discussion may contribute to several fields; an original experience may inform multiple research directions; and a development in any area may affect the wider framework.

The archive therefore remains open to connections that were not recognized when a record was first created. New relationships may be added later without changing what the original record documented at the time.

Chronology, provenance and later interpretation

Dates and sequence should be shown when they are supported by the original record or reliable publication history. Where the date or exact chronology is not available, the archive should not invent one or force a record into an artificial timeline.

Historical content should retain its original meaning and context. Later annotations, corrections, cross-references and interpretations may be added, but they should be identifiable as later additions rather than silently inserted into an earlier record.

Where original discussions, documents, measurements or other source materials have been selected for public sharing, records should link to them so readers can trace the basis and development of the published account. A summary is not a substitute for an original source.

How the history connects with current research

The Science of Consciousness research page presents the current research perspective. The Research Framework, Research Direction, Research and Discoveries, and other subject pages provide additional views into the same undertaking.

The existing Research Map shows selected relationships. A more comprehensive relational map will be developed in a later stage, with the aim of tracing connections across research subjects, records, sources, concepts and website pages.

Continuously expanding record

This page is an initial shared index, not a claim that every historical discussion or source has already been recovered and published. It will expand as further records are identified, checked, connected and made available.

The history and the current research evolve together. Neither is a boundary around the other, and neither is separate from the whole.
