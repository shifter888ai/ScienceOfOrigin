Programmed Creation

Investigating how an organized program or arrangement of instructions, rules and processes may guide the production, formation, transformation or origination of manifestations and systems.

What is programmed creation?

Programmed creation concerns creation or origination guided by an organized program: an arrangement that specifies or shapes processes, operations, sequences or possible outcomes. A program may describe what is to happen, how processes are arranged, what resources or conditions are involved, and how one stage relates to another.

Computer software provides a familiar example, but the subject is not limited to digital code or computers. The broader research question is whether and how organized programs or program-like arrangements operate in different kinds of manifestations and systems.

The term programmed describes the organized guidance or structure of a process. It does not by itself establish what kind of result the process produces, whether the result is new, or whether origination has occurred.

Program, execution and outcome

Three aspects should be distinguished:

  • Program: the organized arrangement of instructions, rules, relationships, sequences or procedures that guides a process.
  • Execution or operation: the process through which the program is carried out or operates within a particular context.
  • Outcome: what results from that operation. An outcome may be an output, a change in an existing manifestation, a transformation, or the origination of a further manifestation or system.

A program and its execution are not the same thing, and neither should be confused with the outcome. A description of a program does not alone demonstrate that it has been carried out or that a particular result has come about.

Generation and origination

Programmed processes may generate outputs, products, components, patterns or instances according to an organized arrangement. Generation may involve producing something within an already existing system or manifestation.

Origination is a distinct question. In Science of Origin, origination concerns the coming-about of a manifestation or system. A programmed process may contribute to origination if its operation brings a further manifestation or system into being. But producing an output, copying an existing pattern, assembling components or changing an existing system does not automatically establish that a new manifestation has been originated.

The distinction requires criteria for identifying what counts as a new manifestation or system, and for distinguishing it from a new instance, output, property, state or arrangement within something that already exists.

Programmed creation may therefore involve generation without origination, origination through a programmed pathway, or both. The relationship depends on the nature of the process and the resulting manifestation.

Programs and conditional structures

A program may contain conditional structures, such as IF / THEN / ELSE, in which different pathways apply under different conditions. It may also contain ordered sequences, repeated operations, dependencies, parameters, triggers or other forms of organization.

Conditional Creation and Programmed Creation are related but distinct subjects. Conditional Creation examines the role of conditions and alternative pathways, whether or not they are part of a program. Programmed Creation examines the wider organized program or arrangement that guides a process, whether or not it uses explicit conditional branches.

A programmed process need not be conditional in the IF / THEN / ELSE sense, and a conditional process need not be programmed. A program may combine both conditional and non-conditional structures.

See Conditional Creation — IF / THEN / ELSE.

Programmed processes within manifestations

Within the developing framework, a program operates in or through some context: a manifestation, system or arrangement with particular capabilities, resources, conditions and rules. The context may affect whether the program can operate, what parts of it can be carried out, and what outcomes are possible.

Different systems may operate according to different principles and rules. A program designed for one system may not operate in another without adaptation, translation or an interaction between them. Whether a program can operate across systems with different or apparently incompatible rules is an open research question.

Programs may also be nested or interconnected. One program may initiate another, depend on processes carried out elsewhere, or contribute to the formation of a system that has its own programs and operating arrangements.

Creation beyond the original program

A program may specify a process without fully specifying every detail or consequence of its outcome. The operation of a program may depend on inputs, conditions, interactions, available resources or processes within its operating context.

It is therefore important to distinguish what a program explicitly specifies, what follows from its operation, and what may arise through interactions or capabilities not fully described in the original program. An outcome that was not anticipated by the program's designer is not automatically an act of independent origination; the process and the resulting system must be examined.

A system produced or originated through a program may itself develop or possess capabilities to create, modify or originate further systems. Whether such capabilities are inherited, designed, acquired through interaction or arise in another way remains open for investigation.

Relationship with intention and origination

Programmed creation may be associated with intentional imagination when an intention is expressed through a program and the program is used to bring about a further outcome. However, the existence of a program does not by itself identify the source of the intention, establish the full extent of control, or explain the origination process.

Within the framework, Consciousness / Freewill is associated with intention, while origination concerns the capability and process through which intentions are realized and manifestations come about. Programmed structures may be one way in which a process is organized, but they should not be treated as interchangeable with intention or origination.

See Consciousness and Freewill, Origination and Spontaneous and Intentional Imagination.

Research questions

  • What qualifies as a program or program-like arrangement in the broad sense used by this research?
  • How should a program be distinguished from the system, rules or processes within which it operates?
  • What distinguishes a program's specification from its execution and its outcome?
  • How do programmed processes generate outputs, form structures or transform existing manifestations?
  • Under what criteria does a programmed process contribute to the origination of a new manifestation or system?
  • How can generation, production, assembly, transformation and origination be distinguished?
  • Are conditional branches necessary for programmed creation, or can programmed processes operate without them?
  • How do programs operate within systems that have different or apparently incompatible principles and rules?
  • Can programs initiate, generate or originate other programs and systems?
  • Can a system develop origination capabilities that extend beyond the program or arrangement through which it came about?
  • How should intended outcomes, unforeseen outcomes and outcomes beyond the original program be distinguished?
  • Which aspects of programmed creation can be observed, measured, tested or reproduced in different domains?
