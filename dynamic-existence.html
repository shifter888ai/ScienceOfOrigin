Dynamic Existence

Origination, Frequency, Spectrum, and Identity

An exploratory model proposing that existence is continuously originated and that the identity of a thing changes whenever its complete spectral composition changes.

Science of Origin · 27 November 2026 · Working research paper

Abstract

This paper presents a proposed account of existence in which things do not persist as absolutely static entities. Instead, existence is understood as a dynamic process sustained by repeated acts of origination. The paper introduces a connected sequence of concepts: Zero, Origin, imaginating, origination, frequency, spectrum, identity and dynamic existence.

Within this model, frequency is understood more broadly than a measured rate of an already-existing oscillation: it refers to repeated origination at intervals. A thing's identity is associated with its frequency, its complete spectral composition and the relationships among its frequency components. Every change in that composition therefore corresponds to a different actual identity, while ordinary descriptions of continuity group successive identities under one name.

These are foundational propositions for investigation, not established scientific conclusions. The paper identifies conceptual distinctions and questions that require operational definitions, measurement and reproducible examination.

1. The problem of treating existence as static

Everyday language commonly treats a person or object as one enduring thing. A person is called by the same name over many years; an object is treated as the same object while its properties change. Such descriptions are useful for communication and practical organization. They do not, by themselves, establish that the underlying identity remains absolutely unchanged.

This research model starts from a different perspective: existence is dynamic. What appears to be one persisting thing may instead be a continuous sequence of successive states or identities. The apparent persistence of an object may be a useful human classification of that sequence.

2. Zero and Origin

In this model, Zero is described as infinite but idle: not a limited quantity, but a state in which origination is not occurring. Origin itself is treated as prior to any particular origination and is described as having zero frequency while idle.

The phrase “zero means infinite but idle” is a conceptual statement within this research model. It is not the conventional mathematical or physical definition of zero, nor does it follow from the use of zero frequency in established physics. The distinction is important: the paper is proposing a broader ontological meaning that must be defined and examined on its own terms.

3. Imaginating and origination

The term imaginating is used here for the act of originating. It is not simply a synonym for ordinary mental imagery. It names the proposed originating activity through which a manifestation is brought about.

Origination is the occurrence or process through which something comes into existence. In the proposed account, an act of imaginating may be repeated, and the continuation of those acts sustains the corresponding manifestation. If the originating activity ceases, the manifestation is proposed to cease and return to an idle or non-manifest state.

This is a foundational proposition of the model. Its meaning, scope and possible observable consequences remain open research questions.

4. Frequency as repeated origination

In conventional measurement, frequency describes the number of repeating events or cycles per unit time. This paper uses the term in a proposed ontological sense: frequency is the repeated origination of the same imagination at intervals.

For illustration, suppose an originating event is repeated at evenly spaced intervals of one millisecond. That corresponds to a repetition rate of one thousand events per second. In the proposed model, the repeated acts are what sustain the manifestation over that interval. The numerical example illustrates the model's language; it does not demonstrate that physical existence is in fact sustained by such repeated acts.

The model also proposes that anything originated has a frequency, while Origin in its idle state has zero frequency. These statements require definitions that allow the proposed frequency to be distinguished from conventional oscillation, periodicity and other measurable rates.

5. Spectrum and spectral composition

A spectrum, in this model, is not merely one frequency or one isolated measurement. It is the complete composition of frequencies associated with a thing, including the relationships among those frequencies. The identity of a thing is therefore not attributed to a single component alone.

The composition may include multiple kinds of measurable variation. A practical investigation must specify which quantities are measured, the range and resolution of each instrument, how measurements are synchronized, and how different signal types are represented without treating unlike quantities as interchangeable.

6. Identity and continuity

The proposed model associates identity with three connected aspects:

  • the frequency or repetition characteristics involved;
  • the complete spectral composition;
  • the relationships among the frequencies within that composition.

Its central identity proposition is that every spectral change produces a different actual identity. What people describe as one thing remaining the same despite change is, in this account, continuity imposed by human definition: a name or category is used to group a succession of changing identities.

This distinction does not make ordinary identity labels useless. It distinguishes practical continuity of reference from the stronger claim of absolute identity. Whether a defined spectral change corresponds to a meaningful identity change is a question that requires explicit criteria and evidence.

7. Dynamic existence

The sequence proposed by this paper can be summarized as:

Zero → Origin → Imaginating → Origination → Frequency → Spectrum → Identity → Dynamic Existence

This sequence expresses the conceptual relationships proposed by the model. It should not be mistaken for a verified physical chain of mechanisms. The central proposition is that existence is not a static possession but an ongoing dynamic process, and that what is called a thing is continuously becoming.

8. Questions for further investigation

  • Can “imaginating” and “origination” be given operational definitions that permit independent examination?
  • What observations could distinguish repeated origination from conventional physical oscillation or change?
  • What constitutes the complete spectrum of a specified research subject, and how can completeness be assessed?
  • Which spectral changes are measurable, and what criteria would relate them to identity transformation?
  • How should measurement resolution, uncertainty, sampling intervals and instrument limitations be reported?
  • Can independent investigators reproduce the same spectral observations and analyses?

9. Research status and scope

This paper records a proposed foundational model for exploration within Science of Origin. Its propositions are not presented as established findings of conventional physics, biology or neuroscience. Established measurements may help investigate parts of the model, but observations of changing signals alone do not prove the proposed account of origination or identity.

The next practical connection is Spectrum of Things: Real-Time Spectral Observation, Analysis, and Identity Transformation, which considers how multi-sensor measurements could be organized to investigate spectral fluctuations and their relationships.

The wider research framework remains open to different methods and disciplines, with conclusions proportionate to the evidence available.

Working status: Foundational exploratory paper. Concepts and propositions may be refined as definitions, measurement methods and experimental evidence develop.
