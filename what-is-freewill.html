What Is Freewill?

An Exploration of the Nature, Freedom and Expressions of Freewill

Initial research account · Research is ongoing

Abstract

This page explores the fundamental nature of Freewill and its relationship with Consciousness, thought, imagination, creation, change and intention.

Freewill is Consciousness in nature. From another perspective, Freewill describes the freedom of thought, imagination, creation and change.

This exploration considers the absence of any real limitation on Freewill, including the freedom to imagine limitations, impossibilities and the absence of freedom itself. It also examines the relationship between intention and happening, including spontaneous and deliberate intentions and the possibility of multiple intentions existing simultaneously, even when they are contradictory.

This is an initial foundation for Science of Freewill. The descriptions and relationships presented here provide a basis for further exploration and development.

1. Introduction

What is Freewill?

Freewill is commonly approached through the idea of choice or the ability to act freely. However, these descriptions may not encompass its full nature or range of expressions.

Within Science of Origin, Freewill is explored from the perspective of Origin and its relationship with Consciousness.

The purpose of this page is to establish a foundational understanding of Freewill before proceeding into more detailed investigations of its properties, expressions and relationships.

2. Freewill and Consciousness

In nature, Freewill is Consciousness.

Freewill and Consciousness are descriptions of the same fundamental nature from different perspectives.

  • Consciousness describes the nature.
  • Freewill describes freedom inherent in that nature and its expressions.

There is only ONE Consciousness and ONE Freewill. The appearance of different expressions does not imply that the fundamental nature has divided into multiple independent ultimate sources.

Freewill should therefore not be understood as something separate from Consciousness in nature.

3. The Freedom of Freewill

Freewill encompasses freedom of:

  1. Thought.
  2. Imagination.
  3. Creation.
  4. Change.

These are fundamental expressions of Freewill, rather than an exhaustive account of everything it can express.

The freedom of Freewill is not restricted to particular thoughts, possibilities, forms of creation or kinds of change.

Freewill includes the freedom to think, imagine, create and change, as well as the freedom not to do so.

4. No Real Limitation

There is no real limit to Freewill.

Any perceived or imagined limitation is itself an imagination.

Freewill can imagine restrictions, boundaries, impossibilities and the absence of freedom. It can also imagine that something cannot be imagined.

The imagination of a limitation does not establish an actual limitation of Freewill.

Even the imagination of impossibility remains within the freedom of Freewill. Otherwise, Freewill would not be truly free.

Accordingly, the ability to imagine limitations does not mean that Freewill is limited by those imaginations.

5. Freewill, Intention and Happening

Intention and happening are related, but they should not be treated as interchangeable.

Without intention of happening, there is no happening.

This does not mean that every intention must arise deliberately. Intention can emerge spontaneously or deliberately, and spontaneous intention is still intention.

The distinction concerns how intention emerges, rather than whether intention exists.

An intention for something to happen does not, by itself, mean that the intended happening must necessarily be actualized. The relationship between intention and what happens remains an area for further exploration.

6. Multiple and Contradictory Intentions

Freewill can have multiple intentions simultaneously.

These intentions can be contradictory to one another.

The existence of contradictory intentions does not negate Freewill or require that only one intention can exist.

Spontaneous and deliberate intentions can also coexist, including in mixed expressions.

The relationships among multiple intentions, their interactions and their eventual manifestations provide further areas for research.

7. Freewill and Origin

Freewill is one of the five approximate descriptions of the nature and attributes of the Source in the original account of Nature of Origin.

The five descriptions are presented from different perspectives:

  1. Spirit — a description from the perspective of nature.
  2. Consciousness — a description from the perspective of attributes.
  3. Freewill — a description from the perspective of functionality.
  4. Voidness — a description from a visual perspective.
  5. Infinity — a description from a definitional perspective.

These are approximate descriptions, not five separate ultimate sources or a hierarchy.

The original account and its later clarifications should remain distinguishable. This page explores Freewill as one of these perspectives without replacing or collapsing the other descriptions.

8. Relationship to Other Research

Science of Freewill is one of the core research fields within Science of Consciousness.

Its exploration is closely related to several other research topics:

  • Nature of Origin — the original descriptions and later clarifications concerning Origin.
  • Science of Consciousness — the fundamental nature and properties of Consciousness.
  • What Is Intention? — the nature and emergence of spontaneous and deliberate intention.
  • Shared Intentions — multiple participants, shared expressions and the development of intentions.
  • Spectrum / Spectra — the investigation of manifestations and their relationships.

These are related research areas, but their individual subjects and scope should remain distinguishable.

9. Further Research

The initial foundation raises several areas for continued investigation:

  1. The relationship between the nature of Consciousness and the freedom of Freewill.
  2. The full range of thought, imagination, creation and change.
  3. The relationship between imagination and manifestation.
  4. The relationship between intention and happening.
  5. Spontaneous, deliberate and mixed intentions.
  6. The coexistence and interaction of multiple intentions.
  7. The relationship between Freewill and individual manifestations.
  8. The expressions of Freewill within the unified nature of One Consciousness and One Freewill.
  9. The development of appropriate methods for investigating and measuring relevant phenomena.

These areas remain open for further exploration and development.

10. Summary

Freewill is Consciousness in nature.

It describes the freedom of thought, imagination, creation and change.

There is no real limit to Freewill. Any perceived limitation is an imagination, and even the imagination of impossibility is within its freedom.

Intention is related to happening. Without intention of happening, there is no happening. Intention can arise spontaneously or deliberately, and multiple intentions can exist simultaneously, including contradictory intentions.

This initial account establishes a foundation for Science of Freewill while leaving its further properties, expressions and relationships open for continued research.

Research is ongoing. The exploration of Origin has no final conclusion.
