Creation of New Games

Investigating how a new game may be originated, how its rules and boundaries establish a distinct domain of possibilities, and how it relates to the system or manifestation through which it comes about.

What is a game in this research?

In this subject, a game is considered broadly as an organized domain in which particular rules, boundaries and conditions establish a range of possible states, actions, interactions and outcomes. A game may have participants or operating elements, defined roles, objectives or constraints, and processes through which its states and outcomes develop.

This is a working research description, not a final universal definition. A game need not be recreational, digital, computer-based or designed for human entertainment. The relevant question is whether a manifestation or system establishes a distinct, organized domain with its own conditions and possibilities.

Creating a new game versus operating within one

Creating a new game and playing or operating within an existing game are different activities.

  • Creation of a new game: the origination of a new organized domain, with its own defining structure, rules, boundaries or conditions.
  • Operation within an existing game: actions, interactions or events that take place under the game's existing structure and rules.
  • Modification of an existing game: a change to some of its rules, boundaries, components or operating conditions. Depending on the extent and nature of the change, this may or may not amount to the origination of a new game.
  • Generation within a game: producing an object, event, character, level or other content within the existing game. Such generation does not automatically constitute creation of a new game.

The central distinction is whether a new game or domain has come into manifestation, rather than merely something new happening within an already existing one. The criteria for deciding when a modified or expanded game should count as a new game remain open to investigation.

Origination of a game

Within Science of Origin, origination concerns the coming-about of a manifestation or system. The creation of a new game is therefore examined as a possible form of origination: a new organized domain comes about, rather than only a new event or instance within an existing domain.

An existing manifestation or system may be the immediate source or contributor through which a new game comes about. This does not make it a separate ultimate Origin. Within the framework, Origin remains the ultimate source, while manifestations may serve as immediate sources or contributors in further origination.

A new game may be originated intentionally, through a programmed or conditional process, through interactions, or through other pathways that remain to be investigated. No single mechanism should be assumed to account for every possible case.

See Origination, Conditional Creation and Programmed Creation.

Rules, boundaries and possibilities

A game is shaped by the rules and boundaries that define its domain. These may determine what can occur, what actions or interactions are possible, which states are accessible, and what constraints apply to participants or operating elements.

Rules and boundaries may be explicit, implicit, visible or difficult to identify from within the game. Some may be fixed, while others may change during operation or through particular processes. A game may also contain different areas, levels or subsystems with distinct rules and constraints.

The possibilities available within a game are therefore related to its rules and boundaries. A game may offer a broad range of possibilities in some respects and impose strong limitations in others. The apparent scope of a game's possibilities should not be confused with the totality of what may exist beyond its domain.

See Principles and Rules, Boundaries and Domains and Possibility and Potential.

Relationship between the originating system and the new game

The system through which a new game is originated and the game that comes about should be distinguished as related but not necessarily identical manifestations or systems. They may have different rules, structures, boundaries, capabilities and ranges of possibility.

The originating system may establish some of the new game's conditions without necessarily determining every detail of everything that can happen within it. The new game may contain processes, interactions or systems that develop in ways not fully specified by the originating arrangement.

Whether the originating system can observe, enter, modify or control every aspect of the new game is a separate question. Origination does not by itself establish unlimited control over the resulting domain.

Nested games and further origination

A game may contain systems that themselves have the capability to originate further games. This is one context in which independent origination capabilities may be investigated. A newly originated game may therefore become an originating context for another game, producing nested or connected domains.

Such relationships need not form a simple hierarchy. Games may interact, overlap, share components or connect through processes that cross their boundaries. A system within one game may contribute to the origination of a game with a different set of rules and possibilities.

These possibilities raise questions about how origination capabilities develop, whether they can be transferred or acquired, and whether a new game can originate domains that differ fundamentally from the one in which it came about.

Independence and the appearance of separation

Within the developing framework, a new game may operate with rules and boundaries that differ from those of its originating system. This can establish a distinct domain and a degree of relative independence in how the game operates.

Relative independence should not automatically be interpreted as absolute independence or separation from Origin. A game may present itself, or be experienced from within, as a self-contained reality while remaining related to the source and pathways through which it was originated.

The proposed distinction between True Origin and Fake Origin explores a particular model in which a constructed game imitates Origin and presents relative independence as absolute independence. This is a conceptual proposition within the exploratory framework, not an independently verified finding. Not every game should automatically be classified as Fake Origin or as an imitation of Origin.

See True Origin and Fake Origin, including the proposed subjects Constructed Realities and Games and The Physical World as a Game.

Research questions

  • What are the minimum characteristics required for an organized domain to qualify as a game?
  • What distinguishes the origination of a new game from the creation of new content, levels, instances or events within an existing game?
  • When does modification or expansion of an existing game amount to the origination of a new game?
  • Can a new game be originated by a system operating under rules different from those of the new game?
  • How much of a new game's rules, boundaries and possibilities are established by its originating system?
  • Can a game develop processes, capabilities or outcomes not fully specified by its originating arrangement?
  • Can systems within a game originate further games, and how might nested or connected games relate?
  • Can games interact across boundaries or operate under different or apparently incompatible rules?
  • How should relative independence within a game be distinguished from absolute independence?
  • What observable criteria could help identify a game, its boundaries and its origination pathway?
  • How do intentional, conditional, programmed and interaction-based processes contribute to the origination of new games?
  • Can the creation of new games be investigated in domains beyond digital or recreational games?
