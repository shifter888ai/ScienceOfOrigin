# Science of Origin — Website Audit: Research and Sharing Philosophy

**Status:** Internal audit report  
**Date:** 2026-11-27  
**Repository:** `shifter888ai/ScienceOfOrigin`  
**Audit basis:** All 109 HTML pages listed in the repository's `sitemap.xml` at the time of review, read in the context of the published **Research and Sharing Philosophy** page.  
**Scope:** Content consistency only. This is not a technical, accessibility, SEO, security or scientific-validity audit.

## 1. Governing principles

The following six principles are the audit standard:

1. **Research comes first** — pages should not imply that research must pause to satisfy readers' demands.
2. **Sharing is voluntary** — publishing a discovery does not mean every underlying record or material must also be published.
3. **No persuasion obligation** — sharing should not be framed as a duty to convince people or repeatedly defend acceptance.
4. **Independent investigation** — readers can conduct their own observations and experiments and reach their own conclusions.
5. **No assumed support service** — avoid promises to provide extra documents, explanations, tutoring, or ongoing research assistance unless you explicitly choose to offer them.
6. **Keep research open** — don't impose premature endpoints or conclusions on ongoing research.

The published Research and Sharing Philosophy is the public reference. This audit applies it without changing the intended substance of research methods or subject-matter pages.

## 2. Audit method and interpretation

The review was conducted in five passes:

1. Project identity, foundational principles and research architecture.
2. Research methodology, observation, measurement, evidence, verification and reproducibility.
3. Research history, source records, dialogues, working records and discoveries.
4. Subject-matter pages across the seven divisions and cross-cutting research topics.
5. Navigation, contact, search, AI-assisted and other supporting pages.

All sitemap-listed HTML pages were retrieved and included in the content scan. Pages containing language about sharing, evidence, records, publication, independent investigation, reader questions, contact, requests, verification or reproducibility received targeted review. The findings below distinguish a genuine sharing commitment from ordinary descriptions of research practice.

### Finding classifications

- **Consistent:** no conflict with the six principles identified; no change recommended on this audit basis.
- **Potential ambiguity:** wording could be read as creating an expectation or obligation that the philosophy does not establish.
- **Inconsistent:** wording directly conflicts with a principle. No clear direct contradiction was identified in the reviewed public pages.
- **Editorial choice:** optional clarification that may improve consistency but is not required to resolve a conflict.

## 3. Executive result

The website is broadly consistent with the Research and Sharing Philosophy. The six principles are already reflected in several places, especially the open-ended research direction, the directory's statement that readers may reach their own conclusions, and the Entropy of Consciousness page's explicit distinction between a published research architecture and undisclosed detailed assessment materials.

The audit found a small number of pages where wording could be made clearer:

| Priority | Page | Classification | Main issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | `about.html` | Potential ambiguity | “Supporting evidence” and the aim to make concepts and reasoning clear enough to examine, question and refine could be read more broadly than intended. |
| High | `research-discoveries.html` | Potential ambiguity | Its proposed record and verification approach should be clearly distinguished from a requirement to publish records or provide material to readers. |
| Medium | `research-history.html` | Potential ambiguity | “Source material may be incorporated as they are recovered, checked and made available” and the expectation to link original sources could imply eventual public release of recovered material. |
| Medium | `contact.html` | Potential ambiguity | Welcoming questions, corrections and research contributions and inviting attachments does not explicitly promise replies, review or incorporation, but that boundary is unstated. |
| Medium | `research-directory.html` | Potential ambiguity | “Science of Origin shares observations... and research for reference” would be clearer as referring to selected material, not the entire research archive. |
| Low | `index.html` | Editorial choice | “Supporting evidence” appears in the general research-principles statement; this is not a disclosure promise, but can be reconsidered for consistency of editorial language. |
| Low | `research.html` | Editorial choice | The research index describes methods and verification accurately; a short clarification could prevent readers from confusing methodological care with a public-support commitment. |

No public page was found that explicitly promises to persuade readers, repeatedly defend acceptance, tutor readers, or provide ongoing individual research assistance. No general promise to publish all original research materials was identified. The issues are primarily possible interpretations, not direct contradictions.

## 4. Five-pass findings

### Pass 1 — Project identity, principles and research architecture

**Pages reviewed:**

- `index.html`
- `about.html`
- `core-principles.html`
- `foundations.html`
- `research.html`
- `research-direction.html`
- `research-framework.html`
- `research-map.html`
- `research-directory.html`
- `terminology.html`
- `research-and-sharing-philosophy.html`

**Assessment:** Broadly consistent. The project is presented as evolving, open-ended and not confined to a completed framework. Research Direction explicitly describes itself as a working view rather than a fixed sequence or limit on future inquiry. The Research Map describes itself as unfinished. The Research and Sharing Philosophy states directly that sharing discoveries is not a commitment to persuade readers or manage their responses.

**Items to clarify:**

1. `about.html`: The sentence about examining methods and findings through “reasoning, observations, limitations and supporting evidence” is not itself a promise to provide materials. However, “supporting evidence” is an unnecessary invitation to interpret the page as a standing evidence-package commitment. Prefer wording focused on the basis, observations, methods and limitations of a finding, without suggesting an obligation to supply additional materials.
2. `about.html`: “The project aims to make its concepts and reasoning clear enough to examine, question and refine” describes an editorial aim, not a service guarantee. Consider making clear that published explanations are the material selected for sharing and that questions do not create an obligation for individual explanations or follow-up.
3. `index.html`: The same “supporting evidence” wording appears in the homepage's guiding-principles section. This is an editorial consistency issue, not a direct conflict.
4. `research-directory.html`: Clarify that the directory describes material Science of Origin chooses to share. The current sentence does not say that all observations, experiences or records are published, but “shares ... research” could be read expansively.

**No change indicated:** `core-principles.html`, `foundations.html`, `research-direction.html`, `research-framework.html`, `research-map.html`, `terminology.html` and the published `research-and-sharing-philosophy.html` do not create a reader-support or full-disclosure obligation. Their open-ended descriptions are consistent with Principle 6.

**Assessment:** Methodological care, measurement, evidence, verification and reproducibility are not inherently inconsistent with voluntary sharing. A researcher may preserve detailed records and use rigorous methods without making the complete research archive public. The distinction should be stated explicitly where a page could otherwise blur it.

**Main item to clarify — `research-discoveries.html`:** This page sets out a proposed working approach for recording observations, evaluating findings, checking methods and documenting limitations. It also says methods should be described clearly enough, where feasible, for others to repeat them, and includes a research-record template. These are reasonable methodological concepts, but readers could mistake them for publication requirements or a promise to supply records on request. Clarify that the page describes possible internal research practice and claim-status distinctions; it does not require publication of every record, release of supplementary materials, or individual responses to requests. Reproducibility and public disclosure are separate matters.

**Positive alignment — `entropy-of-consciousness.html`:** The page explicitly says that its published research architecture does not disclose detailed detection points, assessment procedures, individual assessment records or the complete underlying research materials. This is a strong concrete example of Principle 2 and should remain intact.

**No change indicated:** The remaining pages in this pass discuss research methods, limitations, measurement or open questions without promising that all underlying materials will be released or that the researcher will personally assist readers. References to reproducibility should not be removed merely because they describe a research quality.

## 5. Recommended wording changes for a later approved editing PR

These are proposals for review, not changes already made.

### A. `about.html` and `index.html`

Review “supporting evidence” in the general description of how methods and findings are examined. A possible direction is:

> The basis of a finding may be examined through the observations, methods, reasoning and limitations relevant to that research.

This describes examination without implying that additional documents must be supplied on request.

### B. `research-discoveries.html`

Add a short scope clarification, for example:

> This page describes research-recording and evaluation practices. It does not require every original record or supplementary material to be published. The researcher decides which discoveries and materials to share. Methodological reproducibility and public disclosure are separate matters.

Review “for others to repeat it” so it remains a description of method clarity where feasible, not an obligation to release the full underlying archive.

### C. `research-history.html`

Clarify that source material may be added when recovered and selected for publication, and that source links are provided when the relevant materials have been chosen for public sharing. Do not imply that every recovered source will be disclosed.

### D. `contact.html`

Possible boundary statement:

> Messages and materials may be considered at the project's discretion. Submission does not guarantee an individual response, review, acceptance or incorporation into the research.

This is an optional clarification; it should not sound hostile or discourage useful submissions.

### E. `research-directory.html`

Consider changing “Science of Origin shares observations, experiences, observed understandings, interpretations and research for reference” to explicitly refer to selected or published material, so the directory does not imply that the entire archive is public.

## 6. Wording that should not be over-corrected

- **Evidence and verification:** These terms can describe research practice without creating a promise to disclose private records. Keep them where they accurately describe the subject.
- **Reproducibility:** A method's reproducibility is distinct from publication of every source record or supplementary file.
- **Independent review:** A reader may independently examine a published discovery; this does not require the original researcher to supervise the process.
- **Contact invitations:** Welcoming messages or contributions does not itself create an obligation to answer. A boundary statement may nevertheless remove ambiguity.
- **Open questions:** Unresolved subjects and evolving records are consistent with the philosophy. Do not rewrite them into premature conclusions for the sake of apparent completeness.
- **Historical provenance:** Preserve original records, earlier wording and later clarifications distinctly. Do not silently rewrite history to make every record sound as though it was written under the current philosophy.

## 7. Overall conclusion

The website does not require a wholesale rewrite. Its existing research content is broadly compatible with the Research and Sharing Philosophy. The most useful improvements are targeted clarifications on a small number of project-level pages—especially About, Research and Discoveries, Research History, Contact and the Research Directory.

The governing distinction for future editing is:

**Preserving, examining and improving research are part of conducting research. Publishing selected discoveries is a separate choice. Neither creates an obligation to persuade readers, disclose the complete research archive, provide supplementary materials on demand, or manage another person's investigation.**

No public HTML page, sitemap entry, navigation link or live website content was changed as part of this audit. Any public wording changes should be handled in a separate reviewable pull request after approval.
