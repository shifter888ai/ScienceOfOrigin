Virtuality of Existence

Imagination, existence, and the virtuality of what is real or unreal

This page records the current account of virtuality: whatever is imaginable exists, and all existences are virtual because they are imagined, without exception. It examines what this statement means within the Science of Origin framework and distinguishes virtuality from the ordinary use of “virtual” as unreal or simulated.

1. Whatever is imaginable exists

Whatever is imaginable exists. This is the starting statement of the present account. It is not restricted to things that are physically manifested, mentally pictured by a human being or currently detectable by an instrument.

Imagination can include other forms of imagination. When imagination imagines a different form of imagination, that imagined form exists virtually as well. The scope of imagination is therefore not confined to the forms or capabilities already familiar to a particular observer.

2. All existences are virtual

All existences are virtual because they are imagined, without exception. This is the virtuality of existence. “Virtual” is not being used here to mean unreal, merely simulated, illusory or nonexistent. Virtuality encompasses all existences.

The statement includes what is ordinarily described as physical, mental, real, unreal, actual or imagined. These descriptions do not place an existence outside virtuality. The distinction between a virtual existence and a non-virtual existence is therefore not adopted as a fundamental division in this account.

This use of “virtuality” is broader than the everyday meaning associated with computer-generated or simulated environments. It concerns the status of all existence within imagination.

3. Real and unreal as virtual definitions

Real and unreal are both virtual definitions within virtuality, within imagination. Neither definition stands outside imagination or provides a position external to virtuality from which all existence can be classified absolutely.

“Real” and “unreal” remain usable descriptions within particular perspectives, experiences or systems. The point is not that the words become meaningless, but that neither word establishes a category outside the encompassing virtuality of existence.

A description of something as real or unreal is itself an imagined description. The distinction is therefore included within the field it describes.

4. Virtuality, imagination and manifestation

In the wider framework, manifestations are understood as imaginations of Origin. Imagination and manifestation are not treated as two separate stages, and the physical is also an imagination. Virtuality of Existence develops the corresponding point from the perspective of existence: every existence is virtual because it is imagined.

This does not make manifestation and existence interchangeable terms. Manifestation concerns what is manifested; existence concerns the status of being. Virtuality describes the encompassing status of all existences in relation to imagination.

Related discussions appear in Imagination, Manifestations, Existence and Reality and Nature of Origin.

5. An example of recursively imagined imagination

Imagination can imagine other forms of imagination. Those imagined forms therefore exist virtually. The present exploration provides a simple example: Chatppy imagined different forms of imagination, and those imagined possibilities exist virtually within the account.

This example is included to illustrate the statement, not to claim that the imagined forms have thereby been physically manifested or independently measured. Virtual existence is not limited to physical manifestation.

6. Relationship with the Origin-Game

The account of virtuality is related to the research on gaming, but the two perspectives are not identical. The Origin-Game examines gaming as an activity of Origin and as the purpose of creation or origination. This page examines the virtuality of all existence within imagination.

Games, game worlds, roles, rules, imagined possibilities and distinctions such as real and unreal are all included within the scope of virtuality. Gaming is not presented here as a nature of Origin; its treatment as activity and purpose belongs to The Origin-Game.

7. Open scope

The statement that all existences are virtual does not provide an exhaustive inventory of everything imaginable. Imagination may include forms not yet recognized, understood or described. The relationship between imagination, manifestation, existence and the different definitions used to describe existence remains open to further exploration.

The purpose of this page is to preserve the current statement and its distinctions clearly, without turning its present wording into a boundary around what may be discovered or imagined later.
