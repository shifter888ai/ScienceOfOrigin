Imagination

Investigating imagination as a foundational concept through which possibilities and manifestations are associated with Origin, beyond the limited meaning of human fantasy or mental imagery.

Imagination in a foundational sense

In Science of Origin, imagination is used in a broad foundational sense. It is not restricted to the human ability to picture something mentally, invent a story or form a fantasy. It concerns the proposed relationship between Origin, the arising of possibilities and the coming about of manifestations.

This use of the term extends beyond familiar human experience. Human imagination may provide one point of reference, but it should not automatically be treated as a complete description or limit of imagination at the foundational level.

Imagination and Origin

Within the developing framework, manifestations are understood as imaginations of Origin. Imagination is therefore considered in relation to the fundamental source, rather than as an independent force or a separate ultimate origin.

The imaginer and the imagined are not proposed as two independently existing ultimate sources. They are expressions associated with the same underlying Origin. The apparent distinction between what imagines and what is imagined does not, by itself, establish an absolute separation at the foundational level.

For the broader account of the source, see Origin.

Imagination and manifestation

In this framework, imagination is related to the arising of possibilities and manifestations. What is imagined is not necessarily limited to an image or idea represented within an already existing mind. The concept also concerns manifestations themselves and the possibilities associated with them.

Manifestation identifies what comes about; origination concerns the capability and process through which it comes about. Imagination is examined in relation to both: it concerns the proposed imaginative nature of manifestations and the modes through which possibilities may arise or be realized.

These terms are related but should not be treated as interchangeable. See Origination and Manifestations.

Spontaneous and intentional imagination

The framework distinguishes two important modes of imagination:

  • Spontaneous imagination — the spontaneous arising of manifestations from Origin, rather than a deliberate act performed within an already established manifestation.
  • Intentional imagination — imagination directed by intention and operating within an existing manifestation, where it may bring about further manifestations within that manifestation's conditions.

These modes describe different contexts and should not be confused. In particular, spontaneous does not mean that origination is without intention at the level of Origin. Within the framework, Consciousness / Freewill is associated with intention, while origination concerns the capability and process through which intentions are realized.

The dedicated treatment of these modes and their possible mechanisms belongs to Division V — Creation.

Imagination and Consciousness / Freewill

Science of Origin treats Consciousness and Freewill as the same aspect of Origin, associated with intention. Imagination is considered in relation to this aspect and to the origination through which intentions may be realized.

This relationship does not require treating imagination, Consciousness / Freewill and origination as separate ultimate sources. They describe related aspects and concepts within the developing framework: Consciousness / Freewill as an aspect of Origin, imagination in relation to possibilities and manifestations, and origination as the capability and process through which manifestations come about.

See Consciousness & Freewill for the foundational account of that relationship.

Infinity of imagination

The framework explores imagination in relation to the Infinity associated with Origin. If imagination is not confined to the limits of human thought, familiar physical forms or the possibilities already encountered within one manifestation, its scope may extend beyond any particular set of known possibilities.

The idea of an Infinity of imagination is a foundational proposition for exploration, not a claim that every possible manifestation or its properties have been identified. What Infinity means in this context, and how the proposition may be examined, remain open questions.

See Infinity of Imagination for the dedicated investigation of this proposition.

Research questions

  • What is the precise meaning and scope of imagination in the foundational framework?
  • How does this meaning differ from human fantasy, visualization, mental imagery and ordinary creative thought?
  • What is the relationship between Origin, imagination, the imaginer and the imagined?
  • In what sense are manifestations understood as imaginations of Origin?
  • How should imagination be distinguished from origination while describing their relationship?
  • How are spontaneous and intentional imagination distinguished, and how does intention relate to each?
  • How is imagination related to Consciousness / Freewill and the realization of intentions?
  • What does the proposed Infinity of imagination mean, and what aspects of it can be investigated?
  • Which parts of this framework can be explored through experience, observation or reproducible methods, and which remain conceptual?
