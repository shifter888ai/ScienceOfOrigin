Research Directory

A structured reference to the current research divisions, subjects and related materials of Science of Origin. It is provided for independent exploration and reference, not as a course, curriculum or prescribed path of learning.

Purpose and use

This directory makes the current organization of the research easier to locate and examine. It identifies the primary division in which a subject is presently placed and links to available reference pages.

The organization is provisional and expandable. A listing does not imply that a subject is fully investigated, experimentally established or independently reproduced. The status and basis of each understanding should be examined in its own context.

Science of Origin shares observations, experiences, observed understandings, interpretations and research for reference. Readers remain free to examine the material and reach their own conclusions.

Research organization

  • Major Divisions — broad domains that provide an overall orientation to the research.
  • Fundamental Topics — central subjects and foundational concepts within or across divisions.
  • Extended Topics — more specific subjects, relationships, applications, observations, experiments and research questions.
  • Cross-connections — relationships between subjects that extend beyond their primary division.

These are organizational distinctions, not a hierarchy of importance or evidential status. A topic may connect with several divisions. An Extended Topic may later become a Fundamental Topic; new findings or questions may lead to new topics or divisions.

The current division pages provide the initial subject inventory. The classification of every listed subject into Fundamental or Extended Topics is not yet complete; no classification is implied where it has not been made explicit.

Seven Major Divisions

I. Foundations

Origin, Consciousness / Freewill, imagination, origination and manifestation.

 II. Nature of Existence

Things, life, energy, matter, information, space and time.

 III. Structure

Systems, rules, organization, relationships, boundaries, multiplicity and independence.

 IV. Origin Models

The Oneness Origin and the constructed imitation described as Fake Origin.

 V. Creation

Imagination, conditional creation, programming and further origination.

 VI. Dynamics

Interactions, causality, states, processes, transformation, emergence and possibility.

 VII. Research

Perception, observation, measurement, uncertainty, methods and findings.
