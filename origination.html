Origination

Origination concerns the capability and process through which manifestations come about and may give rise to further manifestations.

What is origination?

In Science of Origin, origination refers to the coming-about of a manifestation: the process through which something that was not present in that form comes into manifestation. It also concerns the capability through which further manifestations may arise.

Origination is not limited to a single beginning or a one-time event. The developing framework treats it as ongoing and open-ended: manifestations may arise, and manifestations may in turn become the immediate source of further origination.

Origin and origination

Origin and origination are closely related, but they are not interchangeable:

  • Origin concerns the fundamental source — the Oneness Origin.
  • Origination concerns the capability and process through which manifestations arise.

Within the framework, Origin remains the ultimate source of every manifestation. However, a manifestation may be the immediate source through which another manifestation comes about. This distinction makes it possible to examine direct and indirect pathways without treating an intermediate manifestation as a separate ultimate Origin.

For the foundational account of the source, see Origin. For the forms and relationships of what comes into manifestation, see Manifestations.

Spontaneous and intentional origination

The framework distinguishes spontaneous and intentional imagination as two important modes related to origination. Here, imagination is used in its broad foundational sense, not merely to mean human fantasy or mental imagery.

  • Spontaneous imagination: the spontaneous arising of manifestations. It is not described as a deliberate act carried out within an already established manifestation.
  • Intentional imagination: imagination directed by intention and operating within a manifestation. It may bring about further manifestations within the conditions of that manifestation.

These modes should not be collapsed into one another. Nor should the distinction be taken to mean that origination is confined to a single initial event followed by a world in which nothing further can originate. The relationship between spontaneous and intentional origination, and the role of intention in each, remain important subjects for clarification.

The mechanisms and possible forms of intentional and conditional creation are explored further in the Creation division.

Continuity and pathways

Origination may be considered in terms of pathways. A manifestation may arise directly from Origin or indirectly through another manifestation. An existing manifestation may also contribute to the origination of further manifestations.

These pathways may branch, converge and interact. More than one manifestation may contribute to a new manifestation, and an originating manifestation may give rise to further pathways. The resulting relationships need not form one simple, linear sequence.

Continuous origination therefore concerns more than the persistence or transformation of existing things. It includes the possibility that further manifestations come about through new acts or processes of origination.

Origination capabilities within manifestations

Within the developing framework, origination is not considered a capability that must remain identical at every level. A manifestation or system may develop its own capability to originate further manifestations. That capability may differ from the capability of the manifestation or system from which it arose.

Interactions among systems may contribute to new systems, and an emergent system may have principles, rules or origination capabilities of its own. These possibilities raise questions about how new forms of origination arise and how they may interact. See Principles and Rules and Systems.

Origination and change

Not every change should automatically be classified as origination. A manifestation may change its properties while remaining the same manifestation; it may also undergo transformation. Origination concerns the coming-about of a manifestation, and distinguishing it from change or transformation is necessary for clear investigation.

These distinctions connect origination with the study of Transformation and Emergence.

Research questions

  • What criteria distinguish origination from a change in the properties or state of an existing manifestation?
  • How should spontaneous and intentional imagination be defined and distinguished?
  • How are intention and Freewill related to different forms of origination?
  • How do direct and indirect origination pathways relate to the ultimate Origin?
  • Can multiple contributing manifestations participate in one process of origination, and how can those contributions be described?
  • Under what conditions can a manifestation or system develop its own origination capabilities?
  • How can continuity, branching and convergence in origination pathways be investigated?
  • Which aspects of origination can be observed, measured or tested, and which remain conceptual?
