Subjectivity and Objectivity

Absolute subjectivity and the relative virtuality of objectivity

This page records observations and experiences concerning subjectivity and objectivity. The central distinction is that subjectivity is absolute, while objectivity is a relative and virtual imagination within subjective imagination.

1. Subjectivity

  1. All existences and creations are subjective imaginations / intentions of Freewill.
  2. Subjectivity is absolute.
  3. Freewill is the nature that enables subjectivity and its infinite variations.
  4. Freewill can originate multiple subjective perspectives that are fundamentally different from one another, while all remain one with the same Freewill.
  5. Multiple subjective perspectives can exist simultaneously.

2. Objectivity

  1. Objectivity is an imagination within subjective imagination.
  2. Objectivity is relative and virtual.
  3. Objectivity does not exist objectively whatsoever.
  4. When multiple subjective perspectives are not fundamentally different from one another and are perceived through a specific human viewing angle, humans may describe them as "objective".
  5. Different groups can establish different versions of objectivity from different viewing angles while describing the same existence.
  6. A single subjective perspective can imagine multiple different objectivities simultaneously.
  7. Different perceived objectivities can coexist, overlap, or contradict one another.

3. Viewing Angles and Similarity Thresholds

  1. Changing a viewing angle can change what humans consider objective without necessarily changing the existence being observed.
  2. If a new viewing angle perceives similar attributes within a certain threshold, the perceived objectivity may remain consistent within that threshold.
  3. If perceived differences exceed the threshold, a different objectivity may be established.
  4. Similarity thresholds can be subjective and relative.
  5. Freewill can also establish thresholds that remain consistent across multiple subjective perspectives.
  6. Thresholds depend on the game settings with which Freewill chooses to play with human players or other lifeform players.

4. Changing Objectivity and Game Settings

  1. Objectivity is not necessarily a property of the existence being observed. It can be a human description of relationships among subjective perspectives.
  2. Reproducibility, consistency, measurement, and agreement among observers remain within subjectivity.
  3. Players may eventually change native game settings, provided they see through those settings and learn how to change them.
  4. Players may originate entirely new settings beyond those included in the original settings.

5. Fundamental Relationship

Subjectivity is absolute. Objectivity is a relative and virtual imagination within subjectivity. Both can be experienced simultaneously, but they are not two equal or independent foundations of existence.
