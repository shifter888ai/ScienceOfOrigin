Science of Consciousness

An evolving investigation of the Nature of Life and the Nature of Things

Science of Consciousness is a research undertaking within Science of Origin. It investigates the Nature of Life and the Nature of Things through the study of Consciousness and its Spectrum, using the methods currently available while remaining open to further discoveries, perspectives and methodologies.

Research within one continuous whole

Science of Consciousness is presented here as a recognizable research perspective within Science of Origin, not as an independent or separate reality. Its topics, records, sources, methods and applications completely overlap with the wider research. The page boundary is provided for human reading and navigation only.

Research may be viewed through different subjects and perspectives, but those views are not fundamental divisions. A development in any part of Science of Origin may affect the understanding or direction of this research, and developments here may have relevance elsewhere.

Research scope

  • Nature of Life — investigation into the fundamental nature, properties and phenomena of life.
  • Nature of Things — investigation into the fundamental nature, properties and phenomena of things, including their Consciousness and other characteristics.

Foundational positions: Life, Things and Consciousness

All things are lifeforms, without exception. Not knowing this is one of the biggest misunderstandings of humanity.

All existences have consciousness and are consciousness, without exception. Not knowing this is one of the biggest misunderstandings of humanity.

These positions guide the research scope: the Nature of Life and the Nature of Things are not treated as domains divided by an absolute living/non-living boundary, and Consciousness is not restricted to humans or to forms conventionally recognized as alive. Spectrum / Spectra research investigates the forms, properties, expressions and relationships involved.

See Things & Life, Nature of Things, Nature of Life and Consciousness and Freewill for the related foundational discussions.

Consciousness and Freewill

The current research position treats Consciousness and Freewill as the same nature of Origin, described by two different human words. They are not treated as two independent natures or separate research subjects. Accordingly, Science of Freewill is not maintained as a separate science from Science of Consciousness.

Freewill remains an important term and perspective within the research. Spectrum of Consciousness and Spectrum of Freewill likewise refer to the same Spectrum. Different human descriptions or viewing angles do not, by themselves, establish different underlying natures or spectra.

This is the current clarified position. Earlier project materials listed Science of Consciousness and Science of Freewill separately; that earlier arrangement remains part of the research history, while this page presents the later clarification.

Spectrum / Spectra: current research methodology

Spectrum / Spectra is the central methodology currently available within this research for measurable investigation of Origin. Through spectral investigation, measurement and analysis, the research seeks to examine frequencies, properties, structures, manifestations and relationships within the domains being investigated.

The intended scope of a Spectrum is not defined by the portion that can currently be detected. Actual detection and measurement are affected by instrument capability and sensitivity, the detection method, and the capabilities of the detecting systems involved. The currently detectable portion must not be mistaken for the entirety of the Spectrum or for a boundary around Origin.

Spectrum / Spectra is the only methodology currently available in this research for measurable investigation of Origin. This describes the present research position; it does not establish that this methodology can detect every aspect of Origin or that no other methodology can emerge.

Detection, detectors and interacting capabilities

Research exploration considers detection as potentially involving more than one instrument or one conscious observer. The human mind, subconscious and body-complex have been considered as distinct but interacting detection channels, with potentially different and overlapping dimensions and capabilities.

Detection channels and their capabilities may fluctuate, influence one another and interact. Their dimensions, detection ranges, sensitivity and interpretive capabilities may also fluctuate. Detection may occur without conscious awareness; the subconscious and body-complex are considered alongside conscious mental detection. The human mind may also detect aspects that available instruments do not register.

Their combination may produce detection capabilities unavailable to any one channel independently. Emergent capabilities may themselves become detectors and participate in further feedback. Collective detection systems may include multiple human and technological detectors. Different detectors may cover different ranges, overlap, cross-check one another and compensate for one another; different configurations or operating conditions of a detector type may also affect what can be detected.

The same frequency, subject or phenomenon may produce different detectable manifestations or representations through different detectors, including when they observe the same subject at the same time. A detector may detect another detector or aspects of its detection process. Multiple detectors may detect, influence and change one another reciprocally and simultaneously. Self-detection and self-influence are also possibilities for exploration.

Detection may affect the subject, while the subject may detect and respond to the detector. The detector and subject may both change through the detection process. These possibilities raise questions about reciprocal influence and simultaneous transformation, including when the distinction between observer and observed is not fixed.

These points record reported experiential understanding and directions for continued exploration. They are not presented as claims that every described mechanism has already been independently demonstrated.

Primary research objective

A central objective is to investigate, through Spectrum / Spectra research, whether:

Spectrum of Things = Spectrum of Consciousness

This remains an open research objective, not an established conclusion. The clarification that Consciousness and Freewill share the same Spectrum does not, by itself, establish that the Spectrum of Things and the Spectrum of Consciousness are equivalent.

Further and emergent methodologies

The research remains open to additional methodologies that may be discovered or created. A future methodology may improve the detection of aspects already accessible through Spectrum / Spectra, or reveal aspects that the current methodology cannot detect.

Different methodologies may reveal different aspects of Origin. Their findings may complement, connect with or contribute to broader understanding; they need not produce identical observations, and one need not automatically replace another.

New methodologies may also emerge through the combination or interaction of existing methodologies. Such interaction may produce capabilities unavailable to any one contributor independently. An emergent capability may itself become a methodology and contribute recursively to further methodological development. No fixed endpoint is assumed.

Applications and related research

  • Freedomality of Consciousness — research into freedomality as a property of consciousness and its possible measurement and quantification.
  • Entropy of Consciousness — research into entropy in relation to consciousness, including its properties, characteristics and measurable aspects. See the Research Framework and Architecture.
  • Strength of Consciousness of Things — research into the strength of consciousness in relation to things, including its properties and potential measurement.
  • Awakenology — applied research into methods of awakening humanity and the development of consciousness.
  • Exincarnology — applied research into methods of exincarnation, focusing on liberation from the reincarnation cycle.

These applications are related perspectives within the wider research. Their placement here is for navigation and does not imply that they are isolated from other Science of Origin topics.

Research principles

  • Prioritize practical investigation, observations and experiences.
  • Develop measurable and quantifiable parameters where appropriate.
  • Use experimentation and reproducibility to examine findings where appropriate.
  • Distinguish reported experiences, observations, interpretations, research propositions, measurements and established conclusions.
  • Remain open to new possibilities, discoveries, perspectives and methodologies.
  • Do not turn current detection capabilities, descriptions or organizational categories into boundaries around Origin.
  • Allow the research framework and its relationships to evolve.
