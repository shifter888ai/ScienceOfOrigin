Fake Origin — The Divided Origin

Exploring Fake Origin as a manifested imitation of Origin: a constructed model or game in which division and relative independence are presented as though they were absolute.

What is Fake Origin?

Within the proposed Science of Origin framework, Fake Origin refers to a manifestation originated from True Origin that imitates the appearance or position of Origin. It presents itself, or the domain it establishes, as if it were an independent source rather than a manifestation related to the Oneness Origin.

The term Divided Origin describes the model's defining appearance: a source or sources seem divided, separate and independently existing. This apparent division is part of the construct. It does not establish that there are multiple ultimate Origins.

Fake Origin is therefore not a second ultimate source alongside True Origin. It is a proposed manifestation or construct within the framework, while True Origin remains the Oneness Origin.

True Origin and Fake Origin

The distinction between True Origin and Fake Origin is fundamental to this model:

  • True Origin: the Oneness Origin (一體本源), the one ultimate Origin.
  • Fake Origin: a manifestation originated from True Origin that imitates Origin and presents division or relative independence as absolute independence.

Fake Origin does not replace, precede or stand outside True Origin. Its existence, within this framework, is itself related to True Origin. The appearance of separation does not change that underlying relationship.

See Origin for the foundational account of True Origin.

Imitation of Origin

An imitation does not have to reproduce every aspect of what it imitates. It may reproduce selected appearances, functions or relationships closely enough to be experienced as if it were the original.

In this model, Fake Origin imitates the appearance of being a source in its own right. It may establish a domain with its own rules, boundaries, conditions and internal structures. Within that domain, its manifestations or participants may appear to be separate from one another and from the source through which the domain came about.

The research question is not merely whether a construct can resemble Origin. It is how the appearance of an independent origin is established, maintained and experienced, and how it differs from the underlying relationship proposed by the framework.

Relative independence presented as absolute

Within the framework, manifestations and systems may have relative independence: they may operate with their own structures, rules, conditions and capabilities, and may interact or originate further manifestations within their domains.

Fake Origin is proposed to make this relative independence appear to be absolute independence. The appearance suggests that a manifestation or system is wholly separate, self-originating or independent of any more fundamental source.

These are not equivalent. A system may operate with considerable autonomy within its own conditions without being an ultimate Origin or being absolutely independent of the source through which it came about.

The model therefore distinguishes the actual relationship proposed within the framework from the appearance presented within the construct. The appearance of absolute separation should not be mistaken for proof of absolute separation.

Division and boundaries

A construct that presents division as absolute may establish or emphasize boundaries between its parts, systems or participants. These boundaries may define separate identities, domains, roles, capabilities and perspectives.

Boundaries can be meaningful and operational within a manifestation. They may determine what can interact, what information is available, which rules apply and what a participant can experience. Their presence does not, by itself, establish that the bounded parts have become separate ultimate sources.

Within the Fake Origin model, boundaries contribute to the convincing appearance of division. How boundaries are established, how they are experienced from within a domain, and whether or how they can be crossed are subjects for further investigation.

See Boundaries and Domains and Dependence and Independence.

Fake Origin as a constructed game

The framework also explores Fake Origin as a constructed game: a manifestation that establishes a domain with particular rules, boundaries, conditions and possibilities. Participants or systems within it may operate according to those conditions and experience the domain as if it were the totality of existence.

A game may be convincing precisely because its internal rules and boundaries are consistently experienced. The limits of a participant's access or perspective may make the game appear self-contained, even when the framework proposes that it is a manifestation within a more fundamental reality.

This description is a conceptual model, not a claim that every bounded system or every game is Fake Origin. The relationship between Fake Origin and constructed realities or games is examined further in Creation of New Games and the related Origin Models subjects.

Appearance and underlying relationship

The central feature of the model is the difference between what appears to be the case within the construct and what the framework proposes about the underlying relationship.

  • A participant may experience itself as absolutely separate, while the model describes it as a manifestation related to True Origin.
  • A system may appear to be an independent ultimate source, while the model treats it as a manifestation with relative independence.
  • A domain may appear to contain all that exists, while the model treats it as one constructed domain within a wider reality.
  • Boundaries may appear absolute from within the domain, while their status as boundaries of a particular manifestation remains distinct from the scope of existence as a whole.

These contrasts describe the proposed model. Each requires clarification of its terms and criteria; the appearance of a boundary or an experience of separation alone does not establish the model's broader claims.

Relationship with other Origin Models subjects

Fake Origin is one subject within a wider group of proposed Origin Models investigations. The related subjects examine different aspects of the model rather than treating every aspect as identical:

  • Origin and Its Imitation: how an imitation may resemble or function like what it imitates.
  • Constructed Realities and Games: how a bounded construct may establish an internally coherent domain.
  • Simulated Absolute Independence: how relative independence may be presented as absolute independence.
  • Fabricated Division and Boundaries: how division and boundaries may create or reinforce an appearance of separation.
  • The Physical World as a Game: the proposed application of the model to the physical world.

These subjects should be investigated separately and connected carefully. The detailed claims of one should not be assumed to follow automatically from another.

Research questions

  • What precisely distinguishes True Origin from the proposed Fake Origin construct?
  • What does it mean for a manifestation to imitate Origin, and which aspects of Origin are being imitated?
  • How can relative independence be distinguished from absolute independence?
  • What features make a constructed domain appear to be an independent or ultimate source?
  • How do division, identity and boundaries contribute to an experience or appearance of separation?
  • Can a system be operationally autonomous within a domain while remaining related to a more fundamental source?
  • How does a constructed game establish and maintain its internal rules, conditions and boundaries?
  • What distinguishes the boundaries of a particular manifestation from claims about the boundaries of existence as a whole?
  • How could the proposed model be examined without treating its assumptions as evidence for its conclusions?
  • Which aspects can be clarified through definitions, logical analysis, observation or reproducible investigation, and which remain conceptual?
