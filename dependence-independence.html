Dependence & Independence

An exploration of how manifestations and systems rely on, support, influence or operate apart from one another, and how independence and autonomy may develop.

Dependence and independence

Dependence concerns a condition in which a manifestation, system, process or capability relies on something else for its existence, operation, continuation or development. Independence concerns the extent to which it can exist or operate without such reliance. These questions connect closely with the identification and organization of Systems and their Structure & Function.

These terms should not automatically be treated as absolute opposites. A system may depend on some conditions or resources while remaining independent in other respects. The subject and the particular form of dependence must therefore be identified.

Different forms of dependence

Dependence may involve existence, formation, resources, energy, information, structure, function, operation, maintenance or origination. A system may rely on another system to begin operating but later continue without direct support. It may also operate independently in one function while remaining dependent in another. Relevant Conditions & Constraints may enable, limit or sustain these relationships.

Research should distinguish the source of a dependency, what is being supplied or enabled, and what would happen if the relevant support or relationship changed or ceased.

Interdependence

Two or more manifestations or systems may depend on one another. Such interdependence may be reciprocal, unequal or conditional. Each system may provide something that supports the other, while the nature and degree of reliance may differ between them. These arrangements can be examined through the study of Relationships & Networks.

Interdependence does not necessarily mean that the systems become one system. They may remain distinguishable while participating in relationships that affect their operation, continuity or development.

Degrees and dimensions of independence

Independence may be a matter of degree and may vary across dimensions. A system might be materially dependent but operationally autonomous, or capable of maintaining its organization while relying on external conditions for resources.

Descriptions such as independent, dependent and autonomous should therefore be used with stated criteria. A claim of independence in one dimension does not establish independence in every dimension or from every possible influence. The scope of such a claim also depends on the boundaries and domains used to identify the subject and its surroundings.

Autonomy and self-maintenance

Autonomy concerns the capacity of a system to regulate or direct aspects of its own operation. Self-maintenance concerns the processes through which it preserves or restores conditions needed for its continuation. These capabilities may contribute to independence, but they do not necessarily eliminate all external dependencies. They may also change through Transformation & Emergence.

Research can examine what a system maintains for itself, which processes or resources it requires from elsewhere, and how its autonomy changes when conditions or relationships change.

Dependence, origination and development

A manifestation may originate through another manifestation and later develop capabilities that are not simply supplied by its source. Its continuing existence or operation may retain some dependencies while other capabilities become increasingly self-directed.

Within the developing Science of Origin framework, an emergent system may develop its own capability for origination. Whether and how such a capability becomes independent of the originating system is an open research question. The origin of a system and its subsequent degree of independence should be examined as distinct matters. This connects with the proposed study of Independent Origination Capabilities.

Independence within larger systems

A component may operate with a degree of autonomy while remaining part of a larger system. It may also depend on the larger system for conditions, resources or relationships that it cannot provide itself. Independence at one level can therefore coexist with dependence at another level. This is one reason to examine Scale & Levels when describing independence.

Likewise, a system may be independent of one particular system but dependent on a wider environment or set of conditions. The scope of any claim about independence should make its boundaries and reference points clear.

Changing dependencies

Dependencies may arise, weaken, strengthen, shift or disappear as a manifestation or system changes. New interactions may create dependencies, while new capabilities or arrangements may reduce existing ones. A system may also become dependent on something that was previously irrelevant to its operation. The role of Interactions should be distinguished from the effects attributed to them.

Such changes may affect continuity, identity, stability and the possibility of further origination. These relationships should be investigated rather than assumed to follow one universal pattern.

Testing dependence and independence

Dependence can be investigated by identifying a proposed dependency and examining what changes when the relevant condition, resource, component or interaction is altered or removed. Such investigations require suitable controls and attention to indirect dependencies and alternative explanations. Claims about causal relationships should be distinguished from observed association, as discussed in Causality & Influence.

Observing that two systems operate together does not by itself establish that one depends on the other. Similarly, failure to observe an effect under one set of conditions does not prove complete independence. Conclusions should be limited to the dimensions, conditions and observation range actually examined, with suitable Measurement & Quantification.

Research directions

  • Clarifying the distinctions among dependence, independence, interdependence, autonomy and self-maintenance.
  • Identifying different dimensions of dependence, including existence, resources, operation, continuity and origination.
  • Examining how dependence may be reciprocal, unequal, conditional or indirect.
  • Investigating degrees of autonomy and the conditions under which systems maintain their own operation.
  • Studying how a manifestation may develop capabilities that reduce or change its dependence on its originating system.
  • Examining how independence at one level may coexist with dependence at another level.
  • Investigating how dependencies change through interaction, transformation and emergence.
  • Developing methods for testing proposed dependencies through controlled changes and observations.
  • Identifying the limits of evidence used to describe a system as independent or autonomous.
Research status: This page establishes working distinctions and research directions. It does not assume that any manifestation or system is completely independent, that autonomy eliminates dependence, or that a system necessarily becomes independent of its origin. Definitions, criteria, methods and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
