Principles and Rules

An exploration of how principles and rules may operate within manifestations, how different systems may coexist and interact, and how new systems may emerge.

Principles and rules within manifestations

In the developing Science of Origin framework, principles and rules are subjects for investigating how manifestations and the systems within them operate. Different manifestations may have different principles and rules. These may not be universal across all manifestations.

The terms principles and rules are used here as related research concepts. Their precise meanings, distinctions and relationships have not yet been fully established and remain open to clarification.

Multiple systems within one manifestation

A single manifestation may contain multiple systems. Those systems may operate according to different principles or rules, including principles or rules that appear incompatible with one another.

Therefore, the principles and rules observed in one system should not automatically be assumed to govern every other system within the same manifestation.

Different and incompatible systems

Systems with different or incompatible principles and rules may nevertheless coexist within a manifestation. Separate systems may also interact even when their operating principles or rules are not the same.

Such interactions raise questions about how systems influence one another, what conditions allow interaction, and whether incompatibility is absolute or applies only to particular processes or contexts.

Interaction and emergence

Interactions between systems may contribute to the emergence or origination of new systems. A resulting system may have principles and rules of its own, which may differ from those of the systems involved in its emergence.

New systems may therefore introduce arrangements and possibilities that were not present in any one contributing system considered separately. This is a research possibility within the developing framework, not an established empirical conclusion.

Independent origination capabilities

An emergent system may develop its own capability for origination. Its origination capability may differ from that of the system or systems from which it emerged.

Further systems may arise through these new capabilities. Their forms of origination may be fundamentally different from those of their originating systems, and they may interact with other systems to produce still-unfamiliar forms.

This possibility opens a research direction beyond tracing a single chain of origination: it asks how multiple systems, distinct rules and evolving origination capabilities may combine to produce new forms of manifestation.

Origin and the scope of possibility

The current conceptual position is that anything is allowed by the Ultimate Source, while expression and experience occur within manifestation(s). How this relationship should be understood, and what it implies for the principles and rules of particular systems, remain open questions.

Different principles or rules within manifestations need not be treated as limits on Origin itself. The distinction between the scope of Origin and the conditions operating within particular manifestations is an important subject for further examination.

Research directions

  • Clarifying what is meant by principles and rules, and whether they represent distinct kinds of conditions.
  • Investigating how principles and rules may differ between manifestations.
  • Examining how multiple systems with different or incompatible rules can coexist within one manifestation.
  • Exploring the conditions under which distinct systems can interact.
  • Studying how interactions may contribute to the emergence of new systems.
  • Investigating whether emergent systems can develop independent origination capabilities.
  • Examining how new forms of origination may differ from, and interact with, those of their originating systems.
  • Distinguishing the scope of Origin from the principles and rules operating within manifestations.
Research status: This page records developing conceptual propositions and research questions. The proposed relationships are open to clarification, examination and revision; they are not presented as independently established scientific findings.
