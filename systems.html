A developing research dimension

Systems

An exploration of systems within manifestations: what constitutes a system, how systems are structured and related, and how they interact, transform and give rise to new systems.

What is a system?

In the developing Science of Origin framework, a system is considered as an organized manifestation or part of a manifestation whose elements, relationships or processes form an identifiable arrangement. This is a working description, not a final definition.

A system may itself be a manifestation, or it may exist as a system within a larger manifestation. The distinction between a manifestation and a system within it requires further clarification.

System boundaries

A system may be distinguished by its particular organization, relationships, processes or operating principles. Its boundary may be clear, shared, overlapping or difficult to identify.

Boundaries are therefore a research question rather than an assumption that every system is fully separate from its surroundings. A system may exchange influence with, depend on or interact with other systems across an identifiable or less-defined boundary.

Components and internal structure

A system may contain components, relationships and processes that together form its internal structure. Some components may themselves be systems, creating nested or layered arrangements.

The organization of a system may be as important to its character as the individual components it contains. How components relate, influence one another and contribute to the whole is a central subject for investigation.

Multiple, nested and interconnected systems

A manifestation may contain multiple systems. Systems may be independent in some respects while remaining connected or interdependent in others. One system may also exist within another, and systems may overlap or share components and relationships.

These arrangements need not form a single simple hierarchy. A system may belong to several relationships or networks at once, and the structure of those relationships may change over time.

System interactions

Systems may interact and influence one another even when they operate according to different or apparently incompatible principles and rules. The conditions that permit such interactions, and the effects they produce, remain important research questions.

Interactions may preserve existing systems, change their relationships, transform their structures or contribute to the origination of new systems.

Identity, continuity and transformation

A system may change its components, relationships, processes or properties while continuing to be regarded as the same system. At some point, however, a transformation may be so fundamental that the resulting arrangement is better considered a new system.

How to distinguish continuity from the origination of a different system is not yet settled. It may require examining which characteristics remain, which relationships persist and whether the system's organization or origination capability has fundamentally changed.

Emergence and new systems

Interactions among systems may contribute to the emergence or origination of new systems. A new system may have an organization, principles, rules or capabilities that differ from those of the systems involved in its emergence.

An emergent system may also develop its own capability for origination. Its subsequent development may therefore lead to forms of systems and origination that are not simply repetitions of those of its originating systems.

These are possibilities in the developing framework, not established empirical conclusions. Their scope and implications remain open to investigation.

Research directions

  • Developing a clearer working definition of a system and its relationship to a manifestation.
  • Identifying how system boundaries can be recognized and described.
  • Investigating components, internal relationships, processes and nested structures.
  • Examining independent, overlapping, interconnected and interdependent systems.
  • Exploring interactions between systems with different or incompatible principles and rules.
  • Distinguishing system continuity, transformation and the origination of a new system.
  • Investigating how interactions contribute to emergence and new origination capabilities.
  • Identifying which aspects of systems can be observed, described, measured or experimentally investigated.
Research status: This page records working descriptions, conceptual possibilities and research questions. The propositions remain open to clarification, examination and revision; they are not presented as independently established scientific findings.
