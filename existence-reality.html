Existence & Reality

An open exploration of what it means to exist, how existence relates to manifestation, and whether reality may take different forms across manifestations and systems.

The nature of existence

Existence is a central subject of Science of Origin. What does it mean for something to exist? Is existence a single condition shared by everything, or can there be fundamentally different forms or modes of existence?

These questions should not be settled by definition alone. The meaning of existence, the criteria by which it may be recognized, and the limits of what can be investigated remain open subjects.

Existence and manifestation

Manifestations are the Things and Life Forms that come into manifestation through origination in the developing framework. The relationship between existence and manifestation requires further clarification.

Does everything that exists necessarily manifest in some way? Could there be forms or aspects of existence that are not manifested, or that cannot be recognized through currently available means? These remain open questions rather than established conclusions.

Forms of existence

It is possible to ask whether existence may take different forms, whether those forms share common properties, and whether some forms may be fundamentally unlike those familiar to human experience.

Any proposed distinction between forms of existence needs clear criteria. Similarity, difference, visibility, measurability and interaction may provide different ways to examine the question, but none should be assumed to define existence in its entirety.

Reality within systems

Systems may operate according to particular principles and rules. These conditions can shape what occurs within a system, how its manifestations relate, and what can be observed or experienced from within it.

A system's internally consistent description of reality should be distinguished from the broader question of whether that description encompasses everything that exists. The principles and rules of one system should not automatically be treated as universal.

Multiple realities

If different manifestations or systems can operate according to different or incompatible principles and rules, it is worth investigating whether they may also involve different forms or conditions of reality.

Different realities might be independent, overlapping, connected or capable of interaction. The possibility of multiple realities does not, by itself, establish that any particular model of them is true. Their meanings and relationships would need to be clarified and investigated.

Perception and observation

Perception and observation influence how a manifestation or system is known and described. What is perceived may depend on the observer, the available means of observation and the conditions under which observation takes place.

It is important to distinguish a change in what an observer knows or experiences from a change in the thing being observed. Whether observation can also affect the observed system is a separate question that requires an appropriate method of investigation.

Reality should not be assumed to be limited to what a particular observer can perceive, measure or understand. Equally, claims about realities beyond observation should remain identified as possibilities unless supported by evidence.

Origin and reality

Origin is the fundamental source and the central subject of this research project. How Origin relates to existence and to the realities of manifestations remains a fundamental question.

Are existence and reality aspects of manifestation, consequences of origination, or concepts that also apply to Origin in a different way? Human descriptions of existence and reality may not necessarily define the nature of Origin. The relationship should remain open to investigation without imposing an answer in advance.

Beyond known existence

Current human knowledge may not encompass every possible form of existence or reality. New observations, methods or conceptual developments may reveal distinctions that are not presently recognized.

Science of Origin remains open to unfamiliar possibilities while distinguishing them from observations, supported findings and established conclusions. Neither familiarity nor unfamiliarity is sufficient, on its own, to determine whether something is possible.

Research directions

  • Clarifying the meanings of existence, reality and manifestation, and how these concepts differ.
  • Identifying meaningful criteria for describing or recognizing different forms of existence.
  • Investigating whether existence necessarily involves manifestation.
  • Examining how principles and rules shape the reality described within a system.
  • Exploring possible relationships among different or incompatible system realities.
  • Distinguishing changes in perception or knowledge from changes in the observed system.
  • Investigating the relationship between Origin, existence, origination and manifested realities.
  • Identifying which questions can be approached through observation, measurement, comparison or experiment, and recording the limits of each method.
Research status: This page records foundational questions and conceptual possibilities for investigation. It does not establish that multiple realities or unobserved forms of existence have been demonstrated. Definitions, criteria, methods and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
