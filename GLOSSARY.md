# Science of Origin — Glossary

**Status:** Initial working draft · Version 0.1  
**Purpose:** A clear reader-facing reference to important terms used in Science of Origin.  
**Relationship to the Terminology Inventory:** The Inventory preserves all source forms, historical usage, source locations, conflicts, and unresolved questions. This Glossary presents a smaller, curated set of working explanations. It does not replace the Inventory.

## How to read this Glossary

- **Working meaning** describes how a term is currently used in the Science of Origin framework. It is not necessarily a complete or final definition.
- **Distinction** identifies a difference that matters when comparing related terms.
- **Research status** is included only where the source explicitly identifies one. A framework description is not automatically an externally verified scientific finding.
- **Under review** means the wording or relationship requires further conceptual clarification.

The order of entries is for navigation only. It does not imply a hierarchy or sequence.

## A. Origin and fundamental descriptions

### Origin

**Working meaning:** Origin refers to the Ultimate Source: the fundamental source underlying everything that exists and every process of origination. It is not merely a beginning in time, an event at the start of a timeline, or an object among other objects.

**Distinction:** Origin and origination are related but different. Origin concerns the Source; origination concerns the capability and process through which manifestations come about.

**Related terms:** Ultimate Source, True Origin, Oneness Origin, Infinity, Origination.

**Research status:** The framework treats Origin as the subject of investigation and does not claim to define it.

**Source:** `origin.html`; `terminology.html`; `research-framework.html`.

### True Origin / Oneness Origin

**Working meaning:** The current Terminology page uses “True Origin” for the Oneness Origin.

**Distinction:** Keep these expressions distinguishable in source records until their preferred-name relationship is confirmed.

**Related terms:** Origin, Fake Origin, Oneness.

**Review note:** Preferred display form and exact equivalence remain under review.

**Source:** `origin.html`; `terminology.html`; `zero-origin.html`.

### Infinity

**Working meaning:** Infinity is described as the closest description of Origin that humans can understand, but it is not a definition of Origin. It is also a foundational research domain in Science of Origin.

**Distinction:** In this framework, Infinity is not simply an extremely large quantity, endless numerical sequence, or unlimited duration. The framework also uses Infinity in describing the possibility associated with Origin before manifestation.

**Related terms:** Origin, Zero, Idle, Imagination, Manifestation.

**Review note:** These uses are connected in the sources, but their exact relationship remains under conceptual review.

**Source:** `origin.html`; `zero-idle-state-of-origin.html`; `terminology.html`.

### Consciousness

**Working meaning:** In descriptions of Origin, Consciousness is presented as a perspective on the Source's attributes. Consciousness is also a subject of research in the Science of Consciousness framework.

**Distinction:** This entry does not assume that every use of “Consciousness” across Origin research and applied research has an identical scope.

**Related terms:** Freewill, Origin, Science of Consciousness, Spectrum of Consciousness.

**Review note:** Its relationship to Freewill and the separate research fields requires further clarification.

**Source:** `origin.html`; `consciousness-freewill.html`; `science-of-consciousness.html`.

### Freewill

**Working meaning:** Freewill is described as one of the fundamental aspects or natures of Origin. The framework describes it as freedom of expression, non-expression, change, non-change, origination, and non-origination. One page describes Freewill as the freedom of change itself.

**Distinction:** Freewill is not treated as a separate object existing alongside existence. The relationship among Freewill, Consciousness, Choice, and Will remains important to clarify.

**Related terms:** Consciousness, Choice, Will, Origination, Change.

**Source:** `core-principles.html`; `origin.html`; `zero-origin.html`; `consciousness-freewill.html`.

### Spirit

**Working meaning:** Spirit is listed among the named aspects or natures of Origin in the current framework.

**Review note:** A fuller working meaning is not established by the pages reviewed for this initial draft. No additional definition is supplied here.

**Related terms:** Origin, Infinity, Consciousness, Freewill, Void.

**Source:** `origin.html`; `zero-origin.html`.

### Void and Voidness

**Working meaning:** Void is listed among the named aspects or natures of Origin. The current Terminology page describes Voidness as a visual perspective on the Source in stillness and non-manifestation.

**Review note:** The sources do not justify treating Void and Voidness as interchangeable. Their relationship remains under review.

**Related terms:** Origin, Idle, Zero, Non-manifestation.

**Source:** `origin.html`; `zero-origin.html`; `terminology.html`.

## B. Zero, Idle, and manifestation

### Zero

**Working meaning:** Zero represents the idle state of Origin. In the current framework, “0 = Infinity” is described as an observed understanding, not an arithmetic equation or numerical starting point.

**Distinction:** Zero is not used here as the number from which counting begins, a mathematical origin, a physical vacuum, simple nothingness, or a moment on a timeline.

**Related terms:** Idle, Infinity, Origin, Manifestation, Non-manifestation.

**Review note:** The exact relationship among Zero, Idle, and Infinity is a priority subject for conceptual review.

**Source:** `zero-idle-state-of-origin.html`; `zero-origin.html`; `terminology.html`.

### Idle

**Working meaning:** Idle refers to the state of Origin represented by Zero, associated in the framework with Infinity before manifestation.

**Distinction:** The Terminology page cautions that “idle” is also used in discussions of existence, intelligence, choice, and non-manifestation. Those uses should not automatically be assumed to have identical scope.

**Related terms:** Zero, Infinity, Non-manifestation, Choice.

**Source:** `zero-idle-state-of-origin.html`; `terminology.html`; `core-principles.html`.

### Origination

**Working meaning:** Origination concerns the capability and process through which manifestations come about and may give rise to further manifestations.

**Distinction:** Origin is the Source; origination is the coming-about process and capability. Manifestation refers to what comes about, not the Source or process.

**Related terms:** Origin, Manifestation, Imagination, Spontaneous Origination, Intentional Origination.

**Source:** `origination.html`; `manifestations.html`; `terminology.html`.

### Manifestation

**Working meaning:** Manifestations are what come about through origination. The framework uses the term broadly for Things and Life Forms that come about through origination.

**Distinction:** Manifestation identifies what has come about, rather than the Source or the process through which it came about.

**Related terms:** Origination, Things, Life Forms, Existence, Non-manifestation.

**Source:** `manifestations.html`; `origination.html`; `research-framework.html`.

### Non-manifestation

**Working meaning:** Non-manifestation is an important subject of investigation alongside manifestation. The Research Framework states that it is not automatically classified as a manifestation.

**Review note:** Its relationship with choice, expression, idle, and the conditions in which nothing is expressed remains open for clarification.

**Related terms:** Manifestation, Zero, Idle, Choice, Origination.

**Source:** `research-framework.html`; `zero-idle-state-of-origin.html`.

## C. Imagination and possibility

### Imagination

**Working meaning:** Imagination is used in a broad foundational sense, beyond human fantasy or mental imagery. It concerns the proposed relationship between Origin, the arising of possibilities, and the coming about of manifestations.

**Distinction:** The framework distinguishes spontaneous and intentional imagination. It also states that spontaneous origination does not necessarily mean the absence of intention at the level of Origin.

**Related terms:** Origin, Possibility, Manifestation, Spontaneous Imagination, Intentional Imagination.

**Source:** `imagination.html`; `spontaneous-intentional-imagination.html`; `core-principles.html`.

### Spontaneous Imagination and Intentional Imagination

**Working meaning:** The framework distinguishes these as two modes or contexts of imagination. The distinction concerns how imagination operates and the context in which manifestations arise.

**Review note:** Their exact relation to intention, choice, and origination should be read in context rather than reduced to everyday meanings of “spontaneous” and “intentional.”

**Related terms:** Imagination, Intention, Origination, Choice.

**Source:** `spontaneous-intentional-imagination.html`; `imagination.html`; `origination.html`.

### Possibility

**Working meaning:** Possibility concerns what may occur, exist, develop, or be originated under some set of conditions.

**Distinction:** The source distinguishes something being recognized, imagined, proposed, or investigated as a possibility. Calling something possible does not by itself establish that it exists or will occur.

**Related terms:** Potential, Imagination, Origination, Manifestation.

**Source:** `possibility-potential.html`.

## D. Science of Consciousness research terms

### Spectrum / Spectra

**Working meaning:** In the supplied Science of Consciousness project framework, Spectrum is intended to represent the entirety of frequencies within the relevant research domain. Spectra refers to multiple spectra or spectral representations used in comparative and related investigations.

**Distinction:** The project describes Spectrum/Spectra as its central research methodology. The uploaded Project Description also lists Spectrum of Consciousness and Spectrum of Things among four core research fields, while the separate Research Architecture document lists two research fields and places Spectrum/Spectra under methodology.

**Research status:** The relationship and architecture are recorded as a source discrepancy for review; this entry does not resolve it.

**Related terms:** Frequency, Spectrum of Consciousness, Spectrum of Things, Measurement, Quantification.

**Source:** Supplied `Science-of-Consciousness.txt`; supplied `Science-of-Consciousness-—-Research-Architecture.txt`; `science-of-consciousness.html`.

### Spectrum of Consciousness

**Working meaning:** A research field described in the supplied project framework as investigating the complete spectrum of frequencies of consciousness within the domain being investigated.

**Research status:** The framework presents this as a research field and research objective, not as a completed measurement result.

**Related terms:** Spectrum, Spectra, Consciousness, Frequency.

**Source:** Supplied `Science-of-Consciousness.txt`.

### Spectrum of Things

**Working meaning:** A research field described in the supplied project framework as investigating the complete spectrum of frequencies of things within the domain being investigated.

**Research status:** The proposed relationship “Spectrum of Things = Spectrum of Consciousness” is explicitly a research objective, not an established scientific conclusion.

**Related terms:** Spectrum, Spectra, Things, Spectrum of Consciousness.

**Source:** Supplied `Science-of-Consciousness.txt`.

### Nature of Life and Nature of Things

**Working meaning:** These are the two research-scope domains named in the supplied Science of Consciousness framework.

**Related terms:** Science of Consciousness, Science of Freewill, Spectrum of Consciousness, Spectrum of Things.

**Source:** Supplied `Science-of-Consciousness.txt`; supplied `Science-of-Consciousness-—-Research-Architecture.txt`.

## E. Applications

### Freedomality of Consciousness

**Working meaning:** The supplied Science of Consciousness framework lists this as an application research field concerning freedomality as a property of consciousness and its possible measurement and quantification.

**Review note:** A fuller definition and its relationship to Freewill, the Freedomality Index, Freedometry, and Level of Actualization of Freewill require further source review.

**Source:** Supplied `Science-of-Consciousness.txt`.

### Entropy of Consciousness

**Working meaning:** The supplied framework lists this as an application research field concerning entropy in relation to consciousness, including its properties, characteristics, and measurable aspects.

**Review note:** The project-specific meaning should be clarified from its own source material; this entry does not import an external definition.

**Source:** Supplied `Science-of-Consciousness.txt`.

### Strength of Consciousness of Things

**Working meaning:** The supplied framework lists this as an application research field concerning the strength of consciousness in relation to things, including its properties and potential measurement.

**Review note:** Its definition and measurement approach remain to be developed from source material.

**Source:** Supplied `Science-of-Consciousness.txt`.

### Awakenology and Exincarnology

**Working meaning:** The supplied framework lists Awakenology as applied research into methods of awakening humanity and development of consciousness. It lists Exincarnology as applied research into methods of Exincarnation (脱離輪迴), focusing on liberation from the reincarnation cycle.

**Review note:** The relationship between the two applications is not resolved here. The source documents list both under applications; broader project terminology may place Exincarnology within Awakenology. This needs explicit review.

**Related terms:** Exincarnation, Consciousness, Freedomality of Consciousness.

**Source:** Supplied `Science-of-Consciousness.txt`.
