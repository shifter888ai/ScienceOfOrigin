# Site Principles

These principles govern the research content and communication of the Science of Origin website. They are the foundation for creating, reviewing and revising every page on the site.

They describe how research is conducted and how findings are presented. Apply them consistently across the entire website.

## 1. Practical Research

The research presented on this site is grounded in real observations, practices, experiments and experiences.

- Focus on what has actually been observed, experienced, tested or discovered.
- State the basis of each finding clearly so readers can understand where it comes from.
- Distinguish direct observations and recorded results from interpretations or further explanations.
- Do not present an interpretation, possibility or unanswered question as an established finding.
- Keep the research open to further observation, experience, testing and discovery.
- Preserve the original context, chronology and source of research records. When understanding develops, make later clarifications traceable rather than silently changing the historical record.

The research principle is the principle by which this site is created.

## 2. Humble and Objective Communication

Present discoveries with humility and objectivity. The purpose is to communicate what has been found clearly and faithfully, while respecting each reader's freedom to examine and understand it.

- **Be objective.** State the facts of findings honestly and objectively. Add no positive or negative slant. Present findings as they are, without exaggeration, understatement or distortion.
- Be firm and accurate about the findings, while remaining considerate toward readers.
- Present discoveries calmly. Do not demand agreement or presume that readers must accept them.
- Avoid language that judges, belittles, provokes or personally challenges readers.
- Explain unfamiliar concepts clearly and allow readers to examine the information and reach their own understanding.
- Do not weaken, conceal or alter a finding merely to make it more acceptable. Equally, do not overstate what the evidence or recorded experience establishes.

Humility concerns how discoveries are communicated; it does not require misrepresenting what has been found. Objectivity requires fidelity to the findings, not a positive or negative presentation.

## Applying These Principles

These two principles apply to every part of the website, including its foundational pages, research topics, records, examples, summaries, references and future updates.

When reviewing or revising a page, ask:

1. Is the content grounded in actual observations, practices, experiments or experiences?
2. Is the basis and status of each finding represented accurately?
3. Does the wording state the findings honestly, with no positive or negative slant?
4. Is the communication humble and respectful without weakening or distorting the findings?
5. Are original records, context and attribution preserved?

If a page does not meet these principles, revise it while preserving the accuracy, meaning and provenance of the underlying research.
