Manifestations

An exploration of what comes into manifestation through origination, and how manifestations exist, relate, transform and give rise to further manifestations.

What is a manifestation?

In the developing framework of Science of Origin, manifestations are the Things and Life Forms that come into manifestation through origination. The term is used broadly; its precise scope and meaning remain open to further investigation.

Origin is understood as the ultimate source of every manifestation. A manifestation may appear directly from Origin or arise through another manifestation. In either case, Origin remains the ultimate source.

Direct and indirect manifestation

  • Direct manifestation: a manifestation that originates directly from Origin.
  • Indirect manifestation: a manifestation that comes about through another manifestation, while ultimately tracing back to Origin.

A manifestation may become the immediate source of further manifestations while remaining a manifestation of the ultimate Origin. This creates chains of origination that may branch, converge and continue through many generations.

Properties and transformation

Properties and characteristics are attributes of manifestations, rather than separate manifestations in themselves. A property does not exist independently of the manifestation to which it belongs.

A manifestation may change some of its properties while remaining the same manifestation. It may also undergo a fundamental transformation and become a different manifestation. In either case, the underlying Origin remains the same.

Different manifestations may share the same immediate or ultimate Origin while possessing very different properties. A manifestation may also have multiple contributing origins, including contributions from different origination chains.

Relationships and interactions

Manifestations may be independent, related or interconnected. Some interact directly and influence one another; others may share the same ultimate Origin without any direct or identifiable causal influence between them.

Interactions among manifestations may contribute to the origination of new manifestations. When this happens, the resulting manifestation may possess properties or characteristics that none of its contributing manifestations possessed individually.

Origination chains may branch, converge and interact. Their relationships can become increasingly complex, and later manifestations may have no immediately identifiable connection to earlier ones even when a longer chain can be traced.

Emergence and new capabilities

A manifestation may develop properties that have not previously appeared in the wider network of origination. Such properties may change its nature, capabilities or potential for further origination.

A manifestation may develop capabilities beyond those of the manifestation from which it originated. Further manifestations may continue this development, potentially producing forms and capabilities that are unfamiliar or incomprehensible to earlier manifestations.

These are possibilities within the developing conceptual framework, not established empirical conclusions. Their meanings and implications require careful examination.

Continuity, cessation and influence

Manifestations may continue, cease, transform or manifest again. When a particular manifestation ceases, that manifestation ceases; its ultimate Origin does not.

A manifestation may initiate processes of origination that continue after it ceases to exist. Its influence may persist through subsequent manifestations, becoming increasingly indirect over time.

Open-ended origination

Individual manifestations may be finite in their properties, capabilities or duration, while the potential for further origination remains open-ended. Chains of origination may have many layers, with no predetermined highest level below Origin.

Some manifestations or aspects of Origin may exceed the ability of other manifestations to comprehend or imagine them. The inability to fully understand Origin does not prevent the investigation of manifestations, their properties, relationships and processes of origination.

Research directions

  • Clarifying what qualifies as a manifestation and how manifestations may be identified.
  • Investigating direct, indirect and multiple contributing origins.
  • Examining continuity, transformation, cessation and re-manifestation.
  • Exploring how manifestations interact and how new properties emerge.
  • Studying branching, convergence and long-range relationships among origination chains.
  • Identifying which aspects can be observed, described, measured or otherwise investigated.
Research status: This page records a developing conceptual framework and research directions. Its propositions are open to clarification, examination and revision; they are not presented as independently established scientific findings.
