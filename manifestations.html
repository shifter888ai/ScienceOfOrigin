Manifestations

Manifestations are what come about through origination. This subject investigates Things and Life Forms as manifestations, and how manifestations exist, relate, change and give rise to further manifestations.

What is a manifestation?

In the developing Science of Origin framework, manifestations refer broadly to Things and Life Forms that come about through origination. The term identifies what has come into manifestation, rather than the source or process through which it came about.

This gives the Foundations division three related but distinct subjects:

  • Origin — the fundamental source, described in this framework as the Oneness Origin.
  • Origination — the capability and process through which manifestations come about and further manifestations may arise.
  • Manifestations — what comes about through origination.

The scope of the term manifestation, and the criteria by which particular things or life forms are identified as manifestations, remain subjects for clarification and investigation.

Life and Consciousness of all manifestations

All Things are Life Forms, without exception. Since manifestations are understood broadly as Things and Life Forms, every manifestation is a Life Form.

All existences have Consciousness and are Consciousness, without exception. Accordingly, Consciousness is not an optional property limited to selected kinds of manifestation. Research investigates how it is expressed and manifested across different forms.

These are foundational positions connected to Things & Life, Nature of Life, Nature of Things and Consciousness and Freewill.

Direct and indirect origination

Within the framework, a manifestation may come about directly from Origin or through the activity or contribution of another manifestation. These are descriptions of origination pathways, not claims that there are multiple ultimate Origins.

  • Direct origination: a manifestation comes about directly from Origin.
  • Indirect origination: a manifestation comes about through one or more intermediate manifestations or processes, while Origin remains the ultimate source in the framework.

A manifestation may therefore be both an outcome of prior origination and an immediate source or contributor in the origination of something further. The pathways may branch, converge and continue across multiple stages.

These distinctions concern the proposed relationships among manifestations. The actual mechanisms and criteria for identifying direct or indirect pathways remain open research questions. See Origination.

Manifestations and their properties

Manifestations may be described through their properties and characteristics: for example, their structure, behavior, capabilities, duration and relationships. In the current framework, these properties are treated as attributes of a manifestation rather than automatically being counted as separate manifestations in their own right.

This is a working distinction, not a final rule for every possible case. Research may need to clarify when a property is simply a characteristic of a manifestation, when it is a process or relationship, and when something should be identified as a distinct manifestation.

Different manifestations may have very different properties while sharing the same ultimate Origin. Similar properties, conversely, do not by themselves establish that two manifestations have the same immediate origination pathway.

Continuity and transformation

A manifestation may change in some respects while continuing to be identified as the same manifestation. It may also undergo a transformation that raises the question of whether it remains the same manifestation or should be considered a different one.

For this reason, a change in properties, a transformation and the origination of a new manifestation should not be treated as interchangeable descriptions. The criteria that distinguish them are central to the investigation.

See Transformation and Emergence for the related study of change, continuity and the appearance of new properties or systems.

Relationships and interactions

Manifestations may be related in different ways. They may interact directly, influence one another, participate in a shared process, or be connected through a longer origination pathway. Other manifestations may share the same ultimate Origin without having any known direct interaction or identifiable causal connection.

Interaction, influence, causal contribution and origination are related ideas, but they are not synonymous. An interaction does not necessarily originate a new manifestation, and a shared Origin does not by itself demonstrate a direct causal relationship between two manifestations.

Where multiple manifestations contribute to the coming-about of something further, their respective roles and the nature of that contribution require separate examination. See Interactions and Systems.

Emergence and new capabilities

Interactions and transformations may be associated with the appearance of properties, structures or capabilities not previously identified in the contributing manifestations. The framework considers whether such developments may also accompany the origination of new manifestations or systems.

A manifestation may have capabilities that differ from those of the manifestation or manifestations through which it came about. Further manifestations may, in turn, develop capabilities that are unfamiliar or difficult for earlier manifestations to comprehend.

Emergence should not automatically be equated with origination. A new property may appear within an existing manifestation or system without necessarily constituting a new manifestation. Determining the distinction requires clear criteria and investigation.

Continuity, cessation and further manifestation

Manifestations may persist, change, transform or cease. When a particular manifestation ceases, the framework distinguishes the cessation of that manifestation from the ultimate Origin to which it is related.

Processes initiated or enabled by a manifestation may continue after that manifestation ceases. In such cases, later manifestations may remain connected to earlier ones through intermediate processes or stages, even when the original manifestation is no longer present.

The term re-manifestation raises further questions: whether it refers to the recurrence of a pattern, the reappearance of similar properties, or the return of the same manifestation. These possibilities should be distinguished rather than assumed to mean the same thing.

Open-ended origination

Within the framework, individual manifestations may be limited in their properties, capabilities or duration, while further origination remains open-ended. Origination pathways may extend through many stages, and a manifestation may become a contributor to developments beyond the capabilities of its own immediate level.

This is a conceptual direction for investigation, not an established conclusion about an infinite sequence of manifestations. The number, limits and structure of origination pathways remain open questions.

Research questions

  • What criteria identify something as a manifestation, and how should the scope of Things and Life Forms be specified?
  • How can direct and indirect origination pathways be distinguished and investigated?
  • How should immediate contributing manifestations be distinguished from the ultimate Origin?
  • When should a property or characteristic be treated as an attribute, a process, a relationship or a distinct manifestation?
  • What criteria distinguish continuity, change, transformation, emergence and the origination of a new manifestation?
  • How can interaction, influence, causal contribution and origination be distinguished?
  • How do multiple manifestations contribute to the coming-about of something further?
  • What does re-manifestation mean, and how can recurrence of a pattern be distinguished from the return of the same manifestation?
  • Which aspects of manifestations and their relationships can be observed, measured or tested?
