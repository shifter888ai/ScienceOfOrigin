Origination Beyond the Creator's Original Design

Investigating how an originated manifestation or system may develop, produce outcomes or give rise to further manifestations beyond what its creator originally designed, intended or anticipated.

What does beyond the original design mean?

A creator may originate a manifestation or system with a particular intention, design, arrangement or range of expected outcomes. Once originated, however, the resulting manifestation may operate under conditions that allow developments the creator did not specify or anticipate.

Beyond the creator's original design refers to developments or outcomes that are not fully contained in, specified by or anticipated within that original design. It does not necessarily mean that the outcome is unrelated to the originating process, that it violates the system's principles and rules, or that the creator has no influence over it.

The subject asks how the relationship between an originating intention or design and what subsequently comes about should be understood.

Intention, design and outcome

Intention, design and outcome are related, but they are not interchangeable:

  • Intention: what the creator intends or seeks to bring about.
  • Design: the arrangement, structure, rules, conditions or pathways specified or established as part of the creation.
  • Outcome: what actually comes about through operation, interaction, development or further origination.

An intention does not necessarily specify every detail of an outcome. A design may establish conditions and possibilities without determining every event that can occur within them. The actual outcome may therefore differ from the creator's expectation without the original intention or design having been meaningless.

Research should identify which aspects were intended, which were specified by the design, which followed from established conditions, and which arose through later processes or interactions.

Possibility within an originated system

An originated system may contain possibilities that its creator did not individually anticipate. Its rules, components, relationships and operating conditions may combine in ways that produce outcomes not explicitly listed in the original design.

Some outcomes may be possible because the creator established a broad set of conditions rather than specifying each result. Others may depend on interactions, changing conditions or capabilities that develop after the system comes into being.

Unanticipated does not automatically mean impossible under the system's rules. Nor does it necessarily mean that the outcome is a new origination. The nature of each outcome requires examination.

Beyond design is not one single process

Developments beyond an original design may take different forms. For example:

  • Unanticipated outcome: an outcome occurs that the creator did not foresee, although it follows from the existing system and its conditions.
  • Variation or generation: the system produces a new instance, arrangement or output without necessarily originating a distinct manifestation or system.
  • Transformation: an existing manifestation or system changes in a way that may not have been specified in its original design.
  • Emergence: new properties, relationships or capabilities become apparent through the system's operation or interactions.
  • Further origination: a new manifestation or system comes about, potentially with its own principles, rules or capabilities.

These descriptions may be related in a particular case, but they should not be treated as synonyms. An unexpected result alone does not establish transformation, emergence or origination.

See Manifestations and Transformation and Emergence.

Development through operation and interaction

After a manifestation or system has been originated, its operation may involve processes and interactions that were not individually specified by its creator. Conditions may change, components may interact, and relationships may develop. These processes may produce new states, arrangements, properties or possibilities.

Interactions with other systems may also contribute to outcomes beyond the original design. The participating systems may have different principles, rules or capabilities, and their interaction may establish conditions that were not present in either system considered alone.

Such developments may remain within the original system, transform it, or contribute to the origination of a further system. Which description applies should be determined from the nature of what comes about.

See Interactions and Possibility and Potential.

Independent origination capabilities

A manifestation or system may develop or possess its own capability to originate further manifestations or systems. If it exercises that capability, the resulting origination may go beyond the original creator's design or expectations.

This is one possible pathway beyond original design, but it is not the only one. An unanticipated outcome may arise without the system originating anything further. Conversely, a system may originate something intentionally even when the originating process and its outcome are not fully anticipated by the earlier creator.

The existence and exercise of an independent origination capability therefore need to be examined separately from the broader question of whether an outcome exceeded the original design. See Independent Origination Capabilities.

Freewill and the limits of total control

Within the Science of Origin framework, every existence is intrinsically Freewill in nature. This is a foundational proposition of the framework, rather than an independently established scientific finding. On this premise, total control over any existence is impossible by nature: an existence cannot be wholly determined or controlled by an originating system because its own Freewill is intrinsic to it.

A system may establish conditions, principles, rules, boundaries and available pathways. It may influence what participants encounter, which possibilities are accessible, and how their intentions are expressed. Such influence may be extensive without becoming total determination.

This distinction separates control of conditions from total control of Freewill. Conditions may be designed or constrained; choices, intentions and further origination cannot thereby be assumed to be completely determined. Influence is not the same as total control.

Within this framework, the openness that remains is not merely a gap in a system's design. It is the condition through which an existence may exercise its Freewill and originate possibilities beyond the system's specified or anticipated outcomes. Total control is impossible; the openness is not simply an accidental shortcoming that could be eliminated by a more complete design.

Game design offers a useful analogy. Game designers often, and in many cases deliberately, preserve some openness rather than predetermine every player action or outcome. They establish rules, boundaries, challenges and available pathways, while leaving room for player choice, exploration and unexpected developments. This openness can be an intentional part of making a game enjoyable. The designer guides the experience without completely determining what each player will do or discover.

The analogy illustrates two different points: the impossibility of total control follows from the framework's premise that every existence is intrinsically Freewill; within designed games, some openness may also be intentionally preserved for the enjoyment and richness of play. The extent to which this analogy applies to other originated systems remains a subject for investigation.

Freewill as a source of beyond-system origination

Freewill may involve more than selecting among possibilities already defined by an originating system. It may also involve originating possibilities, intentions, arrangements or systems that were not contained in the system's original specification.

This suggests a distinction between system-contained possibilities and beyond-system origination. The first concerns outcomes available within the system's established conditions and possibilities. The second concerns the origination of something not previously specified by those conditions. An outcome being unexpected does not by itself establish beyond-system origination; the nature of what has come about must be examined.

If an originated existence can originate further manifestations or systems through its own Freewill, those developments may exceed the original creator's design without being wholly disconnected from the originating pathway. The originating system may remain influential while not being the complete source or determinant of every subsequent possibility.

Creator, influence and responsibility

Origination does not by itself establish that a creator determines or controls every subsequent detail of the originated manifestation. The creator may establish some conditions, while later outcomes depend on the system's operation, the Freewill of its participants, interactions or further origination.

An outcome beyond the original design does not by itself prove that the creator has lost all influence. Influence, constraint, prediction, control and responsibility are distinct questions. A creator may shape conditions and strongly influence pathways while remaining unable to determine every intention or outcome.

Within the framework's premise that every existence is intrinsically Freewill, total control is impossible by nature. The extent and forms of influence, constraint and responsibility should nevertheless be investigated in relation to the particular manifestation, system and pathway.

Relationship with intention and Origin

Within the Science of Origin framework, Consciousness / Freewill is associated with intention, while origination concerns the capability and process through which manifestations come about. Intentional imagination may operate within an existing manifestation and bring about further manifestations under its conditions.

The creator's original intention should not be assumed to contain a complete specification of every subsequent outcome. Equally, developments beyond a creator's design should not be treated as evidence that origination is detached from intention or from the ultimate Origin.

The relationship between intention, origination, the capabilities of an originated system and the resulting outcomes remains open to examination. See Spontaneous and Intentional Imagination and Origination.

Research questions

  • What does it mean, within the Science of Origin framework, to say that every existence is intrinsically Freewill?
  • How should a creator's intention, original design, expectations and actual outcomes be distinguished?
  • What criteria establish that an outcome genuinely goes beyond an original design?
  • Can an originated system produce outcomes not individually specified by its creator while remaining within its established principles and rules?
  • How do operation, changing conditions and interactions contribute to unanticipated outcomes?
  • What distinguishes an unexpected output or variation from transformation, emergence or the origination of a new manifestation?
  • Under what conditions can an originated system develop or exercise its own origination capabilities?
  • Can further origination occur beyond the original creator's expectations without being fully independent of the originating pathway?
  • How can control of conditions and influence over pathways be distinguished from total control of an existence?
  • Can Freewill originate possibilities that were not contained in an originating system's original specification?
  • In which kinds of originated systems is openness deliberately preserved, as game designers often do to support player enjoyment and exploration?
  • How can intentionally designed openness be distinguished from the intrinsic openness that follows, within this framework, from the Freewill of every existence?
  • How should the creator's influence, control, prediction and responsibility be examined separately?
  • How are intention and Consciousness / Freewill related to origination whose outcomes are not fully anticipated?
  • What observations or methods could help identify and investigate these different forms of development?
