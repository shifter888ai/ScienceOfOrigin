Space & Time

An exploration of the nature of space and time, their relationship to Origin and origination, and whether different manifestations and systems may have different forms or conditions of space and time.

The nature of space

Space is commonly used to describe extension, position, distance and the relationships between locations. Within Science of Origin, its fundamental nature remains an open subject of research.

Space may be a fundamental aspect of existence, a condition within which manifestations occur, or something that arises through particular manifestations or systems. These possibilities should remain open until the relevant concepts and evidence can be examined.

The nature of time

Time is commonly used to describe sequence, duration, change and the relationships between events. Whether time is fundamental, universal or dependent on particular manifestations and systems is not yet established within this research framework.

It is also important to distinguish time as measured or experienced within a system from the deeper question of what time itself is.

Space and time within manifestations

Space and time may be conditions through which manifestations exist and change. However, it should not be assumed that every manifestation must share the same spatial or temporal structure.

Different manifestations or systems may have different ways of relating position, distance, sequence and duration. Some may share a common space-time framework, while others may operate under different principles or conditions.

Origination and time

The relationship between origination and time is a central question. Does origination require time, or can origination be understood as more fundamental than the temporal sequences observed within manifestations?

Within a manifestation, origination may be described as occurring before or after other events. That description may depend on the time structure of the manifestation. It does not by itself establish that time is a necessary condition of Origin or of origination itself.

Research should distinguish the temporal order of events within a system from the nature of origination in relation to Origin.

Origin and space-time

Whether Origin exists within space and time, gives rise to space and time, or is not appropriately described by either concept remains an open question.

Space and time may be properties or conditions of manifestations rather than limits that can be assumed to apply to Origin. Conversely, their relationship to Origin may be different from any relationship currently understood through human experience. The framework does not decide between these possibilities in advance.

Different space-time systems

If different manifestations can operate according to different principles and rules, they may also have different spatial or temporal structures. These structures might be distinct, partially compatible or apparently incompatible.

Systems with different space-time conditions may nevertheless interact. How such interactions could occur, what shared conditions they might require, and whether interaction can produce new spatial or temporal structures are questions for investigation.

Beyond known space-time

Current human descriptions of space and time may not encompass every possible form of existence or manifestation. It remains open whether there are forms of space, time or relationships that differ fundamentally from those currently known or experienced.

This is an invitation to investigate without assuming that unfamiliar possibilities are either true or impossible. Proposed possibilities should be distinguished from observations and findings supported by evidence.

Research directions

  • Clarifying the meanings of space, time, position, distance, sequence and duration.
  • Investigating whether space and time are fundamental or arise within manifestations and systems.
  • Examining whether different manifestations can have different spatial or temporal structures.
  • Distinguishing time measured within a system from the relationship between time and origination.
  • Exploring whether origination requires time or whether time is a feature of manifested processes.
  • Investigating possible relationships between Origin and space-time without assuming that human concepts define Origin.
  • Examining how systems with different space-time structures might interact.
  • Identifying which aspects can be observed, measured, compared or experimentally investigated.
Research status: This page records open questions and conceptual possibilities. It does not establish a particular theory of space or time, nor claim that different space-time systems or forms beyond known space-time have been demonstrated. Definitions, methods and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
