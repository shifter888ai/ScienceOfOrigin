II · Nature of Existence

Nature of Things

Investigating what Things are, how they may be described and distinguished, and how they relate to manifestations, properties, systems, Life and Origin.

Things as a subject of existence

Things are a broad subject of investigation within the Nature of Existence. The term is used here as a working category for what may be encountered or identified as a thing within a manifestation. Its precise scope and criteria remain open to clarification.

The familiar objects and entities encountered in everyday experience provide examples, but they should not automatically define the full range of Things that may be considered within Science of Origin.

What constitutes a Thing?

What makes something identifiable as a Thing? Is it its existence, properties, organization, continuity, boundaries, relationships or capacity to interact? Do different kinds of Things require different criteria?

A working description should make clear what is being treated as the Thing under investigation and why. It should also distinguish the subject itself from the concepts, measurements or representations used to describe it.

Things and manifestations

Within the developing Science of Origin framework, manifestations are what come about through origination, and the term broadly concerns Things and Life Forms. This connects the investigation of Things with the foundational subjects of Origin, Origination and Manifestations.

A Thing may be considered as a manifestation, while a system may also be an organized arrangement within or across manifestations. The relationship between a Thing, a manifestation and a system should be specified in each investigation rather than assumed to be identical in every context.

See Manifestations for the framework's working account of what comes about through origination.

Things and properties

Things may be described through properties and characteristics, such as structure, composition, form, behavior, capabilities, duration and relationships. These descriptions help identify and compare Things, but a property should not automatically be treated as a separate Thing.

Some properties may be measurable, while others may require different forms of description or investigation. Research should distinguish a Thing from its properties, states, processes and relations, while remaining open to cases in which those distinctions need refinement.

Things, parts and wholes

A Thing may contain parts, consist of components or participate in a larger arrangement. A part may itself be identifiable as a Thing in another context, and a whole may have properties that are not adequately described by listing its parts separately.

Whether something is treated as a part, a whole or a distinct Thing may depend on the question and scale of investigation. Such choices should be made explicit and should not be mistaken for proof that the selected boundaries are absolute.

Things and systems

Things may be organized into systems, interact with other Things or form components of larger systems. A system is not necessarily just a collection of Things: the organization, relationships and processes among its components may be essential to its character.

Things may also participate in multiple systems or networks at once. Their relationships can be direct, indirect, temporary, persistent or conditional. These possibilities require investigation in their particular contexts.

Things and Life

Life Forms are included in the broad investigation of Things, while the properties and processes associated with Life warrant dedicated examination. The category Thing should therefore be broad enough to include Life Forms without erasing the questions that make Life a distinct research subject.

See Nature of Life for the dedicated investigation of Life and Life Forms, and Things & Life for the broader relationship between these subjects.

Different forms and unfamiliar Things

Known examples should not be assumed to exhaust the possible forms of Things. Other forms may differ in properties, organization, scale, capabilities or conditions of manifestation.

Openness to unfamiliar possibilities does not establish that a proposed Thing exists. Any candidate should be described clearly, distinguished from related concepts and examined through appropriate observations, comparisons or methods.

Origin and Origination of Things

How do Things come into manifestation? This question connects the nature of Things with Origin and Origination. The ultimate source, the process through which something comes about and the resulting Thing are distinct subjects, even when they are closely related.

Research may examine different origination pathways, the role of existing manifestations, and how interactions or processes may contribute to the appearance of further Things. A change in an existing Thing should not automatically be classified as the origination of a new one.

Research directions

  • Clarifying the working scope and criteria of the term Thing.
  • Distinguishing Things from their properties, states, processes, representations and relationships.
  • Investigating how scale and context affect the identification of parts, wholes and distinct Things.
  • Examining the relationship between Things, manifestations and systems.
  • Comparing different forms of Things without assuming that familiar examples exhaust the possibilities.
  • Investigating the relationship between Things and Life Forms while preserving the distinct research focus on Life.
  • Examining how Things come into manifestation and how origination differs from change or transformation.
  • Developing observation, description and measurement methods appropriate to the properties being investigated.
  • Distinguishing observations and reproducible findings from interpretations and unresolved possibilities.
