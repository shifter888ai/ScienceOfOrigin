I · Foundations

Origin

Investigating the fundamental source of existence and origination: the Oneness Origin from which manifestations arise.

Origin as the fundamental source

Science of Origin investigates Origin not merely as a beginning in time, but as the fundamental source underlying everything that exists and every process of origination.

Within the developing framework, Origin is understood as the underlying Infinity and the ultimate Originator. It is not treated as one object among other objects, nor as an event located at the beginning of a timeline.

True Origin — the Oneness Origin

The term True Origin refers to the Oneness Origin (一體本源). It expresses the foundational position that there is one ultimate Origin, rather than multiple independent ultimate sources.

In this framework, Origin has no separate identity apart from the manifestations associated with it. The apparent multiplicity of things does not, by itself, establish the existence of multiple ultimate origins or an absolute separation from the source.

The distinction between True Origin and the constructed imitation called Fake Origin is examined in Origin Models.

Origin and Infinity

Origin is associated with Infinity in the framework. Infinity is not treated simply as an extremely large quantity or an endless sequence within time. It concerns the unrestricted nature attributed to the fundamental source.

The position that nothing can go beyond Infinity is a foundational proposition of this framework. Its meaning and implications require careful clarification rather than being treated as an independently established scientific finding.

The related description of Zero — Idle State of Origin as Infinity before manifestation develops this aspect of the framework further.

Origin and imagination

Science of Origin uses imagination in a broad foundational sense. It is not limited to human fantasy, visualization or the production of mental images. It refers to the proposed capacity through which possibilities and manifestations arise from Origin.

Within this framework, manifestations are understood as imaginations of Origin. The imaginer and the imagined are not proposed as two independently existing ultimate sources; they are considered expressions associated with the same underlying Origin. The relationship between imagination and what comes into manifestation is explored further in Imagination and Manifestations.

The framework distinguishes spontaneous imagination, through which manifestations arise, from intentional imagination, which may operate within an existing manifestation. These distinctions and their mechanisms are developed further under Creation.

Origin and Consciousness / Freewill

The framework connects Origin with Consciousness / Freewill as a fundamental aspect of the source. This relationship is central to the research direction, but the terms, their relationship and the implications for origination require precise examination.

See Consciousness & Freewill for the dedicated subject page.

Origin and origination

Origin and origination are related but distinct subjects. Origin concerns the fundamental source; origination concerns the capability and process through which manifestations come about and may give rise to further manifestations.

This distinction allows the research to examine both the source and the mechanisms or pathways through which things arise, without assuming that every particular manifestation is itself an ultimate origin.

See Origination for the related research subject.

Research questions

  • What does it mean for Origin to be the Oneness Origin?
  • How should Infinity be understood in relation to Origin, beyond numerical or temporal interpretations?
  • What is the relationship between Origin, imagination and manifestation?
  • How are spontaneous and intentional imagination distinguished, and how can their operation be investigated?
  • What is the precise relationship between Origin and Consciousness / Freewill?
  • How can the distinction between Origin and origination be described and examined?
  • Which aspects of these foundational propositions can be investigated through observation, measurement or reproducible methods, and which remain conceptual?
