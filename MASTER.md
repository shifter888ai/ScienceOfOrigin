# Science of Origin — Master Guide

## Purpose

Science of Origin investigates Origin and its manifestations. It does not claim to define Origin. The project shares observed understandings and research for reference, enabling independent examination and further inquiry.

For the project's vision and mission, see [VISION-MISSION.md](VISION-MISSION.md).

## Working structure

- **Research framework:** the overall architecture and current foundational understandings.
- **Major divisions, topics and research branches:** the subjects being investigated.
- **Research Map:** a visual view of subjects and their relationships.
- **Research Directory:** a structured index of research subjects and reference materials.
- **Terminology:** current working meanings, distinctions, relationships and open questions.

The structure is an aid to inquiry, not a limit on it. Topics and relationships may be added, changed or reorganized.

## Working standards

- Keep the website clear, consistent, lightweight and accessible.
- Share research for reference, not instruction.
- Distinguish observations, experiences, interpretations, experiments and independently reproduced findings.
- Keep unresolved questions and unfamiliar possibilities open to investigation.
- Do not present a research objective or interpretation as an established finding.
- Prefer simple, reversible changes and avoid unnecessary complexity.

More specific working guidance is in [SITE-STANDARDS.md](SITE-STANDARDS.md) and [RESEARCH-STANDARDS.md](RESEARCH-STANDARDS.md).

## Website and contribution workflow

- Keep shared site elements and basic presentation consistent across pages.
- Use the existing lightweight HTML, CSS and JavaScript approach unless a clear need justifies a change.
- Make changes on a branch and submit a pull request for review.
- Do not merge or deploy without explicit approval.

## Evolution

Everything in this guide and the linked standards is a working standard. Nothing is permanently fixed. Revise the documents when experience shows that a change would make the project clearer or more effective; apply approved changes consistently.

The map is never the territory, and no document or current framework exhausts the subject of inquiry.
