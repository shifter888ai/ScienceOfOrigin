Conditional Creation — IF / THEN / ELSE

Investigating how conditions and rules may determine whether, when and in what form further manifestations or systems come about.

What is conditional creation?

Conditional creation concerns processes in which the origination of something depends on one or more specified or arising conditions. The basic structure can be expressed as IF a condition is met, THEN a particular process or outcome follows; ELSE an alternative process or outcome applies.

This structure is used as a broad way to investigate conditional origination. It is not limited to computer software, formal logic or human-designed mechanisms. Conditions and conditional rules may be considered wherever a manifestation or system has processes through which different conditions lead to different outcomes.

The IF / THEN / ELSE structure

  • IF — condition: identifies a state, event, relationship, threshold or combination of circumstances relevant to the process.
  • THEN — resulting pathway: identifies what process or outcome follows when the condition is met. This may include the origination of a further manifestation or system.
  • ELSE — alternative pathway: identifies what happens when the condition is not met. It may lead to a different outcome, another condition being examined, no action, or the continuation of an existing state.

These are functional roles in a conditional structure, not necessarily three separate events or three independently existing things. The precise relationship between a condition, the rule that refers to it and the resulting process requires examination in each context.

Generation and origination are not the same

The IF condition does not itself generate or originate an outcome. It identifies or evaluates the circumstance to which a rule applies. The THEN and ELSE branches describe the pathways that follow; what happens along those pathways depends on the process involved.

A conditional pathway may lead to generation, such as producing an output or forming an instance. It may instead lead to a change or transformation within an existing manifestation, or it may contribute to the origination of a new manifestation or system. These are not interchangeable outcomes.

In this research, generation refers to producing or forming something, while origination concerns the coming-about of a manifestation or system. Generation may occur within something that already exists and does not, by itself, establish that a new manifestation has been originated. Conditional creation specifically examines cases in which a conditional pathway contributes to origination.

Accordingly, not every IF / THEN / ELSE structure is a process of origination. It becomes relevant to conditional origination when the resulting pathway brings a further manifestation or system into being. The criteria for identifying that distinction remain a subject for investigation.

Conditions and rules

A condition describes a circumstance relevant to whether a pathway applies. A rule expresses how the system or manifestation responds to that circumstance. A condition and a rule are therefore related, but they should not be treated as interchangeable.

Conditions may concern the state or properties of a manifestation, the presence or absence of something, an interaction, a relationship between systems, or a combination of circumstances. Some conditions may be explicit and identifiable; others may be implicit in how a system operates.

Rules may specify one pathway or several possible pathways. They may also establish priorities, sequences, exceptions or requirements that determine how multiple conditions are evaluated. Whether these structures are present, and how they operate, depends on the manifestation or system being investigated.

See Principles and Rules for the wider examination of rules within manifestations.

Multiple and nested conditions

A conditional process need not involve only one IF / THEN / ELSE decision. Several conditions may need to be met together, any one of several conditions may be sufficient, or one result may lead to another conditional process.

Conditions may therefore be combined, nested or evaluated in sequence. A THEN pathway may initiate another IF / THEN / ELSE structure; an ELSE pathway may lead to a different set of conditions. More complex arrangements may have multiple branches, dependencies or interacting pathways.

The order in which conditions are evaluated may matter. So may the possibility that conditions change while a process is underway, that more than one condition is satisfied, or that different rules appear to prescribe incompatible outcomes. These are questions for investigation rather than assumptions that every conditional system follows one universal structure.

From conditional response to origination

Not every conditional response is an act of creation or origination. A system may respond to a condition by changing its state, continuing an existing process or selecting an available action without bringing a new manifestation into being.

Conditional creation specifically concerns cases in which a conditional pathway contributes to the coming-about of a further manifestation or system. A key research task is to identify what distinguishes such origination from an ordinary response, adjustment, change or transformation within an existing manifestation.

A conditional rule may determine whether a new manifestation comes about, which pathway leads to it, or what conditions shape its form. The rule may be a contributing mechanism, but the relationship between the rule, the process of origination and the resulting manifestation should be examined rather than assumed.

See Origination, Manifestations and Transformation and Emergence.

Conditional creation within manifestations

Within the developing framework, intentional imagination may operate within an existing manifestation and bring about further manifestations under its conditions, principles and rules. Conditional creation offers one possible way to investigate how such conditions may guide or differentiate further origination.

The rules of one manifestation or system need not be universal. A manifestation may contain multiple systems with different rules, and systems with different or apparently incompatible rules may interact. Conditional pathways may therefore need to be understood in relation to the particular system, context and interactions involved.

A system may also originate another system whose rules and conditional structures differ from its own. Whether a resulting system can establish new conditions or develop its own origination capabilities is an open research direction.

Conditional creation is not limited to programming

Computer programs provide familiar examples of explicit IF / THEN / ELSE instructions. They can be useful for describing and testing conditional structures, but they are only one possible domain of investigation.

Conditional creation is broader than programmed creation. It asks whether and how conditional relationships operate in the origination of manifestations and systems, including contexts that are not designed or implemented as software. A conditional process need not be a computer program, and the existence of a program does not by itself establish that a new manifestation has been originated.

Programmed Creation is a related but separate research subject. It examines creation through programmed structures and mechanisms. Conditional Creation focuses on the role of conditions and branching pathways, whether or not they are implemented through programming.

Relationship with spontaneous and intentional imagination

Conditional creation is especially relevant to the investigation of intentional imagination operating within an existing manifestation, where conditions and rules may shape the pathways through which further manifestations arise.

It should not be assumed that all origination is conditional, or that conditional rules explain spontaneous imagination. The distinction between spontaneous and intentional imagination concerns different modes and contexts; conditional creation examines a possible mechanism within some processes of further origination.

Within the framework, spontaneous does not mean without intention at the level of Origin. This distinction remains important when relating conditional processes to the broader account of Origin, Consciousness / Freewill and origination. See Spontaneous and Intentional Imagination.

Research questions

  • What qualifies as a condition in a process of creation or origination?
  • How should conditions be distinguished from the rules that refer to them?
  • What forms can IF / THEN / ELSE structures take beyond explicit computer programming?
  • How do combined, nested, sequential or branching conditions operate?
  • How are multiple conditions, changing conditions and conflicting rules handled within a system?
  • When does a conditional response amount to the origination of a new manifestation or system rather than a change within an existing one?
  • How do conditional rules relate to intention and intentional imagination?
  • Can conditional pathways contribute to the origination of systems with different principles, rules or capabilities?
  • Can an originated system establish new conditional structures or develop its own origination capabilities?
  • How can conditional creation be observed, described, measured or tested in different domains?
  • Which aspects of conditional creation are distinct from programmed creation, spontaneous imagination and other forms of origination?
