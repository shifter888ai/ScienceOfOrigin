Possibility & Potential

An open investigation of what may be possible, what may have the potential to become actual, and how possibilities may arise, develop or remain unrealized across manifestations and systems.

The nature of Possibility

Possibility concerns what may occur, exist, develop or be originated under some set of conditions. A possibility may be recognized, imagined, proposed or investigated, but these are not equivalent states. Describing something as possible does not establish that it exists or will occur.

Research must clarify the conditions and meaning of a possibility, including the system, manifestation, principles and rules within which it is being considered.

Possibility and Potential

Possibility and potential are related but need not mean the same thing. Possibility concerns whether an outcome or form may occur. Potential concerns a capacity, tendency or set of conditions through which something may develop or become actual.

A possibility may be conceivable without an identified potential or pathway for its realization. Conversely, a potential may remain unrecognized or unrealized. These distinctions require precise definitions in each research context.

Actual and Potential Existence

Some things are actual within a particular manifestation or system, while other things may be described as potential or not yet realized. The distinction raises questions about what it means for something to exist potentially, and whether potential existence is a useful description or requires a different account.

Research should distinguish what is observed to exist, what is inferred from present conditions, and what is proposed as a possible future or alternative. The framework does not assume that every conceivable possibility has a form of existence.

Conditions for Possibility

Whether an outcome is possible may depend on conditions. These may include the properties and states of participating things, available resources, relationships, principles, rules and interactions with other systems.

Some conditions may enable a possibility, while others may be necessary for a particular route to its realization. Identifying such conditions can help distinguish a meaningful possibility from an unsupported suggestion.

Constraints and Limitations

Every investigated system may have constraints that shape what can occur within it. Constraints may arise from its structure, operating principles, rules, available resources or relationships with its surroundings.

A limitation within one system should not automatically be treated as a universal limitation. Different manifestations or systems may operate under different, even incompatible, principles and rules. The scope of any claimed constraint therefore needs to be stated clearly.

Multiple and Alternative Possibilities

Given a particular set of conditions, more than one outcome may be possible. Alternative possibilities may coexist, compete, depend on different conditions or represent different paths of development.

Research can examine whether alternatives are mutually exclusive, whether they can occur together, and how changes in conditions alter the range of available outcomes. The existence of multiple possibilities should not be confused with equal likelihood.

Unrealized Possibilities

A possibility may never become actual. It may remain unrealized because necessary conditions are absent, because another outcome occurs, or because the relevant system changes before the possibility can develop.

It may be difficult to distinguish an unrealized possibility from an outcome that was never genuinely possible under the conditions that existed. This distinction calls for careful definitions and evidence rather than retrospective assumption.

Potential within Systems

A system may have capacities that are not expressed in its current state. Changes in internal organization, external conditions or relationships may allow different capacities to become expressed.

Research can investigate how potential relates to a system’s structure and principles, whether potential is fixed or can change, and whether new capacities can develop through the system’s own transformation.

Possibilities Emerging from Interactions

Interactions among things and systems may create conditions for outcomes that would not arise from examining each participant independently. New relationships, arrangements or system properties may open possibilities that were not available before the interaction.

Different systems may interact even when they operate according to different or apparently incompatible principles and rules. Whether such interactions can produce genuinely new possibilities, and how those possibilities should be characterized, remain open research questions.

Origination and New Possibilities

Origination is a central subject in Science of Origin. The framework considers origination as spontaneous and continuous, and investigates how manifestations originate. This raises the question of whether origination can bring about possibilities that were not available within the conditions or systems that preceded them.

New systems may also develop independent origination capabilities. If this occurs, they may give rise to further systems and possibilities. The range and nature of such possibilities are not assumed in advance and require investigation.

Beyond Known Possibilities

What is currently recognized as possible may reflect the limits of existing knowledge, concepts and methods rather than the full range of what can occur. Unfamiliar possibilities may require new ways of describing and investigating manifestations, systems and origination.

Openness to unknown possibilities is not evidence that any particular possibility is real. Proposed possibilities should be clearly identified as proposals until supported by observations, appropriate methods and reproducible findings.

Research Directions

  • Clarifying the distinctions among possibility, potential, capacity, actuality and realization.
  • Identifying the conditions under which a proposed possibility can occur.
  • Distinguishing system-specific constraints from claims about universal limitations.
  • Investigating multiple, alternative, conditional and unrealized possibilities.
  • Examining how a system’s potential may change through transformation and interaction.
  • Investigating whether interactions between systems can create possibilities unavailable to either system independently.
  • Exploring the relationship between Origination and the emergence of new possibilities.
  • Examining whether newly originated systems can develop new potentials and independent origination capabilities.
  • Developing methods to distinguish conceivable possibilities from those supported by identifiable conditions and evidence.
Research status: This page establishes an open research area and records concepts and questions for investigation. It does not assume that every conceivable possibility is realizable, that potential guarantees an outcome, or that current system constraints apply universally. Definitions, methods and evidence remain open to examination and revision.
