Simulated Boundaries: Illusion and Function in an Undivided Origin

How Apparent Separation Can Have Functional Effects Without Dividing the One

Initial research account · 26 November 2026 · Research is ongoing

Abstract

This paper explores a foundational proposition within Science of Origin: the root is simple and One, and the One remains undivided while expressing itself as multiplicity. What appear to be boundaries between manifestations are understood as simulated boundaries. They are illusions in nature, yet functional in manifestation.

The central distinction is between the apparent or simulated nature of a boundary and the effects associated with it. A simulated boundary may make manifestations distinguishable, organize their relationships, regulate interaction or establish functional conditions without constituting an actual division of the One.

The paper develops this distinction, connects it with the proposed Fake Origin model, and identifies questions and possible lines of investigation. It presents a developing research understanding, not an independently established universal scientific law or a completed explanation of the mechanisms involved.

1. The Root Is Simple and One

The current foundational understanding of Science of Origin is that the root is simple and One. The One does not become divided in order to express multiplicity. It remains One while expressing itself in many forms.

Multiplicity therefore need not imply that the root has been split into separate origins or independent pieces. Diversity may belong to the expression of the One rather than to a division of the One itself.

This distinction is essential to the present inquiry. If the root remains undivided, the appearance of distinct manifestations requires examination without assuming that their distinguishability proves an ultimate separation.

The One remains One while expressing itself as many.

2. What Is a Simulated Boundary?

A simulated boundary is an apparent distinction, limit or separation that operates within manifestation without constituting an ultimate division in the nature of the One.

Such a boundary may be experienced or represented as if it marks where one manifestation ends and another begins, what belongs to a particular domain, or which interactions are available. Its appearance can be convincing and its effects can be consequential. Neither fact, by itself, establishes that the One has actually been divided.

In this paper, simulated does not mean that the effects are necessarily imaginary, trivial or without consequence. It describes the boundary's status in relation to the undivided root. The distinction presented at the level of manifestation is not treated as proof of an ultimate division.

This use of the term builds on the earlier research account There Is No Boundary, which examines apparent boundaries as virtual and breakable. The present paper develops a further aspect: their functional role.

3. Illusion and Function Are Not Contradictory

The central proposition is that simulated boundaries are illusions but functional. These two descriptions refer to different aspects and should not be confused.

  • Illusion in nature: the apparent boundary does not divide the One or establish an ultimate separation.
  • Function in manifestation: the simulated boundary can have effects within the manifested arrangement, including effects on distinction, organization, interaction and operation.

A functional effect does not prove that the boundary is ultimate or fundamental. Conversely, describing a boundary as simulated does not mean that its functional consequences can simply be ignored.

The distinction may be expressed as follows:

Illusory in nature, functional in manifestation.

This is not a claim that every perceived boundary performs every possible function. The function, scope and effects of a particular simulated boundary require examination in their own context.

4. Possible Functions of Simulated Boundaries

Within manifestation, simulated boundaries may serve several distinguishable functions. These are proposed categories for investigation rather than a completed classification.

  • Distinction: making one manifestation, role, state or domain distinguishable from another.
  • Organization: providing an apparent or operational structure through which manifestations and relationships can be arranged.
  • Interaction: shaping which forms of contact, exchange or influence are available.
  • Regulation: establishing conditions, permissions, restrictions or rules within a particular manifestation or domain.
  • Individuality: supporting the recognition or experience of particular identities without requiring that the One Consciousness be divided.
  • Experience and perspective: contributing to the way a manifestation encounters and interprets its own context and other manifestations.

These functions may overlap. A single simulated boundary may contribute to several of them, and different boundaries may operate in different ways. The presence, strength and consequences of each function remain questions for specific investigation.

5. Functional Effect Does Not Establish Ultimate Separation

A distinction may be effective within a particular context without being absolute. A system may regulate access; a domain may operate under specific rules; a manifestation may have a recognizable identity; or an interaction may be unavailable through a particular route. These effects may be real within the relevant operation.

However, the existence of such effects does not, by itself, demonstrate that the manifestations involved have separate ultimate origins or that the One has been divided. A functional distinction and an absolute ontological separation are different claims.

Research should therefore identify the exact effect being observed and avoid extending a local or functional description into a conclusion about ultimate separation without additional grounds.

This distinction also avoids the opposite error: assuming that because a boundary is simulated, every practical limitation or consequence associated with it must be ineffective. The simulation may be illusory in nature while its operation remains consequential within manifestation.

6. Simulated Division and Fake Origin

The proposed Fake Origin model describes a manifested imitation of Origin in which relative independence and division are presented as though they were absolute. Within this model, simulated boundaries may contribute to the appearance that manifestations are wholly separate from one another or from their ultimate source.

The distinction is not that every functional boundary is itself Fake Origin. Rather, a simulated boundary may have a limited function within manifestation, while an interpretation of that boundary may present its scope as absolute and thereby reinforce an appearance of divided Origin.

Accordingly, the present paper connects with Fake Origin — The Divided Origin and Fabricated Division and Boundaries. It does not assume that every distinction, domain or functional restriction is deliberately fabricated, nor that every case has already been explained by the Fake Origin model.

7. Oneness, Multiplicity and Individuality

If the One remains One while expressing itself as many, individuality does not necessarily require ultimate separation. Manifestations may be distinguishable in their characteristics, functions, perspectives or relationships while remaining expressions within an undivided root.

This provides a way to examine the relationship between Oneness and multiplicity without treating them as mutually exclusive. The many are not necessarily fragments produced by dividing the One; their distinctions may be expressed through simulated boundaries that have functional effects.

This perspective may also inform research into Focal Points, Quantum, Frequency and Spectrum, and Manifestations. The precise relationships among these subjects remain open to further investigation.

8. Questions for Further Research

  • How does a simulated boundary arise or become established within a manifestation?
  • What distinguishes the appearance of a boundary from the functions it performs?
  • Can a simulated boundary perform several functions at once, and can those functions change?
  • How do simulated boundaries affect identity, individuality, interaction and the experience of separation?
  • What makes a simulated boundary persistent, permeable, breakable or changeable?
  • How can functional effects be examined without assuming that they establish ultimate separation?
  • How does the appearance of divided Origin relate to the operation of simulated boundaries within the proposed Fake Origin model?
  • Are there manifestations or relationships in which distinction is possible without any identifiable boundary-like function?

9. Possible Lines of Investigation

Further inquiry may begin with particular cases rather than assuming that all simulated boundaries operate in the same way. For each case, researchers can:

  1. Describe the apparent boundary and the manifestations or domains it appears to distinguish.
  2. Identify the specific function attributed to it, such as distinction, regulation or interaction.
  3. Record the observable or reported effects separately from interpretations about the boundary's ultimate nature.
  4. Examine whether the boundary is fixed, conditional, permeable, breakable or dependent on a particular perspective or context.
  5. Compare what changes when the boundary's operation changes, while avoiding the assumption that a change in function proves a change in ultimate Oneness.

These steps provide a way to clarify descriptions and compare cases. They do not, by themselves, establish the metaphysical proposition that the One is undivided or identify a complete mechanism by which simulated boundaries operate. Those questions remain open within the broader research.

10. Summary

The root is simple and One. The One remains undivided while expressing itself as many.

What appear to be boundaries between manifestations are understood in this framework as simulated boundaries. They are illusions in nature, but they can be functional in manifestation.

Their functional effects may help distinguish, organize, regulate or shape interactions among manifestations. Those effects do not establish that the One has been divided or that separate ultimate Origins exist.

Simulated boundaries create functional multiplicity without dividing the One.

The distinction between illusion and function provides a basis for further inquiry into Oneness, multiplicity, individuality, manifestation and the proposed Fake Origin model. The mechanisms and full range of functions remain open for investigation.

This is all that is being made available for now.
